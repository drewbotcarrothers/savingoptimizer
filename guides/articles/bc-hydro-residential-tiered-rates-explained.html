How BC Hydro’s residential tiered rate works (and when flat or time-of-day options matter)

B.C. rate schedules, not Ontario TOU · About 15 minutes · 20 Sep 2026

B.C. households hit Step 2 on a January electric-heat bill and assume the only lever is a colder house. BC Hydro’s residential offer is now a menu: the inclining-block (tiered) rate, an optional flat rate, and an optional time-of-day overlay on either. That is not Ontario Time-of-Use, and pasting a Toronto ¢ into a Vancouver conversation will lie to you.

This is BC Hydro Account Online tactics with the ¢ published on the residential pages (used 20 Sep 2026; BCUC lists the tiered schedule as of 1 July 2026). Confirm live prices before you switch — the Electric Tariff wins if a webpage drifts. FortisBC electricity is a different utility. New Westminster, Penticton, and Nelson are not BC Hydro.

Disclosure: Smart thermostats and LED kits are offer types. Saving Optimizer may earn a commission if we later add partner links. We do not currently claim BC Hydro, FortisBC, thermostat-brand, or retailer partnerships. This is education — not a rate enrolment.

Key takeaways

  • Tiered (RS 1101): 11.87 ¢ up to 22.1918 kWh/day (about 1,350 kWh on a 60-day bill, 675 kWh on a 30-day bill), then 14.08 ¢. Daily basic 23.44 ¢.
  • Optional flat (RS 1151): 12.70 ¢ on every kWh, daily basic 25.00 ¢. Built for higher-use and electric-heat homes that live on Step 2.
  • Optional time-of-day: −5 ¢ from 11 p.m.–7 a.m., +5 ¢ from 4–9 p.m., no change in between. It stacks on tiered or flat. EV overnight is the use case; a condo that cooks at 6 p.m. is not.
  • The daily basic still lands on a low-use apartment. You cannot conserve your way out of customer-related costs.
  • Run the Account Online rate estimator on 12 months of your kWh. Interior electric heat in January is a different house than a Lower Mainland gas-heat condo.

Inclining-block basics: Step 1 threshold and Step 2 premium

On the tiered plan you pay a lower energy ¢ up to a daily threshold, then a higher ¢. BC Hydro multiplies 22.1918 kWh × the number of days in that billing period. Meters are read on different days, so the line on your bill moves.

  • Bi-monthly (typical house): about 1,350 kWh of Step 1 on a 60-day bill. BCUC’s 62-day illustration is 1,376 kWh.
  • Monthly: about 675 kWh of Step 1 on a 30-day bill.

Energy ¢ on the residential tiered page (used 20 Sep 2026): Step 1 11.87 ¢/kWh, Step 2 14.08 ¢/kWh. The Step 2 premium is conservation signalling — it is also what a poorly insulated duplex on baseboards lives in from November to March. A Lower Mainland condo on gas heat may never see it.

Labelled 60-day sketch: 1,800 kWh = 1,350 × 11.87 ¢ + 450 × 14.08 ¢ ≈ $223.60 energy, plus 60 × 23.44 ¢ ≈ $14.06 basic. That is not your bill (taxes and riders exist) and it is not Ontario.

Daily basic charge and why low-usage homes still pay it

The basic charge is a small daily amount that partly recovers customer service, metering, billing, and customer-related distribution. On tiered it is 23.44 ¢/day (about $7.10 on a 30-day bill). On flat it is 25.00 ¢/day. The minimum charge is that basic — a vacant week still prints it.

This is why “I barely used any power” and a $20 bill can both be true. Chasing Step 2 with the lights off does not erase the basic. If the complaint is the daily line, you are in the wrong argument; if the complaint is a 2,400 kWh January, you are in the right one.

Optional flat rate and time-of-day overlays (verify current options)

BC Hydro now lets most residential accounts choose. The pages can move; verify in Account Online the week you click.

Residential options on BC Hydro pages used 20 Sep 2026. Tariff wins if a number drifts.
Plan Energy Who it is built for
Tiered (RS 1101) 11.87 ¢ then 14.08 ¢; basic 23.44 ¢/day Average or lower use — apartments, condos, non-electric heat
Flat (RS 1151) 12.70 ¢ all kWh; basic 25.00 ¢/day Higher use — detached / townhome electric heat that lives on Step 2
Time-of-day (RS 2101 overlay) −5 ¢ 11 p.m.–7 a.m.; +5 ¢ 4–9 p.m.; else unchanged Flexible hours, especially overnight EV charging

Time-of-day is not a third energy ¢ replacing the plan. It is a plus/minus on top of tiered or flat. BC Hydro’s 2026 explainer says apartments and condos with little flexibility are generally a poor fit. You can add it to an EV-only second meter in some setups — ask; do not assume. You can switch back.

Eligibility exclusions exist (certain account types). If the estimator and the tariff PDF disagree, believe the PDF and a phone call.

Seasonal usage patterns for Lower Mainland vs Interior electric heat

A Burnaby condo on gas heat in a mild February is not a Prince George or Kelowna house on baseboards. Degree-days and the heating fuel decide whether you graze Step 2 or camp there.

  • Lower Mainland, gas heat: winter kWh are lights, DHW if electric, electronics, and maybe a heat pump. Tiered often still fits. Time-of-day is an EV conversation.
  • Interior or North, electric heat: January kWh explode. Flat exists because Step 2 becomes the real rate. Air sealing and zoning beat a rate argument — see baseboards and attic R-values.
  • Shoulder months: a July AC bill in the Interior can also punch Step 2. Model the year, not the anecdote.

Do not use one January to elect flat for the next five years of a vacant-nest condo.

Bill-cutting levers that beat chasing Step 2 alone

  1. Stop exfiltrating heat — weatherstripping, attic hatch, dryer flap. Rate math on a sieve is theatre.
  2. Zone unused rooms if you are on electric heat. A closed door and a set-back stat are cheaper than a ¢ argument.
  3. Shift what can move if you add time-of-day: dishwasher, laundry, EV. Dinner at 6 p.m. is the +5 ¢ surcharge.
  4. LEDs and the second fridge — real, small, not a 2,000 kWh January.
  5. Then run the estimator. A plan switch is the last 2 ¢, not the first 30%.

Equal Payment Plan, if you have it, is cashflow. It does not change Step 1. See equal billing.

Where to pull usage history in Account Online

Log in. Consumption graphs sit on the account home. BC Hydro’s time-of-day explainer (2026): look for “Learn more about all rate plans” near the graphs, then the estimator. HydroHome may show an Advisor Card with an estimated annual difference and a one-click switch — treat that as a prompt to read the hours, not as a mandate.

  1. Download or screenshot 12 months of kWh (and daily averages).
  2. Run the estimator on tiered, flat, and each with time-of-day.
  3. Write the annual gap in dollars. A $40 year is not a project.
  4. If you switch, calendar a six-month check. You can switch back.

If you are on FortisBC electricity or a municipal utility, stop. Their tariffs are not these ¢.

Frequently asked questions

Is BC Hydro’s Step 2 the same as Ontario Tiered?

No. BC Hydro’s threshold is 22.1918 kWh per day (about 1,350 kWh on a 60-day bill). Ontario’s residential threshold is 600 kWh in summer and 1,000 kWh in winter on a monthly clock. Do not mix the two.

Should I switch to the flat rate if I hit Step 2 in January?

Only if the Account Online estimator says so on 12 months of your kWh. One cold bill is a bad sample. Flat is 12.70 ¢ all kWh with a 25.00 ¢ daily basic — it helps high-use electric heat and can cost a low-use condo.

What does time-of-day actually change?

A −5 ¢ overnight discount (11 p.m.–7 a.m.) and a +5 ¢ surcharge 4–9 p.m., added to whichever of tiered or flat you are on. Other hours are unchanged. It is optional and reversible.

Why do I still pay a basic charge if I used almost nothing?

The daily basic (23.44 ¢ on tiered, 25.00 ¢ on flat) recovers customer-related costs. The minimum charge is that basic. Vacant-week conservation does not erase it.

Where do I see whether I should switch?

Log in to Account Online and run the rate estimator on your history. HydroHome may also surface an Advisor Card. Confirm live ¢ on the tariff pages before you click.
