First-time home buyer programs in Canada: a 2026 province-by-province map

A .ca page beats a 2022 listicle · About 9 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 23 Oct 2026

First-time home buyer programs in Canada are mostly tax relief, not grants. Ontario refunds up to $4,000 of land transfer tax, Toronto adds up to $4,475, and B.C. waives property transfer tax on the first $500,000 (worth up to $8,000) for homes up to $835,000. If you buy a new home, the federal first-time buyer GST rebate (law since March 12, 2026) can return up to $50,000, and Ontario now adds up to $80,000 of the provincial part of the HST.

Each program has its own definition of “first-time,” so a 50-year-old who sold years ago can qualify for one and not another. National listicles also keep ranking down-payment pilots that closed years ago. The useful skill is knowing the program types, the official .ca page, and the rule that disqualifies you — then verifying the week you make an offer.

This is a verification-first map, not a catalogue. Pair it with FTHBI replacements, Ontario LTT, and GST/HST.

Key takeaways

  • Treat every national “grant list” as expired until the provincial or city .ca page says otherwise the week you offer.
  • Ontario first-time LTT refund max $4,000 (ontario.ca, 10 Feb 2026); Toronto MLTT rebate up to $4,475.
  • B.C. first-time exemption: no property transfer tax on the first $500,000 for homes up to $835,000 (worth up to $8,000), phased out by $860,000 (gov.bc.ca).
  • New homes: federal first-time GST rebate up to $50,000 (homes up to $1 million); Ontario first-time rebate up to $80,000 of the provincial HST (CRA).
  • “First-time” means different things: Ontario’s refund needs never owned anywhere; the FHSA, HBP and GST rebate use a four-year look-back.
  • Alberta has no provincial LTT to refund. Québec’s mutation/welcome tax is municipal.
  • FHSA and HBP are federal wrappers. They do not replace a closed municipal pilot — and FTHBI is still closed (21 Mar 2024).

How do you research first-time buyer programs without outdated lists?

Start on the provincial or city .ca page, check its “last updated” date, and confirm with your lawyer that the program still applies in your closing month.

  1. Search site:gov.bc.ca first-time (or ontario.ca, quebec.ca, alberta.ca) — not “best first-time grants Canada 2022.”
  2. Open the page’s “last updated” line. If it is older than a budget cycle, look for a replacement bulletin.
  3. Call or email the program inbox with your income, household size, and target price. Save the reply PDF.
  4. Ask the lawyer, not the salesperson, whether the program still funds closings in your month.
  5. If the only source is a realtor blog without a .ca link, treat the dollar amount as zero until proven.

What types of first-time buyer programs exist in Canada?

Four types: land or property transfer tax relief, down-payment help, tax credits, and GST/HST rebates on new homes. Each pays at a different time.

Program types and when the money shows up. Confirm amounts on the official page.
Type What it changes Typical failure mode
Land-transfer / property-transfer relief Cash at or after closing Spouse history; you already owned abroad
Down-payment / shared-equity / forgivable loan Cash to the mortgage desk Income or price cap; funds exhausted; second-home rules
Tax credit (non-refundable) A later Notice of Assessment, not closing week You needed cash on closing day
GST/HST new-housing GST (and in Ontario, provincial HST) on a new build You bought a resale, or signed before March 20, 2025

What do Ontario, B.C., Québec and Alberta offer first-time buyers?

Ontario and Toronto refund land transfer tax, B.C. exempts part of its property transfer tax, Québec’s welcome tax is municipal, and Alberta has no land transfer tax to refund.

  • Ontario. First-time provincial LTT refund maximum $4,000; full relief on the first $368,000 (ontario.ca, updated 10 Feb 2026). Toronto adds municipal LTT and a first-time rebate up to $4,475. Both require that you have never owned a home anywhere in the world and that you move in within nine months. New-home HST relief is separate (next section).
  • B.C. First-time buyers pay no property transfer tax on the first $500,000 if the home is worth $835,000 or less (since April 1, 2024). Above $500,000 the exemption is a flat $8,000; it shrinks between $835,000 and $860,000 and ends at $860,000. On gov.bc.ca’s own table, a $700,000 home owes $12,000 of tax, the exemption is $8,000, and $4,000 is left to pay. You need one year of B.C. residence (or two B.C. tax returns in the last six years).
  • Québec. The welcome tax (droit de mutation) is collected by the municipality. Some cities have first-time or new-build relief that comes and goes. TAL is the tenancy board, not a grant office. Check the city’s finance page and Revenu Québec — not a Toronto blog.
  • Alberta. No provincial land-transfer tax. That is the feature. Local down-payment or Habitat-style programs still expire. Do not invent an LTT refund to make a Calgary offer “feel like Ontario.”

Atlantic provinces, Manitoba, and Saskatchewan each have or have had first-time credits and land-transfer rules. Same method: official page, date stamp, lawyer.

What changed for first-time buyers of new homes in 2026?

Two rebates now cover sales tax on a new home. Both apply only to homes bought from a builder (or owner-built), not resales.

  • Federal first-time home buyers’ GST/HST rebate. 100% of the GST (or federal part of the HST) on a new home up to $1 million, up to $50,000, reduced between $1 million and $1.5 million. It applies to builder agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 12, 2026, and CRA lists applications as open.
  • Ontario first-time home buyers’ rebate. Up to $80,000 of the 8% provincial part of the HST, following the federal rebate’s eligibility rules. CRA says it is now available.

Ontario also has a temporary enhanced new housing rebate for any eligible buyer (not only first-timers) for builder agreements signed April 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027. Ask the builder and your lawyer which rebate is being credited on your statement of adjustments.

Who counts as a first-time buyer? It depends on the program

There is no single definition. Check each program’s own test before you count its dollars.

First-time tests on the official pages (as of 23 Oct 2026).
Program First-time test
Ontario LTT refund / Toronto MLTT rebateNever owned a home anywhere in the world, ever; spouse never owned one while your spouse. You cannot re-qualify.
B.C. first time home buyers’ exemptionNever owned a principal residence anywhere, and never received the exemption before.
FHSA, HBP, federal GST rebateDid not live in a home you (or your spouse or partner) owned in the current year or the previous four calendar years.

That gap matters for mid-career buyers. Someone who sold a home years ago and has rented since can often use the FHSA, the HBP and the new-home GST rebate again, but gets $0 from the Ontario or Toronto land transfer tax refund.

Parents helping a child buy. In Ontario, if a parent who has owned a home and a first-time-buyer child take title 50/50, the child can claim only 50% of the refund (at most $2,000 of the $4,000). If the parent is on title only because the bank insisted, pay the tax at registration and apply to the Ministry of Finance with proof the parent holds title in trust for the child (ontario.ca).

Do income and price caps disqualify mid-career buyers?

For down-payment pilots, often yes. Land transfer tax refunds care about first-time status and occupancy, not income.

Down-payment pilots are often built for households under a household-income ceiling (figures like $90,000–$150,000 appear on historical federal and municipal pages) and homes under a price cap. A 48-year-old household at $180,000 household income buying a $900,000 resale is usually outside those programs. Know which cap you are facing before you spend a Saturday on an application.

Can you stack provincial programs with the FHSA and HBP?

Often yes, if you qualify for each one on its own rules. They are separate tools that pay at different times.

CRA’s FHSA ($8,000 a year / $40,000 lifetime) and HBP ($60,000; for a first withdrawal in 2022 to 2028, repayments start in the fifth year after the withdrawal) do not care whether Ontario also refunds LTT. You can often use all three on one purchase if you independently qualify.

You cannot count the same dollars twice. The First-Time Home Buyer Incentive is not on this list — it closed to new applications on March 21, 2024. The GST/HST first-time rebate is a new-build tax tool, not a down payment. Sequence: qualify for each program on its own page, then build one closing worksheet.

Which expired programs still show up in search?

Mostly the closed federal Incentive and old municipal down-payment pilots. Treat any amount with no year and no .ca link as zero.

  • FTHBI “how to apply” posts after 21 Mar 2024.
  • Municipal RAP / down-payment pages that 301 to a “program closed” banner.
  • A dollar amount with no year and no URL.
  • Influencer math that adds Ontario LTT + Toronto rebate + a B.C. PTT number on the same house.

What should you verify before making an offer?

Six things, each on the official page: the URL and date, the first-time test, caps, occupancy, how it is claimed, and how it stacks.

  1. Official URL and “last updated” date captured in your notes (our figures are as of 23 Oct 2026).
  2. First-time definition, including spouse and foreign-property history.
  3. Income and price caps, if any.
  4. Occupancy deadline (Ontario LTT and Toronto MLTT: move in within nine months).
  5. Whether the lawyer claims it at registration, or you apply afterwards (Ontario: within 18 months).
  6. How it stacks with the FHSA, HBP and GST/HST rebates.

If buying only works with a grant you cannot confirm, run rent vs buy with zero in the grant column.

Frequently asked questions

Where should I look up first-time buyer programs in my province?

Start on the provincial or municipal .ca site, not a national listicle. Search the government domain, read the last-updated date, and ask your real-estate lawyer to confirm the program still funds your closing month.

Do all provinces offer a land-transfer-tax refund?

No. Ontario has a first-time refund (max $4,000) and Toronto adds a rebate (up to $4,475). B.C. exempts the first $500,000 of property transfer tax for homes up to $835,000. Alberta has no provincial land transfer tax. Québec’s welcome tax is municipal. Verify your jurisdiction.

Can I stack a provincial program with an FHSA and the Home Buyers’ Plan?

Often yes, because they are different tools — if you meet each program’s own rules. Do not count the same dollars twice. The First-Time Home Buyer Incentive is closed and is not part of the stack.

Why do I keep seeing programs that no longer exist?

Search rankings are slow. Pilots expire, funds run out, and blogs are not updated. If the official page says closed, the dollar amount on page four of Google is not closing cash.

Can I be a first-time buyer again if I sold a home years ago?

For the FHSA, the Home Buyers’ Plan and the federal new-home GST rebate, often yes: they look at whether you lived in a home you or your spouse owned in the current year or the previous four calendar years. Ontario’s land transfer tax refund is different: anyone who has ever owned a home anywhere does not qualify.

Is there a first-time buyer rebate for new homes in 2026?

Yes. The federal first-time home buyers’ GST/HST rebate can return up to $50,000 on a new home up to $1 million (builder agreements from March 20, 2025, before 2031), and Ontario adds up to $80,000 of the provincial part of the HST. Resales do not qualify.

Is this legal advice?

No. It is a research method. Your lawyer runs the affidavits and statements.
