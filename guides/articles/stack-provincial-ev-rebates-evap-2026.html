How to stack provincial EV rebates with federal EVAP in 2026 (province map)

Not every province still matches iZEV-era stacks · About 16 minutes · 18 Sep 2026

Buyers still walk into dealerships expecting 2022 math: a fat federal rebate plus a matching provincial cheque in every province. That map is gone. Transport Canada’s EVAP is the live federal passenger program. A handful of provinces still add money. Ontario, Alberta, Saskatchewan, and British Columbia generally do not — not on the vehicle. If your worksheet only works because you pasted an old iZEV-plus-CleanBC total, it does not work.

Federal mechanics (amounts, $50,000 rule, dealer portal) are in how EVAP replaced iZEV. This page is the stack: who still pays, used-EV notes, charger money that outlives purchase rebates, and a checklist before you put a deposit. Then re-run commuting TCO.

Disclosure: EV dealer research tools and Level 2 charger deals are offer types. Saving Optimizer may earn a commission if we later add partner links. We do not currently claim dealer or program partnerships. Provincial amounts change and envelopes close — this is orientation, not a guarantee you will be paid.

Key takeaways

  • EVAP Q&A: the federal incentive is applied on top of provincial/territorial programs where both exist.
  • Québec Roulez vert 2026: up to $2,000 new BEV, $1,000 used BEV; program scheduled to end 31 Dec 2026. Registration date sets the year.
  • Manitoba: $4,000 new / $2,500 used via MPI; original window to 31 Mar 2026, extended in Budget 2026 with no new sunset on the province page — verify remaining funds.
  • PEI remains among the last generous Atlantic passenger amounts in 2026 public guides (often cited ~$4,000 BEV). Ontario / Alberta / Saskatchewan / B.C. passenger: federal only.
  • Charger rebates can still exist when purchase rebates do not (BC Hydro $350). Do not mix the envelopes.

Québec Roulez vert timelines and used-EV notes

Québec’s own table (registration year): new fully electric $4,000 in 2025, $2,000 in 2026, $0 in 2027. Used fully electric: $2,000 / $1,000 / $0. PHEV amounts split by battery (8–15 kWh vs 15 kWh+). The government announced the wind-down in Budget 2024–2025: the program ends 31 December 2026. Only the registration date sets the amount — a 2026 deposit with a January 2027 registration is a 2027 row ($0).

Dealers can apply the rebate on the contract; you can also claim through program online services. Taxes are calculated on the price before the Québec rebate in program copy. Vehicles registered during the Feb–Mar 2025 pause were not eligible. Confirm the eligible-vehicle list and the $60,000-style MSRP cap on quebec.ca — it is not EVAP’s $50,000 final-transaction test.

Used EVs: Québec is one of the few places with a meaningful 2026 used amount. Federal EVAP will not stack on used. A $1,000 provincial used cheque is not a reason to skip a PPI — see private-sale diligence.

Manitoba / PEI stacking examples vs Ontario/Alberta gaps

Manitoba. Province page (reviewed Sep 2026): $4,000 new, $2,500 pre-owned, leases $1,000–$4,000 by term. Original program 1 Jul 2024–31 Mar 2026; extended in Budget 2026. Applications through MPI at evrebate.mpi.mb.ca after registration. Public guides cite a ~$70,000 MSRP cap and Manitoba-dealer / in-province registration rules. Tesla and some China-built exclusions have appeared in 2025–2026 explainers — read the live MPI FAQ, not a screenshot. Worked sketch (labelled): $42,000 eligible BEV − $5,000 EVAP − $4,000 MPI = $33,000 before tax. That is the stack Ontario does not have.

PEI. 2026 public guides still show ~$4,000 BEV / $2,000 PHEV at Island dealerships (out-of-province Atlantic purchases sometimes need a post-registration application). Tesla exclusions appear in some provincial rules. Confirm the PEI energy/transport page the week you buy — Atlantic programs have closed before.

Ontario and Alberta. No passenger purchase rebate. EVAP only, if the deal clears the $50,000 (or Canadian-made) test. A $52,000 loaded compact in Toronto does not become eligible because “Alberta used to have something.” B.C. CleanBC passenger rebate paused in 2024; do not add $4,000 to a Surrey worksheet.

Do not relocate on paper. Programs check residency, registration, and often the selling dealer. A friend’s Manitoba address is fraud, not a stack.

Charger rebates that still exist when purchase rebates do not

B.C. is the teaching case: $0 passenger provincial, still up to $350 (plus $200 load management) from BC Hydro for a listed Level 2. Québec still pairs Roulez vert with ~$600 station assistance through 2026. Ontario: vehicle EVAP, charger usually full freight. Walk the wall-unit math in Level 2 costs. Never tell yourself the charger rebate “is EVAP.”

Eligibility gotchas (MSRP vs transaction value)

  • EVAP looks at final transaction value for most cars ($50,000), including options and dealer fees in common explainers, excluding some taxes/PDI/winter tires — confirm Transport Canada’s definition on the assessment. Canadian-made: no that cap. Production must be in Canada or an FTA country.
  • Québec has used MSRP bands. A car can miss EVAP at $51,000 transaction and still get $2,000 Roulez vert if it is under the Québec cap — or miss both.
  • Declining federal years: dealer portal assessment date locks the EVAP column ($5,000 in 2026, $4,000 in 2027). A December delivery with a January file is a $1,000 haircut.
  • Leases are pro-rated federally (12-month minimum) and in Manitoba’s table. A short lease is not a full $5,000+$4,000 grab.
  • One federal personal incentive per person for the program life in public summaries. Two adults, two VINs, two files.

A decision checklist before you put a deposit

  1. Open tc.canada.ca EVAP: remaining funds, vehicle list, $50,000 definition, dealer enrolment.
  2. Open your provincial page the same morning. Note whether it is accepting applications and what proof of residency they want.
  3. Ask the dealer in email: EVAP assessment calendar year? Transaction value they will file? Will they process the provincial amount on the contract or is that your homework (Manitoba MPI cheque after registration)?
  4. Re-run TCO without the provincial line. If the deal dies, you cannot afford a closed envelope.
  5. If used: skip EVAP, check only provincial used amounts, then PPI and lien checks.

Where to verify official pages the week you buy

  • Federal: Transport Canada EVAP home, overview, Q&A (stacking is Q16 in the 2026 Q&A), vehicle list.
  • Québec: quebec.ca Roulez vert (amounts, end date, used table, normative framework).
  • Manitoba: gov.mb.ca EV rebate + evrebate.mpi.mb.ca.
  • PEI / territories / NL: the provincial energy or transportation incentive page — not a Facebook group.
  • B.C. charger: bchydro.com home charger rebate (vehicle is federal-only).

Programs are first-come. A blog dated March 2026 is a starting map. Your deposit date is not a grandfather clause unless the statute says so. Québec is explicit that registration date wins. Believe that.

Frequently asked questions

Can I stack a provincial EV rebate with EVAP?

Transport Canada says the federal incentive is applied on top of any provincial or territorial program. That only helps if your province still has a passenger rebate and you meet both rule sets. Ontario, Alberta, Saskatchewan, and B.C. passenger purchases are typically federal-only in 2026.

When does Québec’s Roulez vert end?

The Québec government scheduled Roulez vert to end 31 December 2026. 2026 new fully electric amounts are up to $2,000; used fully electric up to $1,000. Registration date sets the amount, not the deposit date.

Is Manitoba’s $4,000 rebate still open?

The original window ran to 31 March 2026. Budget 2026 extended the program. Manitoba’s own page still lists $4,000 new / $2,500 used via MPI. It did not publish a new sunset in that copy — treat remaining funds as first-come. Verify evrebate.mpi.mb.ca the week you buy.

Does EVAP’s $50,000 cap apply to the provincial rebate too?

No. Each program has its own price test. Québec has used a $60,000 MSRP-style cap; Manitoba a $70,000 MSRP-style cap in public guides. A car can miss EVAP and still get a provincial amount, or the reverse. Read both definitions on the bill of sale.

Do used EVs get the federal rebate?

No. EVAP is new vehicles. Québec still lists a used fully electric amount in 2026 ($1,000). Manitoba lists $2,500 used. That is provincial only.
