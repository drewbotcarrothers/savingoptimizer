The Adoption Expense Tax Credit in Canada

Claim it once, in the year the adoption period ends · About 6 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 24 Nov 2026

If you adopt a child in Canada, you can claim up to $19,580 of eligible adoption expenses per child on your federal return for 2025, and some provinces add their own credit. You claim it once, in the tax year the adoption period ends, even if the process took years. Keep every receipt from the start.

Key takeaways

  • Federal maximum for 2025: $19,580 per child (CRA, checked 24 Nov 2026).
  • Provincial maximums vary: Ontario $15,551, Alberta $19,354, BC $19,580.
  • Claim in the tax year the adoption period ends.
  • Subtract any reimbursements or assistance.
  • The child must be under 18 when the adoption order is issued.

What counts

  • Fees to a licensed adoption agency.
  • Court costs and legal and administrative fees.
  • Reasonable travel and living costs for the child and parents.
  • Document translation.
  • Mandatory fees to a foreign institution and the child's immigration costs.

How to claim

Enter the federal amount on line 31300 and the provincial amount on line 58330 of your provincial Form 428. The adoption period starts when you apply to a provincial ministry, a licensed agency or a Canadian court, and ends at the later of the adoption order or when the child comes to live with you permanently. Don't send receipts; keep them in case the CRA asks.

Example: $19,580 of eligible expenses at the 15% federal rate is a federal credit of about $2,937. See Canada child benefit amounts and EI parental benefits.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the adoption tax credit in Canada?

You can claim up to $19,580 of eligible expenses per child federally for 2025.

When do I claim adoption expenses?

In the tax year the adoption period ends, even if it took more than one year.

What expenses can I claim?

Licensed agency fees, legal and court costs, reasonable travel, translation, and mandatory foreign and immigration fees.

Do provinces have an adoption credit?

Some do, including Ontario, Alberta, BC, Manitoba, Newfoundland and Labrador and Yukon.

Can both parents claim?

Yes, but the combined claim can't exceed the maximum for the child.
