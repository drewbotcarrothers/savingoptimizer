How to calculate your real cost of owning a car in Canada (2026 framework)

Ratehub $1,373 context, provincial layers, per-km · About 17 minutes · 16 Sep 2026

Most owners can name the car payment and the last fill-up. Few can name insurance to the dollar, the condo stall, or the fact that a paid-off car still burns capital. Ratehub’s 2026 national cash mix lands around $1,373 per month — a slight bump from $1,370 in 2025 — and even that line excludes a clean depreciation charge if you bought with cash. This framework is how you build your number, with provincial fees and parking on top of that national average.

Use it before you keep a second car, before you finance a new vs used compact, and before you reject Communauto or Zipcar as “expensive.”

Disclosure: Insurance quote tools and auto-loan comparison tools are offer types. Saving Optimizer may earn a commission if we later add partner links. No lender or insurer partnerships are claimed. Ratehub’s $1,373 figure is their 2026 published mix, reviewed 16 Sep 2026 — not a quote for your VIN.

Key takeaways

  • Ratehub 2026 cash TCO ≈ $1,373/month ($522 principal, $192 interest, $165 gas, $120 maintenance, $10 admin, $200 parking, $164 insurance).
  • CAA “cost per km” tables that omit insurance, loan, and parking will look cheap. They are a different instrument.
  • Parking in Toronto, Vancouver, and Montréal can be a permit (~$20s) or a financed stall (~$400–$600). The $200 average hides both.
  • Build per-month and per-km. High fixed / low km is how second cars fail the test.
  • Opportunity cost: cash tied in a $20,000 car is not earning a GIC. Count it if you are deciding sell vs keep.

Cash costs vs opportunity cost of capital

Cash costs leave the account: loan, insurance, gas, parking, repairs, plates. Economic costs also include depreciation (the car’s market value this January vs next) and what the equity in the car could have earned.

If you owe $18,000 at 6%, interest is cash (Ratehub’s $192/month is their mix). If you own a $18,000 car outright, you have no interest line — you still have depreciation and an opportunity-cost line. A simple method: 4% of current private-sale value / 12. On $18,000 that is $60/month. Not precise. Better than $0.

Do not double-count principal and depreciation. Principal is how you pay the bank; depreciation is how the asset fades. For a keep-or-sell decision on a paid-off car, use depreciation + opportunity cost + operating cash. For “can I afford this loan,” use the cash table.

Insurance, registration, and inspection by province

Insurance is provincial religion. Ratehub’s mix uses $164/month (~$1,968/year). Their quoting site has also cited ~$1,973 national average at the start of 2026. Ontario FSRA-linked averages in consumer copy sit higher in the GTA than rural. Quebec splits SAAQ (injury) and private damage. ICBC, MPI, SGI are public-auto worlds. Shop with broker vs direct education — then paste your premium here, not the blog average.

Registration / plates / inspection: a small admin line in Ratehub ($10/month). Real life: Ontario plate sticker era vs new rules, B.C. ICBC insurance/renewal combo, Quebec SAAQ licence, safety certificates on used transfers. Put the annual bill / 12. Safety certs belong in the year you buy, not every year (unless your province inspects annually).

Parking: street, condo, and workplace

Ratehub plugs $200/month. RIDEZ-style city pieces argue urban parking can add 15–40% to TCO when you count condo stalls. Toronto residential permits can be cheap relative to a stall you financed at $80,000 in a downtown tower. Workplace “free” parking is a taxable-benefit conversation with CRA in some cases (high level: if it is a prized downtown stall, it is not emotionally free even when Payroll is quiet).

Write three numbers: home, work, and typical outing (hospital, concert). Tickets are parking by other means.

Fuel or electricity + maintenance reserves

Ratehub 2026 gas: $165/month (down from $203 in 2025 in their table). Your km and the vehicle’s L/100 km own this line. Electricity: home off-peak vs $0.50+/kWh highway DC. Do not use gasoline averages for an EV.

Maintenance in that table jumped to $120/month in 2026 from $79. Treat it as a reminder to fund a reserve, not as CAA’s exact RAV4. Winter tires, wipers, and one set of brakes belong in the reserve. 407 ETR is not maintenance; put tolls on their own line if you commute that highway.

Pump loyalty (PC / Petro-Points / Scene+) is cents — see gas programs after the dashboard exists.

Per-km and per-month dashboards

Per-month = sum of the table. Per-km = (cash total − parking you would pay anyway?) / km, or more honestly: variable (fuel, tolls, wear) / km plus a separate fixed stack.

Example method: $400 fixed (insurance + parking + depreciation) + $0.18/km variable. A 8 km store run is $1.44 variable and $0 extra fixed. The question is whether that trip needed the $400 stack to exist. If the stack exists anyway, use the car. If the stack exists because of those trips, price Communauto hours instead.

15,000 km/year at $1,373/month is about $1.10/km all-in in the Ratehub cartoon ($16,476 / 15,000). Your km might be 8,000 — then all-in per km explodes because the $400 stall did not shrink.

Compare against transit + car-share alternatives

Build a second column:

  • Transit actuals: TTC cap ceiling $155.10 adult or $143 12-Month; GO PAYG for your stations; Compass/OPUS as applicable (overview, TTC, GO).
  • Car-share hours from last month’s “car jobs.”
  • Occasional rental or intercity bus/VIA if that is the weekend pattern.

If column B is $500 and column A is $1,200, you have a project. If column B is $1,100 because you still need a van for work, you have a cheaper vehicle project, not a fantasy bus commute. New EV incentives (EVAP) change purchase math; they do not erase parking.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average cost of owning a car in Canada in 2026?

Ratehub’s 2026 cash table sums to about $1,373 per month for a typical financed mix of new and used, including $522 principal, $192 interest, $165 gas, $120 maintenance, $10 admin, $200 parking, and $164 insurance. It is a national sketch, not your household. CAA operating-cost tools often omit insurance, loan, and parking — do not mix the frameworks.

What costs do people forget?

Parking (home, work, street), depreciation if the car is paid off, winter tires, 407 and tickets, opportunity cost of the cash tied up, and insurance on a car that barely moves. Fuel is visible; stalls are not.

Should I use per-kilometre or per-month?

Both. Per-month shows the fixed stack (insurance, parking, depreciation). Per-km shows whether an extra trip should be transit, car-share, or the car you already insure. High fixed cost plus low km is the car-share use case.

Does Ratehub include depreciation?

Their published 2026 cash table is principal, interest, gas, maintenance, admin, parking, and insurance — cash out the door. Economic depreciation on a cash-bought car is extra. Principal is not the same as depreciation, but it is the cash cousin if you financed.

How do I compare this to transit?

Put last month’s car dashboard next to TTC/GO/Compass/OPUS actual fares plus car-share. See TTC capping, GO PAYG, and the transit overview. If the car’s avoidable cost exceeds those by a wide margin and the bus can do the job, you have your answer.
