The Real Cost of Being a Wedding Guest in Canada: Gift, Travel, Hotel, Outfit and Pre-Wedding Events

Add up every piece before you RSVP, then decide what you can do · About 10 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 1 Oct 2026

Being a wedding guest in Canada can cost far more than the gift once you add travel, a hotel, an outfit and pre-wedding events like showers and bachelor or bachelorette parties. Before you RSVP, add up every piece. For an out-of-town wedding, hotel taxes alone add up quickly: in Toronto, a 6% Municipal Accommodation Tax plus 13% HST apply on top of the room rate as of 1 October 2026. For a wedding abroad, add a passport if needed ($163.50 for 10 years for adults) and travel health insurance, which travel.gc.ca advises buying before any trip outside Canada. Then decide what you can do: attend everything, attend some events, or decline warmly and send a gift.

Key takeaways

  • Total the gift, travel, hotel, outfit and pre-wedding events.
  • Toronto hotels add 6% MAT and 13% HST to the room rate.
  • Weddings abroad: passport, travel health insurance and possibly time off.
  • You can attend the ceremony only, or some events, or decline.
  • Your gift can reflect your travel costs.
  • Example with made-up inputs: local vs out-of-town worksheet.

Gift

The gift is the cost most people think of first. See how much to give for ways to set an amount. If you are travelling or are part of the wedding party, it is reasonable to factor those costs into the gift. The CRA lists most gifts among amounts that are not reported or taxed, so cash gifts are not income for the couple.

Hotel taxes vary by city. The City of Toronto says its temporary 8.5% Municipal Accommodation Tax rate ended on 1 August 2026, and the rate is now 6%. Add 13% HST in Ontario. If the couple has arranged a room block, compare it with other options and check its cancellation date.

If a flight goes wrong

If you fly and the flight is delayed or cancelled for reasons within the airline's control that are not about safety, the Canadian Transportation Agency says large airlines owe $400 for arriving 3 to 6 hours late, $700 for 6 to 9 hours and $1,000 for 9 hours or more; small airlines owe $125, $250 and $500. You must have been told 14 days or less before departure and claim in writing within one year. Book a flight that arrives a day early for important weddings, so a delay does not mean missing the ceremony, and keep boarding passes and receipts in case you need to claim.

Weddings abroad

  • Passport: IRCC's adult fees from 31 March 2026 are $163.50 for 10 years and $122.50 for 5 years.
  • Insurance: travel.gc.ca says your provincial plan may cover none or only a small part of medical costs abroad and never pays up front, and recommends trip interruption and travel health insurance before you leave.
  • Travel advice: check the destination's advisory on travel.gc.ca.
  • Costs: resort minimum stays, transfers and excursions can add up; see destination weddings for couples and guests.

Outfit

Rewearing something you own is the cheapest option. Renting, borrowing or buying second-hand also work for formal dress codes. Check the dress code on the invitation or wedding website before buying anything, and remember shoes, accessories, hair and any alterations in the total. If you are in the wedding party, ask early whether the couple is covering outfits; see formal outfits on a budget.

Pre-wedding events

Engagement parties, showers and bachelor or bachelorette parties each bring costs: a gift, travel and a share of the event. Wedding party members may face the most. See bachelor and bachelorette party splits for fair ways to share costs. You do not have to attend everything.

If you are in the wedding party

Bridesmaids, groomsmen and other attendants often carry the largest costs: an outfit chosen by the couple, hair and makeup, multiple pre-wedding events, a share of the bachelor or bachelorette party and sometimes travel. Before saying yes, ask the couple what is expected and what they are covering. It is better to discuss a budget limit at the start than to drop out later. If you agree to be in the wedding party, ask for the dates of every event so you can plan time off and travel early.

Ways to cut costs

  • Share a room or rental with other guests.
  • Travel by train or carpool where practical.
  • Book refundable accommodation early, then compare prices again closer to the date.
  • Rewear, borrow or rent an outfit.
  • Combine the gift with travel savings, such as a group gift.
  • Skip optional pre-wedding events without guilt.

Saying no or attending partially

If the full cost is too much, consider attending the ceremony but not the destination trip, going to the local shower but not the travel weekend, or attending the wedding and skipping a second-night event. If you cannot attend, reply promptly, say you are sorry to miss it, and send a gift or card. A quick, honest reply helps the couple plan their numbers.

Worksheet

  1. Gift.
  2. Travel: flights or gas, parking, transfers.
  3. Accommodation with all taxes.
  4. Meals and local transport.
  5. Outfit, hair, accessories.
  6. Pre-wedding events.
  7. Passport, insurance, time off, child or pet care.
  8. Total, then decide what fits.

Example with made-up inputs

These numbers are an example with made-up inputs, apart from Toronto's 6% MAT, Ontario's 13% HST and IRCC passport fees. A local wedding: $200 gift, $60 rideshares, $0 outfit (rewear), $50 shower gift: $310. An out-of-town wedding in Toronto for a guest from Ottawa: $150 gift, $220 train, two hotel nights at $230 ($460, plus $27.60 MAT and $59.80 HST on the room rate, $547.40), $150 meals, $80 local transport, $120 rented outfit, a $180 bachelorette share: $1,447.40. A wedding in Mexico adds flights, a resort stay and $163.50 if a passport is needed.

Common mistakes

  • RSVPing before adding up costs.
  • Forgetting accommodation taxes.
  • Booking non-refundable travel too early.
  • Skipping travel health insurance abroad.
  • Feeling obliged to attend every event.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to be a wedding guest?

It depends on the gift, travel, accommodation, outfit and pre-wedding events. Add them up before you RSVP.

What taxes apply to a Toronto hotel?

A 6% Municipal Accommodation Tax on stays from 1 August 2026, plus 13% HST.

Can I give a smaller gift if I travel to the wedding?

It is reasonable to factor travel costs into the gift. The amount is your choice.

Do I need travel insurance for a wedding abroad?

travel.gc.ca advises buying trip interruption and travel health insurance before leaving Canada, since provincial plans may cover little or nothing abroad.

What if my flight to the wedding is delayed?

Large airlines owe $400 to $1,000 for long delays within their control and not safety-related, if claimed in writing within a year.

Is it rude to decline a wedding because of cost?

No. Reply promptly and kindly, and send a card or gift if you can.
