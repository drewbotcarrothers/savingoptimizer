Renting a condo in Canada: fee inclusions, amenities, and questions that save money

Fees included is not everything included · About 16 minutes · 20 September 2026

Condo renters assume amenities are free and discover parking, locker, and guest-suite fees after the e-transfer. “Condo fees included” is a real sentence — it usually means the landlord pays the corporation. It is not a synonym for heat, hydro, a stall, or tenant insurance. Write the stack before you celebrate a sticker win.

CMHC’s condominium FAQs exist because buildings share costs. Tenants do not get the status certificate, but they still live inside the rules. Hunt listings with the Kijiji / PadMapper discipline; compare heat on the lease-inclusion worksheet.

Key takeaways

  • “Fees included” = common-element invoice, not automatically suite utilities.
  • Parking, locker, and amenity guest fees are often extra. Put dollars on the viewing notes.
  • Ask submeter vs bulk before you sign — submeter guide.
  • Move-in elevator deposits and COIs are a first-class cost — elevator rules.
  • Special assessments are usually the owner’s problem; extra rent still needs a lawful path.

What “condo fees included” usually means for tenants

The owner pays monthly common expenses. That pot typically covers caretaker, building insurance, common-area hydro, amenities, and a contribution to the reserve. You pay rent. Those two invoices should not be double-counted — and they should not be silently missing your stall.

Parking, lockers, and amenity access gotchas

  • Parking: included stall, extra $100–$250+/month in many downtown markets, or “wait-list, good luck.” Tandem stalls and EV chargers have their own rules and fees. Street permits are a different invoice — Transportation covers Toronto residential permits.
  • Locker: not implied by a one-bed. Measure what you own before you sign a small unit — volume you cannot store becomes mover cost.
  • Amenities: gym and pool are often in the fee. Guest suites and party rooms are often $50–$150/night plus a deposit. Visitor parking can be hourly. Ask before your parents book a flight.
  • Fobs: extra fobs sometimes cost $50–$150. Count how many you need.

A $150 stall plus $80 locker plus $60 hydro can erase a “fees included” sticker win. Put them next to rent the way the roommate page puts add-ons next to a 2-bed split.

Submetered utilities vs bulk

Ask which commodities have a suite meter. Bulk-included electricity is predictable. Submetered electricity with electric heat is a winter story — demand January/February PDFs. The submeter page is the worksheet. Do not copy a $40 hydro line from a fan-coil listing onto a baseboard listing.

Moving and guest suite rules that cost money

Towers treat move-in as a reserved utility:

  • Book the freight elevator 2–4 weeks out; weekdays only is common.
  • COI from the movers, often $2 million naming the corporation.
  • Refundable damage deposit — confirm whether you or the landlord posts it.

If you need a guest suite the week the furniture arrives, book that separately. A blown elevator slot plus a downtown hotel is how “cheap rent” loses. Full pattern notes: condo moving rules.

Insurance requirements landlords impose

Leases commonly demand proof of tenant insurance with $1 million or $2 million liability and a contents limit that covers your stuff. The corporation’s policy is not your couch. Compare policies on coverage, not the cheapest PDF — tenant insurance shopping. Going bare to save ~$20/month is how a dishwasher leak becomes a relationship and a collections file.

Ask whether the landlord wants to be listed as an interested party. That is a one-line broker request, not a reason to skip insurance.

Who pays if a special assessment hits

The corporation invoices the owner. You pay rent. A one-off “assessment surcharge” on your tenant invoice is usually a refuse unless a statute allows that exact fee. Ontario guideline / N1, B.C. RTB-7, and Québec TAL still apply — levy page, pushback.

Ask for a lease sentence: landlord pays all special assessments and corporation fee increases; tenant pays rent and the utilities listed. Construction noise is a maintenance conversation, not a silent extra $200.

Viewing checklist unique to condo towers

  1. Fees included — which commodities, which stalls, which lockers.
  2. Heat source and last-winter hydro if extra.
  3. Parking / locker numbers and monthly extra.
  4. Amenity guest fees and visitor parking.
  5. Move-in: elevator lead time, deposit payer, COI.
  6. Tenant insurance limit required.
  7. Who pays specials; any levy “being discussed.”
  8. In-suite laundry, dryer type, and whether it is on your meter.
  9. Smoking, pets, and short-term guest rules (bylaws, not just the landlord’s vibe).
  10. Cell reception and internet: fibre in the building vs you shop a provider.
  11. Garbage room hours and whether you need a fob at 9 p.m.
  12. Window condition — failed seals are a repair email, not a $12 film project.

Bring the application package so a serious ask does not look like a delay tactic. Deposits still follow the province — ON / B.C. / Québec cash — not a national “first last plus key money.”

Frequently asked questions

What does “condo fees included” mean for a Canadian tenant?

It usually means the landlord pays the corporation or strata common-element fees (caretaker, building insurance, amenities, common hydro). It does not automatically include your suite’s electricity, parking, locker, guest-suite nights, or tenant insurance. Write each line on the lease.

Who pays a special assessment if I rent the condo?

The owner typically pays the levy. Passing it to you as extra rent still has to follow provincial increase rules. Ask for a lease sentence that says the landlord pays all special assessments. See the special-assessment budgeting page. Not legal advice.

Do I need tenant insurance in a condo tower?

Landlords commonly require $1 million or $2 million liability plus contents. The building’s cameras are not your policy. Compare coverage — lease requirement first, banner price second. Going bare to save $20 is how a dishwasher leak becomes a life event.

What condo moving costs should I budget as a renter?

Elevator booking, a refundable damage deposit, a certificate of insurance from the movers, and possible weekday-only windows. Confirm whether you or the landlord posts the deposit. Book both buildings. Details on the condo elevator page.

Is this legal advice?

No. Tenant-insurance comparison is an offer type. LTB, B.C. Residential Tenancy, and TAL pages speak to rent, deposits, and increases. CMHC condo FAQs explain buildings, not your lease.
