Bachelor and Bachelorette Parties in Canada: Costs, Fair Splits and Saying No

Agree on a per-person ceiling before anyone books anything · About 10 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 1 Oct 2026

Bachelor and bachelorette parties in Canada can cost anything from the price of dinner to a travel weekend, so the most important step is agreeing on a per-person budget before anything is booked. A common approach is for attendees to split the guest of honour's share of shared costs, but say so in advance. Split shared items evenly, keep optional extras pay-as-you-go, and give everyone a total that includes taxes: in Toronto, a hotel stay carries a 6% Municipal Accommodation Tax plus 13% HST as of 1 October 2026. Anyone who cannot afford the trip should be able to join part of it, or decline, without pressure.

Key takeaways

  • Set a per-person ceiling first.
  • Decide upfront whether attendees cover the guest of honour.
  • Split shared costs evenly; optional extras are pay-as-you-go.
  • Quote totals with taxes: Toronto hotels add 6% MAT plus 13% HST.
  • Offer a lower-cost way to join.
  • Example with made-up inputs: a split for eight people.

Who pays for the guest of honour

There is no rule. Options include: attendees split the honoree's share of shared costs (accommodation, dinner, activities); the honoree pays their own way; or the honoree pays travel and the group covers the rest. Whatever you choose, include it when you quote the per-person cost, so nobody is surprised. For a group of eight covering one honoree, each of seven people pays one-seventh of the shared costs instead of one-eighth. If the couple is having a joint party, decide whether both honorees are covered, which changes the split again. Write the arrangement in the first message to the group, along with the budget range, so people can decide whether to come before any money moves.

Splitting fairly

  • Shared fixed costs (rental, private room, guide): split evenly among those attending.
  • Shared food: split evenly if ordered family-style; otherwise pay for your own.
  • Drinks: pay your own, or a fixed kitty if everyone agrees.
  • Optional activities: only those who join pay.
  • Partial attendees: pay for the parts they attend.
  • Use one organizer to collect money before booking, and a shared spreadsheet or app to track who has paid and what is still owed.

Hotel and accommodation taxes

Accommodation prices are often shown before taxes. In Toronto, the City says the temporary 8.5% Municipal Accommodation Tax rate ended on 1 August 2026, and the rate on transient accommodation is now 6%. HST at 13% also applies in Ontario. Other cities and provinces have their own accommodation taxes; check the total at checkout before splitting the cost.

Flights and travel protection

If the group flies, Canada's Air Passenger Protection Regulations require a large airline to pay $400 if you arrive 3 to 6 hours late, $700 for 6 to 9 hours and $1,000 for 9 hours or more, when the delay or cancellation is within its control and not required for safety, you were told 14 days or less before departure, and you claim in writing within one year. Small airlines owe $125, $250 or $500. For trips outside Canada, travel.gc.ca advises buying trip interruption and travel health insurance before leaving, because provincial plans may cover none or only a small part of care abroad and never pay up front.

Getting home safely

Build safe transport into the plan and the budget: a designated driver, taxis or rideshares, transit, or accommodation within walking distance. Share the address of the accommodation and the plan with everyone, and keep one person responsible for checking that the whole group gets back. If anyone drives the next morning, plan for that too.

Gifts and extras

Decide early whether the group is also buying a gift, matching shirts or decorations, and include those costs in the per-person quote. Small extras add up quickly across a group of eight or ten, and they are the easiest items to cut if the total runs high.

Saying no politely

It is fine to decline, or to come to part of the event. A short, warm message works: say you are excited for the couple, that the trip is not in your budget this year, and offer to join the local dinner, help plan, or contribute to a gift. Organizers can make this easier by offering a lower-cost option and not asking people to explain. A friend saying no to the bachelor party is not saying no to the wedding.

Booking tips

  1. Poll the group on dates and a budget range first.
  2. Book refundable accommodation until numbers are firmly confirmed.
  3. Collect deposits before paying anything yourself.
  4. Share an itinerary with costs per item.
  5. Check cancellation terms for activities.
  6. Keep receipts and settle up within a week.

Example with made-up inputs

These numbers are an example with made-up inputs, apart from Toronto's 6% MAT and Ontario's 13% HST. Eight people go to Toronto for one night; seven pay for the honoree. Two hotel rooms at a made-up $260 each are $520, plus 6% MAT ($31.20) and 13% HST on the room rate ($67.60), for $618.80 (check your actual bill, since tax calculations can differ). A group dinner is $640 including tax and tip, and an activity is $45 per person, $360. Shared total: $1,618.80. Split seven ways: about $231.26 each. Drinks and transport are on each person.

Common mistakes

  • Booking before agreeing on a budget.
  • Quoting room prices without taxes.
  • Surprising people with the honoree's share.
  • Pressuring people who cannot afford it.
  • One person fronting everything and chasing payments later.

Frequently asked questions

Who pays for the bride or groom at a bachelor or bachelorette party?

There is no rule. A common approach is for attendees to split the honoree's share, but agree before booking.

How do you split bachelor party costs fairly?

Split shared fixed costs evenly, pay your own drinks and optional activities, and charge partial attendees only for what they attend.

What taxes apply to a Toronto hotel?

A 6% Municipal Accommodation Tax on stays beginning 1 August 2026, plus 13% HST.

What if our flight is delayed?

Large airlines owe $400, $700 or $1,000 for delays of 3, 6 or 9+ hours within their control and not safety-related, if you claim within a year.

Do we need travel insurance for a trip to the U.S.?

travel.gc.ca advises buying trip interruption and travel health insurance before any trip outside Canada.

Is it rude to skip the bachelor party?

No. Decline warmly, offer to join a local part or help, and keep it brief.
