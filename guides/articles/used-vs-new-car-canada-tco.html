Used vs new car in Canada: total cost of ownership math for households

Depreciation, insurance, CPO, EV incentives · About 16 minutes · 16 Sep 2026

Households compare the sticker on a new compact to the asking price on a 2019 with 140,000 km and call that research. Insurance, interest, depreciation, and the shop bill never enter the spreadsheet. This is Canadian TCO — including the insurance quote and, if you are eyeing a new EV, EVAP — not a U.S. lease-hack thread.

Ratehub’s 2026 all-in cash mix for a typical financed car is about $1,373/month. That blend of new and used is a ceiling conversation, not your VIN. Build the lines. Then pick used, CPO, or new.

Disclosure: Auto-loan rate comparison tools and insurance quote tools are offer types. Saving Optimizer may earn a commission if we later add partner links. We do not arrange credit or bind insurance. Worked dollar examples below are labelled assumptions, not quotes.

Key takeaways

  • Depreciation and interest dominate new. Repair variance dominates older used. CPO sits in the middle as paid risk transfer.
  • Always quote insurance on both vehicles. A “cheap” used performance car can cost more per month than a boring new Honda.
  • EVAP does not apply to used. It can flip a new-EV vs used-gas comparison if the car actually qualifies.
  • Do a PPI (pre-purchase inspection) on private sales. Ontario UVIP and CARFAX Canada are inputs, not a mechanical inspection.
  • If the car would sit, read car-share vs owning before you finance anything.

Purchase price, interest, and depreciation curves

New: you pay retail (or a little under) and then own an asset that is worth a lot less the day you plate it. Used: you buy a chunk of that loss from the first owner. Five-year-old used has already done the steepest curve; fifteen-year-old used has a different curve (parts, rust, shop labour).

Interest: a new-car 4.9% special on a $32,000 loan can still cost thousands. A used 8.9% on $18,000 can cost more interest than you think because the rate is ugly. Compare total interest in the amortization, not the monthly debit. If cash is an option, the “payment” is an opportunity-cost problem — see the TCO dashboard.

Insurance rating differences new vs used

Insurers price the vehicle, the drivers, and the postal code. New often means higher collision/comprehensive because the payout is larger. Some late-model crossovers also sit on theft lists. Used can be cheaper to insure — or not, if it is a sport trim, rebuilt title, or a car that parts thieves like.

Process: same broker/direct worksheet, two VINs, same deductibles (how to shop). Lenders will force collision on a financed car. A $4,000 beater you own outright can drop collision — FSRA even flags collision as questionable under ~$2,000 value — which is a used-only lever.

Maintenance and surprise repairs on older vehicles

CAA-style operating costs (fuel, maintenance, licence) often look gentle because they average. Your year is not average: water pump, rusted brake lines, a $4,200 CVT. Budget a repair reserve on anything out of warranty: $80–$150/month into a separate account is a method, not a prediction. If you cannot fund the reserve, you cannot fund the car — you are waiting for a credit card emergency.

Canadian winter: undercoating debates, winter tires (mandatory discount ask in Ontario), and rust. A cheap import from a no-salt province can still be a gift. A cheap GTA car with swollen rear quarters is a leak.

Warranty and CPO as risk transfer

New: full factory warranty. That is worth something if you would have paid the shop. It is worth less if you drive 8,000 km a year in a simple appliance.

CPO: dealer-certified used, extra inspection, remaining/extended warranty, often a higher price than private sale. You are buying process and a phone number. Price the CPO premium against (a) private price + PPI + reserve and (b) new. If CPO costs almost as much as a base new car on sale, the new car’s warranty and EVAP (if EV) may win.

Private sale: Ontario UVIP (Used Vehicle Information Package) is a history snapshot, not a clean bill. CARFAX Canada / equivalent: liens, brands, odometer tales — still hire a mechanic. Out-of-province cars need extra caution on rust and registration.

Fuel and possible EV incentives if considering new EV

Fuel in Ratehub’s 2026 mix is $165/month — down from 2025 in their table. Electricity for a commute EV can undercut that if you charge at home off-peak; public DC fast charge can erase the win. New eligible EVs may take EVAP ($5,000 / $2,500 in 2026) if they clear the $50,000 final-value rule or are Canadian-made. Used EVs: no EVAP; some provinces (Manitoba) have advertised used rebates — verify.

Do not buy a new EV to “save gas” if you drive 6,000 km a year. The capital is the story. Do not skip a qualifying EVAP car if you were buying new anyway and the model fits.

When new still wins (reliability need, long hold, incentives)

  • You will keep the car 10–12 years and you hate shops. New (or young CPO) spreads depreciation over a long hold.
  • A qualifying new EV with EVAP, cheap home charging, and insurance that does not explode.
  • Employer or medical need for uptime that a $14,000 car cannot promise.
  • 0% or very cheap manufacturer money that is still cheaper than used-loan rates after you model residual. Cheap money on a trim you did not need is not cheap.

When used wins: you have a mechanic, a PPI, a repair reserve, and you were never going to keep a new car longer than four years (the expensive years).

When neither wins: transit plus car-share.

Frequently asked questions

Is a used car always cheaper in Canada?

Often on five-year cash, because someone else ate the steepest depreciation. Not always: a cheap used car with a failed transmission, a high insurance rating, or a 9.9% loan can lose to a modest new car held ten years — or to no car.

Does new-car insurance cost more?

Frequently. Replacement cost is higher, some theft rings prefer late-model SUVs, and lenders require collision. Quote both vehicles with the same drivers and deductibles. Do not use a national average.

Is certified pre-owned worth it?

CPO is a price you pay to transfer some repair risk and to get a remaining factory-style warranty. It is not magic. Compare CPO premium versus an independent PPI plus a savings buffer. See also provincial consumer protections and vehicle history (CARFAX Canada, not the US report).

Should EV rebates change the used-vs-new decision?

EVAP (from 16 Feb 2026) is for eligible new EVs — up to $5,000 BEV / $2,500 PHEV with a $50,000 final-value rule (no cap if made in Canada). It does not apply to used. A new EV with EVAP can beat a used gas car on energy cost; run TCO, not the rebate headline.

What about auto loans?

Price the payment with a loan-rate comparison, then ignore the payment and look at total interest plus depreciation. A longer term on a new car can hide a worse TCO. Offer type: rate-comparison tools — not a lender.
