Used vs New Car in Canada: Total Cost of Ownership Math for Households

Sticker price is not the bill · About 16 minutes · 16 Sep 2026

Households compare two window stickers and call it research. Ratehub’s 2026 Canadian model — using AutoTrader Q3 2025 average selling prices — still finds a used car cheaper over nine years by about $32,000 ($120,420 vs $152,292). That gap is interest, depreciation, and a longer new-car loan, not a moral victory. It can vanish if the used car needs an engine or if a new EV clears EVAP and a provincial stack.

This is Canadian TCO with insurance and incentive context, not a U.S. lease-hack thread. Use the worksheet in real cost of owning a car for your actual postal code.

Disclosure: Auto loan rate comparison tools and insurance quote tools are opportunity types. Saving Optimizer may earn a commission if we later add partner links. We do not issue loans or policies. Compare lenders and licensed insurers directly; never finance points or stretch a term to “afford new.”

Key takeaways

  • Ratehub 2026: used ~$1,343/month while financed vs new ~$1,504; nine-year cash ~$120k vs ~$152k.
  • New-car average sticker in that build: $63,264 (plus tax). Used: $36,911. Your compact will not match those averages — they mix trucks and luxury.
  • Depreciation is steepest early. Buying a 3–5 year old car is how households skip the cliff.
  • Quote insurance on both VINs. New ADAS bumpers are not theoretical.
  • New still wins for warranty-critical use, long holds, and documented EV incentives — not for status.

Purchase price, interest, and depreciation curves

Ratehub’s methodology (updated 29 Apr 2026, figures from 2025 data): average new APR about 6.45% (StatsCan Dec 2025 context they cite); used financing often 10–12% with shorter terms (~6 years vs ~8). A cheaper used sticker can still have a painful payment if the rate is junk. Shop the loan as hard as the car — shorter term if you can, cash down to cover tax if you can, never a 96-month trap on a depreciating asset.

Depreciation: Ratehub’s Volkswagen Atlas example was about $559/month of value lost over five years, ~10.6%/year, with a vehicle often worth about half after five. That is not cash leaving the chequing account, but it is what you do not get back at trade-in. The first three years are where new cars “pay” that cliff. A used car already ate someone else’s cliff.

Insurance rating differences new vs used

Insurers price the car, not your feelings. A new VIN can mean higher comprehensive (theft, glass, sensors) even if you are the same driver. Some used cars are cheaper to insure; some stolen-in-the-GTA used SUVs are not. Shop quotes on both vehicles with the same deductibles before you fall in love at the dealer. Ratehub’s national insurance sketch ($164/month) is an average mix, not a compact vs compact comparison.

Maintenance and surprise repairs on older vehicles

Ratehub uses about $120/month maintenance in the 2026 cash stack, up sharply from $79 the year before (tariffs, shop rates, J.D. Power visit costs). StatsCan-style household repair series have been rising; independent shops also report data-access friction that pushes some jobs back to dealers.

A 10-year-old car with a known timing-chain job is a different product from a 4-year-old CPO. Budget a repair reserve: many households use $1,000–$2,000/year on older vehicles and still come out ahead of new-car depreciation. The failure mode is a $6,000 surprise with no savings. That is a PPI and a Carfax Canada / provincial history problem, not a reason to buy every car new.

Warranty and CPO as risk transfer

Factory warranty on new is included in the sticker. Certified pre-owned wraps a used car in inspection plus remaining and sometimes extended coverage. You pay a premium over private sale. That premium is rational if you cannot afford downtime. It is not rational if the CPO price is within a few thousand of new after EVAP — then run the numbers again.

Extended warranties sold in F&I offices are a separate, often poor, product. Read exclusions. A prepaid maintenance plan is not the same as bumper-to-bumper. Third-party 2026 explainers (e.g. Ridez on CPO in Canada) are useful for questions to ask, not as a blessing to buy every add-on.

Private sale: Ontario UVIP, a PPI, and a history report are the adult stack. Skipping all three to “save $800” is how people fund a transmission.

Fuel and possible EV incentives if considering new EV

Ratehub’s gasoline line is $165/month in the base 2026 table (RAV4, 20,000 km, ~$1.251/L early-year national). They also flagged an April 2026 spike toward ~$1.749/L (~$231/month) during a fuel shock. Electricity for a similar-distance EV in their sketch was roughly $23–$54/month depending on provincial kWh prices. That fuel gap is real and still smaller than parking + insurance in many cities.

EVAP (new eligible only): up to $5,000 BEV in 2026 under a $50,000 FTV rule, with Canadian-made exceptions. Stack provincial programs where they exist. A used EV gets none of that federal cheque. Do not buy new just for $5,000 if the used equivalent is $18,000 cheaper after tax.

If five-year total cash (payment + insurance + fuel + maintenance + parking − resale) is still thousands apart, pick used unless you have a warranty-critical job. If a new EV after EVAP undercuts the used gas compact on five-year energy + incentives, it can flip — only with documented eligibility.

When new still wins (reliability need, long hold, incentives)

  • You keep cars 10+ years and will outlast the depreciation cliff.
  • There is no second car and a week in the shop costs shifts or childcare.
  • A written EVAP + provincial stack plus a fair FTV beats the used market for that body style.
  • Accessibility, towing, or climate packages you cannot find used without paying new money anyway.

New does not win because a salesperson said residual value, because a YouTuber leased one in Arizona, or because the used lot felt sketchy — that last one is a CPO/PPI problem. If you do not need a household car most days, price car-share before either sticker.

Frequently asked questions

Is a used car cheaper to own than a new car in Canada?

Usually, on a nine-year cash basis. Ratehub’s 2026 national model puts used ownership around $1,343/month and $120,420 over nine years, versus $1,504/month and $152,292 for new (AutoTrader Q3 2025 average stickers of $36,911 used and $63,264 new). Your insurance, repairs, and parking will move the gap. A lemon used car can lose.

Does a new car cost more to insure?

Often yes: higher replacement value, more ADAS sensors to repair, and theft lists that punish some new models. Quote both VINs with identical deductibles before you decide. Saving Optimizer is not a broker — see the shopping guide.

When does buying new still win?

When you will keep the car a long time, you need warranty uptime (shift work, no second car), a documented EVAP + provincial stack makes the after-incentive price competitive, or the used market for that model is still near-new money. Reliability need is a cost, not a vibe.

Is certified pre-owned worth it in Canada?

CPO is risk transfer: you pay more than a private sale for inspection, remaining factory warranty, and sometimes extra coverage. It can beat both a raw private car you cannot inspect and a new car’s depreciation. Price the CPO premium against a pre-purchase inspection (PPI) on a similar private car.

Do used cars qualify for EVAP?

No. EVAP is for eligible new purchases and leases. Some provinces run separate used-EV rebates (for example Manitoba’s pre-owned tier). Confirm the provincial page. Do not tell a private seller you will ‘get the federal rebate’ on their five-year-old hatchback.
