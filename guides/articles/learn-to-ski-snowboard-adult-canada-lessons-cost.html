Learning to Ski or Snowboard as an Adult in Canada: Lesson Packages and What They Include

Start with a package that bundles the lesson, rentals and a beginner ticket · About 10 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 1 Oct 2026

The cheapest way for an adult to learn to ski or snowboard in Canada is usually a beginner package that bundles a group lesson, rental equipment and a beginner-area lift ticket, followed by a multi-week program or a few more lessons once you can stop and turn. Horseshoe Resort near Barrie, for example, says its Discovery Ski Package, offered on weekends, includes a group lesson, rentals and a beginner lift ticket, while private lessons are sold with rentals and lift tickets separately. Rent for your whole first season, avoid holiday weeks when tickets cost more, and buy only clothing you will use anyway. Plan a first season of several days rather than a single outing, and adjust as you go.

Beginner packages: lift, rental and lesson bundles

Most hills sell a first-timer package. It removes three separate purchases and points you to the right terrain. Horseshoe Resort's first-time skier page says its Discovery Ski Package, available on weekends, includes a group lesson, rentals and a beginner lift ticket. Its snow school describes Discover lessons as including what you need for a first experience on the slopes. Other hills have their own names and contents, so read what is included.

Group vs private lessons

Group lessons are cheaper per hour and can be fun, especially with friends. Private lessons give one instructor's full attention, move faster, and suit adults who are nervous or want to fix a specific problem. Horseshoe's first-time page says private lessons are booked at Guest Services, with rentals and lift tickets sold separately, and points beginners who want a group lesson to the Discovery package. A common approach is one or two group lessons, then a single private lesson to fix whatever is holding you back.

Learn-to-ski programs and multi-week series

Multi-week programs meet once a week for several weeks with the same group and instructor, often on a weeknight or weekend morning. Horseshoe's snow school says its multi-week programs offer consistent weekly instruction for different ages and ability levels. Weekly practice helps adults keep what they learn, and the per-session cost is often lower than single lessons. Register early, since spots can fill before the season starts.

Renting before buying gear

Rent everything for your first season. Rental equipment for beginners is designed to be forgiving, and you will not know what you like until you have skied a few days. Once you are sure, buy boots first. The guide to buying, leasing or renting ski gear has a break-even worksheet. Clothing is the one category worth buying early: waterproof outerwear, gloves, goggles and a base layer, ideally second-hand or on sale.

Beginner terrain passes

Beginners spend their first days on gentle slopes served by a carpet lift or a slow chair. Some hills sell a cheaper ticket limited to the beginner area. A beginner ticket is worth buying for the first two or three days; switch to a full ticket when you can link turns on easy runs and want to try more of the hill.

  • Ask at Guest Services which hills are best for beginners.
  • Use the beginner area until you can stop and turn confidently.
  • Ski early in the day when snow is in better shape and slopes are quieter.
  • Take breaks; tired legs cause most beginner falls.

Skiing or snowboarding?

Adults often ask which is easier or cheaper to learn. The costs are similar: both need a lesson, rentals and a ticket, and rental packages at hills such as Horseshoe cover either. The real difference is how the first days feel, which varies from person to person. If friends or family already do one, learning the same sport means you can ski or ride together sooner, which is worth more than any small price difference. If you are unsure, try one with a beginner package and the other later in the season.

Safety and comfort

Wear a helmet; rental packages often include one. Dress in layers you can remove, keep a dry pair of gloves in the car, and drink water during the day. Read the hill's safety and risk awareness information before your first visit, and follow the instructor's advice on which runs to try. A tired beginner is more likely to fall, so finishing early on a good run is better than one more run on tired legs.

When to go

Holiday periods are busier and usually cost more. Horseshoe's lift ticket page lists the Winter Holiday Break (18 December to 3 January), Family Day weekend (12 to 15 February) and March Break (12 to 21 March) as holiday periods. Midweek days outside those periods are usually quieter, which also makes learning easier.

A first-season budget

Plan the season, not just the first day. A realistic first season for an adult might be a beginner package, two or three more days with rentals and a beginner or full ticket, and one more lesson.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost for an adult to learn to ski in Canada?

It depends on the hill and how many days you ski. A beginner package that bundles a lesson, rentals and a beginner lift ticket is usually the cheapest start. In a made-up example, a six-day first season cost $670.

What is included in a beginner ski package?

It varies by hill. Horseshoe Resort says its weekend Discovery Ski Package includes a group lesson, rentals and a beginner lift ticket.

Are private ski lessons worth it for adults?

They cost more but move faster, and suit nervous beginners or anyone stuck on a specific problem. Many adults take a group lesson first, then one private lesson.

When is the cheapest time to learn to ski?

Midweek days outside holiday periods are usually quieter. Horseshoe lists 18 December to 3 January, 12 to 15 February and 12 to 21 March as holiday periods.

Should I buy ski gear before my first lesson?

No. Rent for your first season and buy only clothing you will use anyway. Buy boots first once you know you will keep skiing.
