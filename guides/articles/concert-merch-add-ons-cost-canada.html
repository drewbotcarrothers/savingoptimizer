Concert Merch and Add-Ons: A Canadian Cost Guide

The ticket is the start of the bill · About 6 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 24 Nov 2026

Concert add-ons, such as merch, parking, drinks and VIP upgrades, can cost almost as much as the ticket. Set one budget for the whole night before you buy the ticket, decide on merch ahead of time, and check whether the artist's online store sells the same shirt after the show. Transit and eating before the show are the easiest savings.

Key takeaways

  • Budget for the whole night: ticket and fees, transit or parking, food, drinks and merch.
  • Check the all-in ticket price, including fees, first.
  • Many artists sell the same merch in their online store. Buying later avoids lineups and impulse buys.
  • VIP packages are worth it only if you value the specific perks, such as early entry or a meet-and-greet.
  • Example: a $95 ticket night costs $205 with parking, two drinks, a shirt and food, or $159 with a plan.

Merch

Venue merch tables often sell exclusive tour items, and lineups can be long. Look at the artist's online store before the show to see prices and what's only sold on tour. Set a number, such as one item, and pay with a card you track. Avoid unofficial sellers outside the venue.

VIP upgrades

VIP packages bundle early entry, merch, lounges or meet-and-greets. Price out the bundled items separately; if you wouldn't buy most of them, skip the package. Watch for resale VIP listings; our resale ticket guide covers scams, and dynamic pricing explains price changes.

Plan: an example concert night

These are example amounts, not quotes. Ticket with fees: $95. Add-ons: parking $25, two drinks $24, a shirt $45 and food $16.

Add-ons: $25 + $24 + $45 + $16 = $110. Total: $205. With transit ($7), eating at home first, one drink ($12) and the shirt bought online later at the same price, add-ons fall to $64 and the total to $159, a $46 saving.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I budget for a concert besides the ticket?

Add transit or parking, food, drinks and any merch. In our example, add-ons were $110 on top of a $95 ticket.

Is concert merch cheaper online?

Not always cheaper, but many artists sell the same items online, which avoids lineups and impulse buys. Tour-only items may only be sold at the venue.

Are VIP concert packages worth it?

Only if you value the specific perks. Price each item separately and compare.

How can I save on concert parking?

Take transit, carpool or cycle. Parking near major venues can cost more than a transit fare for two.

Should I buy merch from sellers outside the venue?

Avoid unofficial sellers. Quality varies and the artist gets nothing.
