Lunar New Year 2027 Budget in Canada: Red Envelopes, Food and Visits

Set the red envelope list first, then size dinner and visits around what is left · About 9 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 1 Oct 2026

Lunar New Year 2027 falls on Saturday 6 February 2027, the start of the Year of the Goat, according to the Royal Observatory at Royal Museums Greenwich. Build the budget in this order: write your red envelope list by relationship and pick one amount per tier, then size the reunion dinner, visiting gifts and decorations around what is left. Red envelopes are usually the largest and least flexible line, so setting them first keeps the rest honest. Order new bills from your bank branch a few weeks ahead, and look for free public celebrations instead of paid outings.

Key takeaways

  • Lunar New Year 2027: Saturday 6 February, the Year of the Goat.
  • List red envelope recipients by tier and give the same amount within a tier.
  • Ask your bank branch about new bills well before the week of the holiday.
  • Reunion dinner at home usually costs far less per person than a set menu out.
  • Reuse decorations; buy perishable flowers and fruit last.
  • Example with made-up inputs: a $1,280 plan trimmed to $860 without dropping anyone.

The date and the season

The Royal Observatory notes that the date of Lunar New Year differs every year and gives Saturday 6 February 2027 for the Year of the Goat. Because it falls on a Saturday, many families will hold reunion dinner on the Friday evening and do visits over the weekend. Celebrations often continue for days afterwards, so budget for the whole period, not just one meal. If you plan to travel to see family, check your work schedule early and book before fares rise.

Red envelopes: set tiers first

Red envelopes, known by names such as lai see and hongbao, are given by many families, often to children and younger relatives. Customs and amounts vary between families and communities, so this guide does not prescribe numbers. What saves money is consistency: decide tiers and amounts once, write them down, and stick to them.

Keep a few spare envelopes for surprise visits. Many families prefer new, crisp bills; ask your bank branch what it can supply and when, because branches can run short close to the holiday. If you give by e-transfer to relatives far away, decide that in advance too, and include it in the total.

Reunion dinner

The reunion dinner is the main meal of the season. Hosting at home usually costs less per person than a restaurant set menu, but it takes time and a long shopping list. Shop in two trips: dry goods, frozen items and drinks a week or two ahead, and fresh fish, greens and fruit in the last two days. Ask relatives to bring a dish each to share costs.

For food safety with large meals, Health Canada advises keeping food out of the danger zone between 4°C and 60°C, refrigerating leftovers within two hours, eating them within two to three days and reheating to 74°C.

Visiting gifts, decorations and clothes

Visiting families often bring a gift such as fruit, sweets or a snack box. Decide on one standard gift and buy them together; buying ten of the same box is easier to price-compare than ten different gifts. Decorations such as banners, lanterns and couplets can be reused if you take them down carefully, so store them flat. Fresh flowers and plants are perishable; buy them last and only what you will display. New clothes are traditional for some families; set a cap per person or choose one new item rather than a full outfit.

Free public celebrations

Many cities hold public celebrations that cost nothing to attend. The City of Vancouver promoted the 52nd annual Chinatown Spring Festival Parade on Sunday 22 February 2026, starting at 11 am at the Millennium Gate and lasting about three hours, and encouraged people to walk, bike or take transit. Watch your city's events page and community associations for 2027 dates; organizers announce them closer to the season.

Example with made-up inputs: one household's plan

These numbers are an example with made-up inputs. They are not suggested red envelope amounts. A household first sketches $1,280: $600 in red envelopes, a $320 restaurant dinner for eight, $120 in visiting gifts, $60 in decorations, $80 in new clothes and $100 for a paid show. They keep the red envelope list exactly as planned at $600. They host dinner at home with relatives bringing dishes, for $140. They buy one standard fruit box for each visit, for $70, reuse last year's decorations and buy only fresh flowers for $20, limit new clothes to the children for $30, and go to a free parade instead of the show. The final plan is $860, a saving of $420, and nobody is dropped from the list.

Notice where the saving came from: dinner and the outing account for $280 of the $420. Those are the lines with the most choice. Red envelopes stayed fixed because they matter most to the people receiving them, and cutting them would have saved money at the cost of the point of the holiday.

Keeping track

Use one note or spreadsheet for the season, with a column for planned and actual spending on each line. Put the red envelope list in the same file, with names, tiers and whether each envelope has been given. After the holiday, write down what you actually spent and what you would change; next year's budget then starts from real numbers instead of memory.

Steps

  1. Mark Saturday 6 February 2027 and decide which days you will host or visit.
  2. Write the red envelope list by tier with one amount per tier.
  3. Ask your bank branch about new bills a few weeks ahead.
  4. Choose home or restaurant for reunion dinner and book or shop early.
  5. Pick one standard visiting gift and buy in one trip.
  6. Reuse decorations and check your city's free events.

Frequently asked questions

When is Lunar New Year 2027?

How do I budget for red envelopes?
