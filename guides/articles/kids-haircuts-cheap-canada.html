Kids' Haircuts: Cheap Salons, Home Clippers and Making It Painless

Menu price, then the kit · About 12 minutes · By Andrew Carrothers · Updated 29 Sep 2026

Kids’ haircuts in Canada cost roughly $15 to $40 on the three published menus here: Sprout Style in Edmonton lists a child’s cut at $23, Melonhead lists The Classic at $35, and Rudy’s in Brampton starts boys at $25. A kids’ haircut is a short bill that repeats. The saving is the line on a menu that matches the hair you actually want, minus the add-ons you did not mean to buy, or a home kit you will use for the visits you skip. This page prices three published menus, then runs clipper payback on made-up inputs. Adult cuts, including the tip inside an annual total, stay on the haircut costs guide. Student clinics stay on the beauty school guide. Nothing here ranks a salon, and nothing here is medical advice.

  • Melonhead, with Bramalea, Oakville, and Toronto locations, lists The Classic at $35, My First Haircut at $35, The Bang Trim at $18, and a Sensory-Friendly Visit at $40. The same page says prices may vary by location.

Kids' salon pricing

Read the age band and the tool, then the add-ons. At Sprout Style the child’s cut (4 to 12) is $23 and the clipper-only buzz is $15.

Bangs are $10. A first-haircut package at $22 includes a certificate and a lock of hair. Mom’s haircut only is $35 and up, and dad’s haircut is $25, so a parent in the same chair is a second line, not a free ride. Shampoo is $5 and up. A clipper design is $3 and up. Those add-ons are how a $23 cut leaves the shop as something else. The menu does not say whether tax is in the figure.

Melonhead’s page says prices may vary by location and that extra time or charges may apply for length, density, texture, and styling. The Classic, described as a maintenance cut on standard hair with a style-out, is $35. The Transformation, for major changes, thick hair, or precision fades, is $40. The Curly Cut is $65. The Bang Trim is $18. The Doodle Design add-on is $8, and The Tangle Tamer is $15. Toronto booking on that page is by phone. Bramalea and Oakville use the online window. Rudy’s in Brampton uses “from” prices that step up by age: boys 6 to 11 from $35 and 12 to 17 from $40, girls 6 to 11 from $40 and 12 to 17 from $55. A “from” price is the floor on that page. Ask what the visit will be before you sit down. None of these three is a ranking. They are the menus that printed numbers.

Home clipper cost payback

Payback is the kit divided by the salon visits you will skip, after you have decided the style is one you can keep even. The home clipper guide is that method for an adult cut, including blade replacement.

For a child, use the kids’ line you would have booked, not an adult barber price you do not pay. Example with made-up inputs, not Sprout, Melonhead, or Rudy’s: a visit of $28, two children, six visits each if you stay at the salon. That is 12 times $28, or $336. A kit of $55 plus two salon visits at $28 is $111 in the first year. The gap, $225, exists only inside this example. If you still book six visits because the home cuts look wrong, the kit is an extra $55, not a saving.

Year two drops the kit if it still runs. In the same example the salon year is still $336 and the hybrid year is two visits, $56, until a blade or a new kit shows up. Write your own kit price in the blank column after you have checked the retailer. A price tracker for a household order is the Amazon.ca price guide. Before a used kit, search the brand and model on the recalls and safety alerts page at recalls-rappels.canada.ca. That page is a search. This guide does not cite a clipper recall. Clothing for the same children is a separate bill, on the kids’ clothing guide.

Technique basics

Start with a longer guard than the length you think you want. Take one section, then look, then decide whether a shorter guard is still the plan.

A patch that looks short is easier to blend from a longer guard than to grow back before school. Work while the child is willing, and stop when that ends. A half-finished cut can wait until the next calm window. A towel on the shoulders and a clipper that is oiled the way its booklet says will do more than a speech about sitting still.

The back of the neck is the part you cannot see well if you are the only adult. A second person, or a second mirror, is the tool. Do not turn a home cut into a fade, a hard part, or a design unless you have already done that shape on a patient head. Sprout lists a clipper design as an add-on from $3. That is a salon line, not a reason to experiment at the kitchen table on a morning you cannot fix. If hair is matted, Melonhead’s Tangle Tamer is a priced detangle at $15, and Sprout lists wash and detangle at $15. A home cut on hair you cannot get a comb through is how a kit gets blamed for a knot.

Discount days

A discount counts when the menu or the booking page prints it. The Sprout, Melonhead, and Rudy’s pages checked 29 Sep 2026 did not print a weekday percent, a sibling rate, or a “kids’ day.” Sprout’s first-haircut package at $22 is a package price with a certificate and a lock of hair.

It is not a percent off the child’s cut. Melonhead’s bang trim at $18 is a smaller service, not a coupon. If your salon prints a Tuesday rate, use that printed rate and the conditions under it. A rate you heard about and cannot find on the page is not the plan.

A student clinic is the other printed path, and it is not a discount code. The beauty school guide is how to find a public clinic and what the appointment is like. This page does not quote a student price. Expect a longer visit and a cut that is also someone’s practice. That trade is worth it when your calendar can absorb the time. It is a poor trade on a morning the child will not sit for an extra half hour.

When should you go pro?

Go pro for the visit the menu is built for, and for the cut you cannot safely finish. A first haircut with a keepsake is a named service: Sprout’s package at $22 includes a certificate and a lock of hair, and Melonhead’s My First Haircut at $35 includes a lock and a certificate.

A sensory-hard visit has a named line too. Sprout’s page says the stylists work with sensory sensitivities and with children diagnosed with autism. Melonhead lists a Sensory-Friendly Visit at $40, described as a slower visit with extra time. Use the line that matches the child. Do not assume the Classic price includes that extra time.

A chemical service, colour, or a scalp that is broken or sore is not a home clipper job. This page is not medical advice. Book a salon and tell them what you see. A fade, a curly cut priced as its own service (Melonhead’s Curly Cut is $65), or any shape you cannot hold still for belongs in the chair. The adult who wants a cut the same day should use the parent line if the menu has one, or the adult haircut guide, rather than treating a kids’ price as the household price.

Frequently asked questions

How can I save on kids' haircuts?

Start with the line on a named menu that matches the cut, not the cheapest line on the page. Sprout Style in Edmonton lists a clipper-only buzz at $15 and a child’s cut (ages 4 to 12) at $23, checked 29 Sep 2026. A weekday percent that is not printed on the menu you are booking is not a plan. Student clinics are a different appointment, described on the beauty school guide, and this page does not quote a clinic price.

Is it worth cutting kids' hair at home?

It is worth it when you will actually use the kit for the visits you skip, and when the style is one you can keep even. Example with made-up inputs: a $55 kit plus two $28 salon visits is $111 in the first year, against $336 if two children each have six $28 salon visits. That gap is arithmetic on made-up prices, not a shelf price and not Sprout’s menu. The home clipper guide is the payback method for an adult cut.

What clippers do I need?

A clipper with a set of guards, oil if the booklet calls for it, and a cape or towel you will use is enough for this page, which names no model. Search the brand and model on Health Canada’s recalls and safety alerts page before you buy a used kit. Hair clippers are in heading 85.10 of the 2026 Customs Tariff, and no 85.10 item appears in the United States Surtax Order (2026). How a specific product is classified is a question for CBSA (1-800-461-9999) or your carrier.

How do I make a haircut less stressful?

Book the service the salon already designed for a hard visit, if that line is on the menu. Sprout Style’s page, checked 29 Sep 2026, says the stylists work with sensory sensitivities and with children diagnosed with autism, and Melonhead lists a Sensory-Friendly Visit at $40 and says prices may vary by location. At home, stop when the child is done and finish on another day. A shorter guard will not fix a visit that has already ended.

How often do kids need a haircut?

This page does not set a week count: hair that covers the eyes, or a shape you no longer want, is the household test. The example with made-up inputs uses six salon visits a year only so the annual column has a number, and your count is the visits you will book. A bang trim, where the menu prints one, is a smaller bill than a full cut. Melonhead lists The Bang Trim at $18.
