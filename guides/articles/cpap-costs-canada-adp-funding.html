CPAP Costs in Canada: Machines, Supplies and Provincial Funding

Get approved before you buy, and claim the rest · About 6 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 24 Nov 2026

A CPAP machine costs about $1,300 to $1,435 at Canadian retailers, but you may not pay that. In Ontario, the Assistive Devices Program pays 75% of the approved price, or 100% if you're on Ontario Works or ODSP. Masks and tubing are on you, but private benefits often help, and CRA lets you claim a prescribed CPAP as a medical expense.

Key takeaways

  • ResMed AirSense 11 AutoSet: about $1,300 to $1,435 at Canadian retailers (October 2026).
  • Ontario ADP pays 75% of the approved price; 100% on Ontario Works, ODSP or ACSD.
  • Buy only after ADP steps are done, or you pay the full price.
  • ADP doesn't fund replacement masks, headgear or tubing.
  • A prescribed CPAP is an eligible medical expense (CRA, checked 24 Nov 2026).

Paying less for supplies

  • Ask your benefits plan how often it pays for masks and filters.
  • Compare vendor prices; markups vary.
  • Buy only from reputable sellers.
  • Keep receipts for the medical expense tax credit.
  • Outside Ontario, ask your sleep clinic about your province's program.

Example: if the ADP price were $1,400, your 25% share would be $350, and ADP would pay $1,050.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a CPAP cost in Canada?

A ResMed AirSense 11 AutoSet listed at about $1,300 to $1,435 at Canadian retailers in October 2026.

Does OHIP cover CPAP?

Ontario's Assistive Devices Program pays 75% of the approved price, or 100% on Ontario Works or ODSP.

Does ADP cover CPAP masks?

No. Replacement masks and headgear aren't funded.

Can I claim a CPAP on my taxes?

Yes. CRA lists a prescribed CPAP as an eligible medical expense.

What if I buy before ADP approves?

Ontario says you pay the full price.
