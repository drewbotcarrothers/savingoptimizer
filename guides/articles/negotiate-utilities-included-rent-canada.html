How to Negotiate Utilities-Included Rent in Canada (When Listings Are Bare)

If it isn't in the lease, it isn't included · About 6 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 24 Nov 2026

You can ask for utilities to be included even when a listing doesn't mention it. Landlords are most open when a unit has been empty for a while. Offer a small rent increase in exchange for heat or hydro, or ask for a monthly cap. Whatever you agree, get it written into the lease.

Key takeaways

  • Best time to ask: when a unit has sat empty or in slower months.
  • Offer slightly higher rent in exchange for heat or hydro included.
  • Put inclusions and any caps in the written lease.
  • Ontario rent increase guideline: 1.9% for 2027; BC limit: 2.2% (checked 24 Nov 2026).
  • BC: included utilities can't push rent above the limit, and fees can only rise with your agreement.

Offer structures

Ways to structure a utilities deal. As of 24 Nov 2026.
Offer Good for Watch out for
All utilities included Predictable budgets Higher rent; check what 'all' means
Heat included only Older buildings with high heat bills Hydro still yours
Monthly cap Shared houses Who reads the bill and how overages are split
Seasonal flat rate Winter-heavy bills Must be in writing

Get it in the lease

  • List each utility included by name.
  • Write any cap as a dollar amount.
  • Say who holds the account.
  • Ask for copies of past bills.
  • Never rely on a verbal promise.

Walk-away math: an example

Example: the landlord offers heat included for $60 more a month. If heat averages $110 a month, you save $50 a month, or $600 a year. If heat averages $40, say no.

Frequently asked questions

Can I negotiate utilities included in rent?

Yes, especially when a unit has been listed for a while.

How much can my landlord raise rent in 2027?

Ontario's guideline is 1.9% and BC's limit is 2.2% for most units.

Can a BC landlord raise included utilities?

Not without your agreement, and not above the rent increase limit.

Should a utilities deal be in writing?

Yes. Put it in the lease.

Is utilities-included rent a good deal?

Compare the extra rent with your likely bills.
