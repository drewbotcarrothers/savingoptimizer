Gaming Consoles in Canada: New, Used or Holiday Bundle?

Price the console, the games and the subscription together · About 10 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 1 Oct 2026

In Canada, the cheapest way into a gaming console is the option with the lowest total cost over the time you will own it, not the lowest sticker. Price three things together: the console, the games you would buy anyway, and the online subscription many games need. A holiday bundle is only a saving if the included game is one you would have bought. A used console can cost less up front but usually comes without a store return window or a manufacturer warranty in your name. A new console from a Canadian retailer carries a return window, a manufacturer warranty and, in Quebec, a legal warranty of good working order.

Key takeaways

  • Compare total cost: console, games you would buy anyway, accessories, and the online subscription for the years you expect to play.
  • A bundle saves money only if the included game or accessory is one you planned to buy. Price the console and the game separately on the same day.
  • Used consoles: test in person, check controllers, confirm the previous owner's account is removed, and do not send money before you see it work.
  • In Quebec, from 5 October 2026, the legal warranty of good working order is 3 years for new game consoles, counted from delivery.
  • Best Buy's Low Price Guarantee excludes Black Friday, Cyber Monday and Boxing Day prices. Black Friday is 27 November 2026.

Start with total cost, not the sticker

A console is a platform. The money goes out over years: games, a second controller, storage, and in many cases a paid online service. Two consoles with similar stickers can have very different totals once the subscription and the games are added.

Write down the games your household actually plays, whether they need an online subscription for multiplayer, and how many years you expect to keep the console. Then add the subscription cost for those years. Subscription prices change, so read the current price on the platform's Canadian site on the day you decide. As of 1 Oct 2026, Nintendo's Canadian site lists Nintendo Switch Online at $29.99 a year for an individual membership and $49.99 a year for a family membership of up to eight accounts, with the Expansion Pack tier at $69.99 individual and $109.99 family. Sony and Microsoft publish their own tiers; the Game Pass versus PS Plus guide covers how to compare them.

Holiday bundles: only count what you would have bought

A bundle pairs a console with a game, a controller, or a subscription trial at one price. Retailers often promote bundles around Black Friday and Boxing Day. Black Friday is Friday 27 November 2026, Cyber Monday is Monday 30 November 2026, and Boxing Day is Saturday 26 December 2026.

Price the bundle against the console alone plus the items you planned to buy, from the same store on the same day. A bundled game nobody in the house wants has a value of zero to you, even if the box prints a higher number. A subscription trial is worth only the months you would otherwise have paid for; note when it ends so it does not renew by surprise.

Check whether the included game is a disc or a download code. A disc can be resold or lent; a code is usually tied to one account. Check whether the bundle's console is a digital-only model, because that changes whether you can buy used discs later.

Price guarantees have limits during sale events. Best Buy Canada's Low Price Guarantee, as of 1 Oct 2026, excludes Black Friday, Cyber Monday and Boxing Day prices, as well as marketplace and clearance items. Read price adjustment policies before relying on a later match.

Used consoles: what to check before you pay

A used console from a private seller usually has no store return window and no warranty in your name. That risk is the price of the lower sticker, so the checks matter.

  • Meet in a public place, or at the seller's home with someone else present, and test the console with a TV or monitor before paying.
  • Play a game for several minutes. Listen for loud fans, and check that discs load if it has a drive.
  • Test every controller. Watch for drift, where a character moves when the stick is untouched.
  • Ask the seller to sign out of their account and reset the console to factory settings in front of you, so it is not linked to someone else.
  • Compare the serial number on the console with the box or receipt if they have one.
  • Pay after it works. Avoid sending an e-transfer or a deposit before you see the console. The trade-in guide covers payment scams.

Refurbished consoles from a retailer or the manufacturer sit between new and used. They usually carry a return window and some warranty, but the terms vary. The refurbished guide and the open-box guide explain what to read.

New consoles: the return window and the warranty

A new console from a Canadian retailer comes with a return window set by that retailer and a manufacturer warranty. As of 1 Oct 2026, Best Buy Canada says most products can be returned within 30 days of purchase or delivery, and Amazon.ca says most items can be returned within 30 days of delivery. Read the policy for the store and item before you buy, because exceptions apply.

In Quebec, the Office de la protection du consommateur says that from 5 October 2026 a legal warranty of good working order applies automatically and free of charge to listed new goods. For game consoles the period is 3 years, counted from delivery. It is in addition to the manufacturer's warranty. Outside Quebec, check your province's consumer protection office and the manufacturer's warranty terms. Before you buy an extended warranty, compare it with what you already have.

Consoles bought from a US store raise a separate question. Video game consoles under tariff item 9504.50.00 are on Schedule 3 of the United States Surtax Order (2026), at 50 percent, but only when the goods originate in the United States. Most consoles are not made there. The US electronics orders guide walks through origin, GST and the warranty question.

The bundle saves $40 against new only because the household wanted that game. The used path saves more but carries the risk.

Frequently asked questions

Is a holiday console bundle a good deal in Canada?

Only if the included game, controller or subscription is something you would have bought anyway. Price the console alone plus those items at the same store on the same day, and give anything you would not use a value of zero.

Is it safe to buy a used gaming console?

It can be if you test it in person before paying. Play a game, test every controller for drift, ask the seller to sign out and factory reset it in front of you, and do not send an e-transfer or deposit first. A private sale usually has no return window or warranty in your name.

What warranty does a new game console have in Quebec?

The Office de la protection du consommateur says that from 5 October 2026 a legal warranty of good working order applies automatically and free to listed new goods, and the period for game consoles is 3 years from delivery. It is in addition to the manufacturer's warranty.

Do I need an online subscription for a console?

Many games need a paid subscription for online multiplayer, so add it to the total for the years you expect to play. Read the current price on the platform's Canadian site; for example, Nintendo lists Switch Online at $29.99 a year individual and $49.99 family as of 1 Oct 2026.

Does Best Buy price-match Black Friday console deals?

As of 1 Oct 2026, Best Buy Canada's Low Price Guarantee excludes Black Friday, Cyber Monday and Boxing Day prices, along with marketplace and clearance items. Read the current terms before you rely on a match.
