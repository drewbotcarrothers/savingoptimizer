Property tax in Canada: how to review and challenge your assessment (without expecting miracles)

Appeal the value, pay the bill on time · About 17 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 17 Oct 2026

You lower a property tax bill in Canada mainly by challenging your property’s assessed value or classification, not the tax rate: the bill is assessed value × the municipal and education rates, and council sets the rates (Ontario.ca). In Ontario that starts with a free MPAC Request for Reconsideration; in B.C. it starts with a Notice of Complaint to the Property Assessment Review Panel by January 31.

A good review is a sold-comps package and a calendar, and the tax is still due on the instalment date. Ontario’s 2026 values are still January 1, 2016 current values unless the property changed (MPAC). B.C. values each roll as of July 1 of the year before (BC Assessment). Those are different clocks. This page is a Canada-wide map, not a filing kit for every province.

Key takeaways

  • Bill ≈ assessed value × tax rates. You appeal the value or class; council sets the rates.
  • Ontario 2026: still 1 Jan 2016 values (province-wide reassessment deferred again). Residential path: free MPAC Request for Reconsideration, then the ARB within 90 days of the decision ($132.50 per roll number).
  • B.C.: value as of 1 Jul, notices mailed around 31 Dec, PARP complaint by 31 Jan, then PAAB by 30 Apr ($30 per residential folio).
  • Evidence is sold comps and fixable facts, not “my neighbour’s taxes feel lower.”
  • Example with illustrative inputs: a 10% win on $800,000 at 0.60% ≈ $480/year. Do not pay a consultant for a rounding error.

How is your property tax bill calculated from the assessment?

Your municipality multiplies the assessed value by its tax rates for your property class, including education tax in Ontario (Ontario.ca). Ontario’s 2026 residential education rate stayed at 0.153% because values still use the same valuation date as 2025 (Ontario Ministry of Finance letter to municipalities, 10 Dec 2025).

The notice shows a current-value assessment (or the provincial equivalent) and a classification (residential, farm, multi-residential). A higher notice does not always mean a higher bill if every house on the street moved together and the city lowers the rate. A flat 2016 Ontario value with a rising municipal rate is how bills climb without a new “market value.”

If you just bought, the sale price is a data point, not an automatic assessment. Ontario’s frozen 2016 valuation date means a 2025 purchase is not restated to 2025 market unless MPAC is valuing a change (new build, addition) as “this property, in its current state, as if sold on 1 Jan 2016.” That is why people appeal the wrong year.

What evidence actually wins an assessment review?

Sales of similar properties near the valuation date, plus any wrong facts on your file. In Ontario, MPAC’s AboutMyProperty shows how your file is built and which properties it treats as similar. In B.C., Assessment Search shows sales. Pull:

  • Same neighbourhood, similar lot and interior area, similar age and quality.
  • Sale dates that match the valuation date (2016 in Ontario’s frozen cycle; July 1 of the prior year in B.C.), not last week’s bidding war, unless you are arguing a change notice on current condition.
  • Adjustments you can explain: finished basement, two-car vs one-car garage, river vs arterial road.
  • Errors on the file: wrong square footage, a basement listed as finished, a demolished garage still counted.

Photos of a cracked walkway help a little. Three closer sales at a lower time-adjusted value help a lot. Classification errors (residential coded as multi-residential) can be a bigger dollar win than a 3% value argument. In B.C., a typical residential PARP hearing is scheduled for 30 minutes, with about 8 minutes for your presentation (B.C. government PARP FAQ), so bring a short, organised comps sheet.

What are the deadlines and fees to appeal in each province?

Ontario non-residential owners can choose an RfR or go straight to the ARB; residential, farm, managed forest and conservation land owners must get an RfR decision first (Tribunals Ontario). MPAC still mails notices when a property changes (618,360 for the 2026 tax year), and digital notices begin in 2026, so register for AboutMyProperty so a PDF does not sit unread. If you miss an Ontario RfR deadline for reasons outside your control, you can ask the ARB for an extension of time in the same tax year (Tribunals Ontario info sheet).

In B.C., the next roll (2027) will use market value as of July 1, 2026, and its notices are due around December 31, 2026. The PARP deadline is January 31, so plan your comps check for early January.

When is hiring an assessment consultant worth it?

When the property is unusual (waterfront, mixed use, a new custom build) or the realistic gap is thousands of dollars a year. Do the comps first. Example with illustrative inputs: your assessment is $800,000, you can defend $720,000, and the combined rate is 0.60%.

Annual gap ≈ $480. A consultant who takes a flat fee or a share of “savings” on that file can keep most of it. Read the retainer. An appeal can also go the other way: the Ontario ARB can find a value higher than the notice when MPAC gives proper notice that it is asking for an increase. That is the opposite of a miracle.

Do you still have to pay property tax while you appeal?

Yes. Appealing does not pause the tax bill. Missed instalments become penalties and, eventually, a tax-sale conversation.

If your lender collects property tax with your mortgage payment, a jump still reaches you through the next adjustment (see property tax on a mortgage). If you pay the city directly, use the instalment calendar, the same trap as first-year ownership cash. Hardship plans exist at some cities; they are not an appeal.

How do a move or renovation change your assessment?

A sale can trigger a new notice (ownership, school support). A renovation, additional dwelling unit, or demolition triggers a value change even on Ontario’s frozen cycle, because MPAC values the current property at the fixed valuation date. In B.C., the roll reflects physical condition as of October 31.

Budget the assessment conversation the same year you pull a permit. The roll follows the property, not the owner. Special assessments on a condo are a corporation invoice, not an assessor’s value; do not file the wrong fight (see condo documents).

How much can an assessment appeal realistically save?

Often nothing, sometimes a few percent. Example with illustrative inputs: a 10% win on an $800,000 assessment at 0.60% is $480 a year. Real, and not a vacation.

Classification fixes can be larger. If every owner on the street won the same cut, the city can still raise the rate next year. Do the review when the comps are honestly lower or the facts on the notice are wrong. Also check Ontario property tax relief or the B.C. Home Owner Grant, and do not treat an assessment appeal as a substitute for shopping home insurance or the mortgage renewal, which are usually bigger levers. Frequently asked questions

Can you lower your property tax bill in Canada?

Usually only by lowering the assessed value or fixing the classification the bill is built on. Property tax is assessed value × the municipal and education tax rates, and council sets the rates. In Ontario you start with a free MPAC Request for Reconsideration; in B.C. you file a Notice of Complaint by January 31. Most reviews end with no change, so check sold comps first.

Does a lower assessment automatically cut my tax bill?

Only if the assessed value used for that tax year falls and the municipality does not fully offset it with a higher rate. You generally appeal the value or classification, not City Hall’s tax rate. Example with illustrative inputs: an $800,000 assessment at a 0.60% combined rate is $4,800 a year; a 10% value win is about $480.

What is Ontario’s 2026 assessment based on?

MPAC says property assessments for the 2026 tax year continue to use fully phased-in January 1, 2016 current values, unless your property changed (for example a new structure, an addition or a change in use). The province deferred the province-wide reassessment again for 2026. AboutMyProperty is MPAC’s comparison and filing tool.

How do I appeal a property assessment in Ontario?

Residential owners first file a free Request for Reconsideration with MPAC by the deadline on the notice (March 31, 2026 for the 2026 tax year). MPAC usually answers within 180 days, or up to 60 more days. If you still disagree, you can appeal to the Assessment Review Board within 90 days of the decision’s mailing date. The ARB fee is $132.50 per roll number, $10 less if you e-file.

What are B.C.’s assessment dates?

BC Assessment values property as of July 1, reflects physical condition as of October 31, and mails notices around December 31. The deadline to file a Notice of Complaint to the Property Assessment Review Panel is January 31 (it moved to February 2 in 2026 because January 31 fell on a weekend). The next step, the Property Assessment Appeal Board, has an April 30 deadline and a $30 fee per residential folio.

Should I hire an assessment consultant?

Only when the gap versus sold comps is large enough that a realistic value cut times your tax rate is well above the fee or contingency share, and you will still help gather the evidence. On a file where the possible saving is a few hundred dollars a year, a fee can take most of it. An appeal can also end with a higher value.

Is this legal advice?

No. This is an educational process map. Use MPAC, Tribunals Ontario, BC Assessment, or your provincial assessor. Pay instalments on the due date even while you review.
