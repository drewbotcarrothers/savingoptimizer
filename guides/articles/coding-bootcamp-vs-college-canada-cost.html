Coding Bootcamp vs College Program in Canada: Cost and Credentials

Compare the whole cost, not just the sticker · About 9 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 24 Nov 2026

A full-time coding bootcamp in Canada costs roughly $9,000 to $16,500 in tuition for about two to four months, while a two-year Ontario college computer program costs about $2,762 a year in tuition at a school like Mohawk College, plus ancillary fees. The college diploma takes longer and you give up more working time, but it is usually eligible for student loans, grants and the tuition tax credit. Most private bootcamps are not designated for federal student aid, so check before you plan on borrowing.

Key takeaways

  • Bootcamp tuition: about $9,000 to $16,500 for full-time programs, per a Canadian price survey in October 2026. Confirm with each school.
  • College tuition: Mohawk College lists $2,762.24 for two semesters in most programs for 2026-27, plus about $1,018 in ancillary fees.
  • Student aid: college programs at public colleges are usually designated. Most bootcamps are not, so check your province's list of approved schools.
  • Tax credit: bootcamp fees qualify only if the school is a post-secondary institution or certified by ESDC for occupational skills.
  • The biggest cost is often lost income: three months of a bootcamp or two years of college without full-time pay.

Price ranges

Bootcamps price by program, not by credit. A survey of Canadian providers published by Focus Courses in October 2026 put full-time bootcamps at about $9,000 to $16,500, with live online programs starting around $7,500 and self-paced courses far lower. University continuing education bootcamps sat in a similar range. These are approximate, exclude tax, and change often, so get the exact price and refund policy from the school in writing.

A public college program costs less per year but runs longer. For 2026-27, Mohawk College lists $2,762.24 in tuition for two semesters in most programs, plus $1,018.17 in compulsory ancillary fees before student association fees. Our college vs university guide has more college examples.

Tax eligibility

The federal tuition credit covers fees paid to a post-secondary institution for post-secondary-level courses, or to an institution certified by Employment and Social Development Canada for occupational skills courses, if you are 16 or older and paid more than $100. Some bootcamps issue a T2202 because they are certified or partner with a college; others do not. Ask the school: "Will I get a T2202?" If yes, you also may be able to use the Canada training credit.

Aid eligibility

Canada Student Loans and Grants are available only at designated schools and programs. Public colleges and universities are designated; most private bootcamps are not. If you are in Ontario, OSAP's list of approved schools is the place to check. Some bootcamps offer instalment plans, income share agreements or partner loans instead. Read those contracts closely: an instalment plan is still debt, and a private loan does not have the interest-free federal terms or the Repayment Assistance Plan.

Outcomes claims

Bootcamps advertise job placement rates. Ask how the number was calculated: who was counted, over what period, and whether contract, part-time or teaching-assistant jobs count as placements. A college publishes Key Performance Indicators through the province, which use a standard method. Neither guarantees you a job, but a standard method makes comparison fairer.

Questions to ask before you pay

  • What is the total cost including tax, laptop requirements and any exam fees?
  • What is the refund policy if I withdraw in week 1, week 3 or halfway?
  • Will I receive a T2202 for tax purposes?
  • Is the program approved for OSAP or my province's student aid?
  • How exactly is the job placement rate calculated?
  • Can I try a free or low-cost intro course first?

Example: total cost including lost income

An example career changer earns $3,500 a month after tax and compares a $14,000, 12-week bootcamp with a two-year college diploma, assuming they earn about $1,400 a month after tax from part-time work during 20 months of college.

Bootcamp: $14,000 tuition plus about $10,500 of lost pay over three months, about $24,500. College: about $7,560 in tuition and ancillary fees over two years, plus about $42,000 of lost pay ($3,500 minus $1,400, for 20 months), about $49,560, offset by any grants. The bootcamp is cheaper in total here because it is short, but the college route may bring grants, co-op pay and a credential employers recognize. Your answer depends on how fast you need to earn again.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a coding bootcamp cost in Canada?

Full-time bootcamps cost roughly $9,000 to $16,500 in tuition, based on a Canadian price survey in October 2026. Online and part-time options can cost less. Confirm the exact price and refund policy with the school.

Can I get OSAP for a coding bootcamp?

Usually not. OSAP and Canada Student Loans are only available at approved schools and programs, and most private bootcamps are not approved. Check OSAP's list of approved schools for the specific program.

Can I claim bootcamp tuition on my taxes?

Only if the school is a post-secondary institution or is certified by ESDC for occupational skills courses and you paid more than $100. Ask whether you'll get a T2202.

Is a college diploma better than a bootcamp?

It depends on your goal. A diploma takes longer and costs more in lost income, but it is a recognized credential, often includes co-op and qualifies for student aid. A bootcamp is faster if you already have related skills.

What should I ask about job placement rates?

Ask who was counted, over what time period and what counts as a job. Standard college KPIs use a consistent method, which makes comparison easier.
