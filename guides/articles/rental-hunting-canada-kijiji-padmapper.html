How to hunt Canadian rentals on Kijiji, Facebook Marketplace, and PadMapper without getting scammed

Platform workflow, not Craigslist folklore · About 16 minutes · 21 Apr 2026

Canadian renters do not have a Craigslist-shaped market. They have Kijiji, Facebook Marketplace, PadMapper and cousins, building wait-lists, and a comment section that will tell you the good unit was already gone. The pain is not “I cannot find the perfect app.” It is weeks of ghost listings and deposit scams while a real two-bed disappears between 9 a.m. and lunch.

This is a platform workflow: alerts, a scam filter, a viewing script, and a tracking sheet. It is not U.S. advice and not a promise that any marketplace is clean.

Disclosure: Rental search platforms and tenant insurance comparison tools are offer types. Saving Optimizer may earn a commission if we later add partner links. We do not currently claim Kijiji, Meta, PadMapper, or insurer partnerships. This is not legal advice; if money already left, police non-emergency and a tenant clinic beat a comment thread.

Key takeaways

  • Run daily alerts on at least two platforms. Weekend binges lose to people who messaged at 8:12 a.m.
  • Never send a deposit before an in-person (or tightly verified) viewing with keys. “I’m in Dubai, my son has the keys” is not a process.
  • Too-cheap rent plus urgency plus wire/e-transfer is a skip, not a steal.
  • Expand the map only after your first-choice radius is actually quiet.
  • A tracking sheet stops you from applying twice to the same ghost and forgetting who asked for references.

Which platforms still surface real listings in major cities

As of 16 Sep 2026, a typical Toronto / Vancouver / Montréal hunt still uses:

  • Kijiji: still a workhorse for private landlords and some small landlords who never learned Marketplace. Duplicate posts and expired photos are common — sort by newest and message immediately.
  • Facebook Marketplace / groups: fast, messy, scam-heavy. Turn on alerts. Prefer sellers with a local history over brand-new profiles with stock photos.
  • PadMapper (and similar map aggregators): useful for radius and price bands; many cards are scraped from elsewhere. Click through and treat the source listing as the one you verify.
  • Building sites, realtor rental boards, and “someone in my office is leaving” — slower, often cleaner.

Craigslist is not the Canadian default. Do not import U.S. “Craigslist only” folklore. Also do not pay a “guaranteed listing” Facebook ad that wants a fee to “hold” a unit you have not seen.

Daily search workflow: alerts, saved filters, and response scripts

Saved filters: beds, max rent, pets, parking, date available, and a map that matches your commute — not a fantasy neighbourhood you cannot reach on the bus you actually take. Write one message you can paste:

Hi, I’m [name], employed [full-time / student], looking to move [date]. I can view today or tomorrow evening, and I can send a completed application (ID, proof of income, references) after the viewing. Is the unit still available, and are utilities included?

Ask one clarifying question so it does not look like a bot. Do not lead with your life story. Do not offer a deposit in the first message.

Red flags: deposits before viewing, too-good rent, overseas landlords

  • Any request for e-transfer, crypto, gift cards, or a “holding deposit” before you have walked the unit.
  • Rent hundreds below everything else on the same street with professional resort photos.
  • Landlord “travelling,” “military,” or “out of country,” keys with a “broker” you cannot meet at the door.
  • Refusal to do a video walk-through if you are relocating, then still demanding money first.
  • Documents that look like a lease but ask you to waive statutory rights you have not read — pause and use a tenant clinic, not a group chat.

Ontario households: a last-month rent deposit is a real legal category; a random “damage deposit” usually is not. Read the Ontario fee cheat sheet before you “agree to whatever” in a Marketplace DM.

How to verify ownership and viewing logistics safely

You cannot run a full title search in a DM. You can:

  • Meet at the unit. Someone who cannot produce keys and access is not showing you that unit.
  • Match the name on the listing to ID and, where it exists, to a mailbox, directory, or hydro account they are willing to show (they may decline — then look harder at other tells).
  • Bring a friend. Tell someone the address. Daytime viewings beat midnight “quick looks.”
  • For condos: guest-park or lobby sign-in should not feel like a break-in. Superintendents who have never heard of the “landlord” are a data point.

This is safety and money, not paranoia. Scammers rent Airbnb photos; they do not enjoy door buzzers.

When to expand neighbourhood radius vs wait for a deal

Expand when: three to five days of alerts produced nothing you would actually live in; your commute math still works one extra transit zone; or you are 30 days from homelessness, not 30 days from “maybe something closer.” Wait when: your current rent is lawful and tolerable, you can keep hunting mornings, and widening would add a $200/month transit or time cost that eats the “cheaper” rent. Price the commute with the same honesty as our Transportation guides — a $150 cheaper unit that needs a second PRESTO product is not cheaper.

Tracking sheet for viewings, applications, and follow-ups

Copy into a spreadsheet. Ghosts get one follow-up, then archive.
Field Why
URL + screenshot date Listings vanish; scams recycle photos.
Ask, inclusions, address Compare apples after six viewings.
Platform / poster handle Spot the same scammer on Kijiji and Marketplace.
Viewing time + who met you Keys vs “I’ll WhatsApp the lockbox.”
Application sent? Follow-up date Professionals follow up once; you should too.
Outcome Ghost / no / offer / signed.

City notes: Toronto, Vancouver, Montréal listing quirks

  • Toronto: “first and last” language is common; last-month rent is the usual lawful deposit category — extra months and damage holds are a pause. Many “units” are rooms. Ask about hydro, AC, and whether the listing is the whole apartment. Send a same-day package.
  • Vancouver: basement and “garden” suites need moisture, egress, and parking honesty. Security deposits have RTB caps (often half-month plus pet rules) — do not improvise Ontario first-and-last on a Burnaby suite. See ON vs B.C. vs Québec move-in cash.
  • Montréal: July 1 moving culture still clusters listings. Security deposits are typically not a Québec thing; TAL assignment/sublet rules matter if you might leave. French listings are not optional if you want the real inventory.

Once you have a lease, tenant insurance is a small stack line compared with losing a bike and a laptop. Shop coverage, not a brand. If the listing only works with a roommate, run the city split math before you say yes. Then keep the unit by negotiating the renewal with comps, not by refreshing Kijiji in a panic every June.

Frequently asked questions

Which site still has real Canadian apartments?

In 2026, most major-city hunts still mix Kijiji, Facebook Marketplace, PadMapper (and similar aggregators), plus building sites and word of mouth. None is scam-proof. The workflow is alerts plus a refusal to pay before a viewing.

How fast should I reply?

Same morning for anything in your price band in Toronto, Vancouver, or central Montréal. Units that are real and priced near the market often go in hours, not weekends.

What is the biggest scam tell?

A deposit or e-transfer before you have stood in the unit with someone who can produce keys and ID, especially if the “landlord” is overseas and the rent is far below nearby listings.

Should I widen the neighbourhood or wait?

Widen after a few quiet days at your first-choice map, not after one bad evening. Waiting only wins if your current rent is tolerable and your alerts are actually on.

Do you partner with Kijiji or PadMapper?

No. Rental search platforms and tenant insurance comparison tools are offer types. This hunt works on free accounts and a spreadsheet.
