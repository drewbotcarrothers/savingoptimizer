Wedding Favours: A Canadian Cost Guide

Give a small thing guests actually use, or skip it · About 5 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 24 Nov 2026

Wedding favours are optional, and many couples now skip them. If you want one, choose something guests will eat or use. A DIY favour can cost well under $1 a guest: Dollarama sells six wedding favour boxes for $2. For 120 guests, DIY treat boxes come to about $107 in this example, against $480 for pre-made favours.

Key takeaways

  • Favours are optional; skipping them is common.
  • Edible favours get taken home; trinkets often get left behind.
  • Dollarama: 6 favour boxes for $2, 40 m of tulle for $3.50 (checked 24 Nov 2026).
  • A donation in lieu of favours, or a late-night snack, are good alternatives.
  • Example: 120 guests, $107 DIY vs $480 pre-made.

Are favours worth it?

Only if they fit your budget and your guests. Favours are one of the easiest line items to cut, and guests rarely notice. If a family tradition expects them, keep them small and edible.

Alternatives

  • A card saying you donated to a charity instead.
  • A late-night snack, like poutine or pizza, served at the reception.
  • A dessert table that guests can take treats from.
  • Seeds, local honey or maple candy if you want a keepsake.

Frequently asked questions

Are wedding favours necessary?

No. They're optional, and many couples skip them.

How much should I spend on wedding favours?

As little as you like. DIY treat boxes can cost under $1 a guest.

What favours do guests actually take home?

Edible ones usually get taken; trinkets are often left behind.

Can I donate instead of giving favours?

Yes. Many couples put a small card at each place setting about the donation.

Where can I buy cheap favour boxes in Canada?

Dollarama sold 6-packs of wedding favour boxes for $2 when checked on 24 Nov 2026.
