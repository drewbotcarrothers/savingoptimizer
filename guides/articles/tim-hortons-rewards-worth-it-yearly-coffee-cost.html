Tim Hortons rewards and the yearly coffee cost

Scan the habit you already have; do not buy a new one · About 8 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 24 Nov 2026

Tims Rewards is worth scanning when you were already buying the coffee. It is not a reason to add a breakfast sandwich, make a second stop or switch from brewing at home. The right comparison is the yearly cost of your actual habit, with rewards valued at the item you will redeem.

Key takeaways

  • Standard earn is 10 points per eligible pre-tax dollar; brewed coffee and tea sit in the 400-point reward level.
  • At that rate, 400 points requires $40 of eligible spending before bonuses.
  • Use the current price at your restaurant. Prices and available items vary.
  • Unused points expire 12 months after the calendar month earned; do not bank a balance you will forget.
  • Skipping one unnecessary purchase usually saves more than optimizing the points on it.

A real local coffee price, not a national menu promise

The Drive Thru Index menu for Tim Hortons, 2575 Danforth Ave., Toronto showed a medium Original Blend at $1.83, small $1.59, large $2.14 and extra large $2.40. Its restaurant snapshot was checked 23 Nov 2026 and read for this guide on 24 Nov 2026. This is a third-party collection of the pickup menu, not an official national price. Tax and local fees are extra. Your restaurant’s app or counter is the final check.

What the standard earn rate means

One point per $0.10 of eligible pre-tax spend is 10 points per dollar. A 400-point coffee therefore takes $40 of eligible spend before bonus offers. At the $1.83 medium price, 10 points per dollar and 0.4575¢ redemption value imply about 4.575% future value. This is not cash back and it is not a flat return on every menu item.

Scan before paying or order through the app. Check the selected reward level so points are not spent on an item you meant to pay for. A promotional offer may require activation, a minimum spend, specific items or a deadline; do not count it until the conditions are met. Standard program earn and any co-branded credit-card earn are separate calculations.

Example: the yearly weekday coffee habit

Example: 260 weekday medium coffees at the observed $1.83 price cost $475.80 a year before tax, assuming the price stays unchanged. At a 400-point coffee reward and standard earn, a simplified rolling model is 249 paid coffees and 11 rewards: $455.67 paid, about $20.13 saved. It assumes 18 points for each $1.83 transaction, no starting balance, no bonus offers, no expiry and no points earned on free coffees. Actual rounding and posting should be checked in the account.

Keep the visits that are useful or enjoyable and budget for them honestly. Do not count the same points both as an annual saving and as free items on top of the year’s consumption.

Four traps to avoid

  • Buying an extra item for a bonus: compare the added cost with the value of the bonus, not the total points balance.
  • Redeeming a bigger drink only because it is “better value”: more caffeine or waste is not a financial benefit.
  • Letting points expire: check the app’s History view and use the oldest balance on planned purchases.
  • Ignoring tax and cup fees: provincial rules and transaction size matter. A reward may not cover a locally required cup fee.

Frequently asked questions

How many Tims Rewards points do I earn?

The standard program earns 10 points per eligible pre-tax dollar, or one point per $0.10. Scan the registered card/app or order through the app, and check exclusions. Credit-card earn is separate.

How many points is a brewed coffee?

The official FAQ places brewed coffee and tea in the 400-point beverage level. Confirm the selected reward level and available item in your app before redeeming.

What is one Tims point worth?

It depends on the item you redeem. At a $1.83 coffee price and 400 points, the value is 0.4575 cents per point. A different size or item can produce a different value.

Do Tims Rewards points expire?

The official FAQ says unused points expire 12 months after the calendar month in which they were earned. Check the app’s Details and History views for expiring points.

Does a free coffee cover a single-use cup fee?

Not necessarily. The official FAQ says a required local single-use cup fee and applicable tax can remain payable when the beverage is redeemed.

Is buying more coffee to earn rewards worthwhile?

Not if the extra spending is only for points. Rewards reduce the cost of an existing habit; they do not turn an unnecessary purchase into a saving.
