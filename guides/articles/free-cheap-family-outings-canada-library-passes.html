Free and Cheap Family Outings in Canada: Library Passes, Memberships and Free Days

Borrow the pass, then decide on the membership · About 8 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 1 Oct 2026

In Canada, the cheapest family outings start at the public library and with free admission programs. Many library systems lend free passes to museums and attractions, Parks Canada offers free admission for youth 17 and under at Parks Canada places, and many museums have free or pay-what-you-can times. An annual membership pays off when you would visit often enough to beat the single-visit price; divide the membership cost by the family's cost for one visit to find the break-even.

Key takeaways

  • Ask your library about attraction passes. Toronto Public Library's Museum + Arts Pass is one example; many systems have their own.
  • Parks Canada: youth 17 and under get free admission at Parks Canada places. Adults still pay.
  • Look up free or pay-what-you-can times on each museum's website.
  • New citizens: Canoo, from the Institute for Canadian Citizenship, gives free admission to participating places for a year.
  • Membership break-even = membership cost ÷ one family visit. Example with made-up inputs: $150 ÷ $60 = 2.5 visits.

Library passes

Many public library systems lend passes to museums, galleries, zoos and science centres, usually for free with a library card. Passes may be limited, reserved in advance or picked up at certain branches. Toronto Public Library's Museum + Arts Pass (MAP) is one well-known example; check your own library's website for "museum pass" or "attraction pass." Libraries also lend things like park passes, kits and games in some systems.

When a membership pays off

An annual family membership makes sense if you would visit enough times to beat the single-visit price. Divide the membership price by the family's cost for one visit; if you expect to go more often than that, the membership saves money. Some memberships include parking, discounts or reciprocal admission at other institutions; check what is included, and do not count perks you will not use.

Step by step: a year of low-cost outings

  1. Get a library card for every family member if your library allows it; some attraction passes are limited per card.
  2. Search your library's website for attraction or museum passes, note how far ahead you can reserve, and set a reminder for the release time if passes go quickly.
  3. List the museums, galleries, science centres and zoos within reach, and note any free or pay-what-you-can times.
  4. Mark Parks Canada places near you; youth 17 and under get in free all year, so only adult admission or a pass counts in your budget.
  5. Check your city's events calendar for free festivals, outdoor rinks, splash pads, trails and library programs.
  6. Pick the one place your family would visit most and run the membership break-even.
  7. Pack food and water; snacks and parking often cost more than admission.

Parks Canada passes and seasonal offers

Youth 17 and under get free admission at Parks Canada places, so for families the cost is adult admission. If you visit several times a year, compare daily admission for the adults with a Parks Canada Discovery Pass, which covers a year of admission at participating places; a family or group version covers people arriving together in one vehicle. The federal Canada Strong Pass has also offered free Parks Canada admission during set periods, most recently from 19 June to 7 September 2026. Watch for future editions, but do not plan around one.

Making a membership pay

  • Check whether the membership includes parking, guest passes or discounts on camps and the gift shop.
  • Ask about reciprocal programs: some science centre, zoo and museum memberships include free or discounted entry at other participating institutions, sometimes in other provinces.
  • Buy at the start of a season you will use, such as before summer for a zoo.
  • Some memberships go on sale or include extra months during promotions; ask.
  • Check whether a grandparent membership could cover the grandchildren.

Common mistakes

  • Buying a membership in a burst of enthusiasm and visiting once.
  • Forgetting the cost of parking, food and the gift shop, which can exceed admission.
  • Missing a library pass's pickup or return rules and losing borrowing privileges.
  • Assuming provincial parks follow Parks Canada's free youth admission; they set their own fees.

Frequently asked questions

Can I borrow museum passes from the library in Canada?

Many public library systems lend free attraction passes with a library card. Toronto Public Library's Museum + Arts Pass is one example. Check your library's website for availability and rules.

Are Parks Canada places free for kids?

Yes. Parks Canada offers free admission for youth 17 and under at Parks Canada places. Adult admission still applies, and provincial parks set their own fees.

When is a family membership worth it?

Divide the membership cost by the cost of one family visit. If you expect to visit more often than that, the membership saves money.

What is Canoo?

Canoo is a program from the Institute for Canadian Citizenship that gives new Canadian citizens free admission to participating cultural and outdoor places for a year.

How do I find free museum days?

Check each museum's website for free or pay-what-you-can times, and watch your city's event listings.

Is there a family pass for Parks Canada?

Yes. The Discovery Pass comes in a family or group version that covers admission at participating Parks Canada places for a year for people arriving together. Youth 17 and under already get in free.

What is the Canada Strong Pass?

A federal offer that has provided free Parks Canada admission during set periods, most recently from 19 June to 7 September 2026. Watch for announcements of future editions.
