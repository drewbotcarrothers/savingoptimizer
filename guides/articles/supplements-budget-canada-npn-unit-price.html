Supplements on a Budget in Canada: NPNs, Unit Prices and What to Ask Your Pharmacist First

Check the NPN, check the unit price, then ask · About 5 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 24 Nov 2026

Before you buy a supplement, ask your pharmacist or doctor whether you need it. If you do, check that it has a Natural Product Number (NPN), then compare the price per tablet, not per bottle. The same Jamieson vitamin D was 7.8 cents a tablet in a small bottle and about 3.3 cents in a big one on sale.

Key takeaways

  • Look for an eight-digit NPN or DIN-HM on the label.
  • Search it in Health Canada's Licensed Natural Health Products Database.
  • Jamieson Vitamin D3 1,000 IU: 7.8 cents a tablet (100 at Walmart) vs about 3.3 cents (240 on sale at Super C), checked 24 Nov 2026.
  • Store brands with the same dose often cost less.
  • Ask about interactions with your medications.

NPN basics

Health Canada says a product licence means it was assessed and found safe, effective and of high quality under its recommended conditions of use. It doesn't mean you need it, and it doesn't make a higher dose better.

Avoiding traps

  • Skip auto-ship subscriptions you didn't plan for.
  • Don't pay more for 'premium' if the dose and NPN match.
  • Check the expiry date on big bottles.
  • Ask if a supplement is prescribed or covered by your plan.
  • Keep it simple: one product, one reason.

Frequently asked questions

What is an NPN?

An eight-digit Natural Product Number that shows Health Canada licensed the product.

Does an NPN mean a supplement works?

Health Canada assessed it for its recommended use. Ask your pharmacist if you need it.

How do I compare supplement prices?

Divide the price by the number of tablets, at the same dose.

Are store-brand supplements as good?

If the dose matches and it has an NPN, it's licensed the same way.

Can I claim supplements on my taxes?

Over-the-counter supplements generally aren't eligible medical expenses.
