Lower your winter heating bill across provinces: gas, electric heat, and equal billing

Fuel first, then the provincial lever · About 17 minutes · 20 Sep 2026

Canadian January bills shock people who treated October as “the gas bill.” The lever that works depends on the heat source and the province’s market, not on a California thermostat thread. Equal billing can save the Visa card and hide a 40% jump in cubic metres. A heat-pump brochure can ignore a 100 A panel.

This is a cross-Canada map that sits on top of the useful-heat conversion worksheet. Renters should stay in heat included vs hydro until they know who pays. Owners picking machines should continue to heat pump vs furnace.

Key takeaways

  • Name the heat: gas furnace, electric baseboard or furnace, heat pump (with or without backup), or oil. The ¢ on the wrong fuel is fan fiction.
  • Provincial patterns differ: Enbridge Ontario is supply + delivery + carbon-related lines; FortisBC has gas and (in its electric territory) an Equal Payment Plan; Hydro-Québec Rate D second tier is 11.142 ¢ from 1 Apr 2026; Alberta shops energy and still pays delivery.
  • Equal / budget billing is cashflow. Conservation is kWh and m³. Do both or neither — do not confuse them.
  • Thermostats: modest recoverable setbacks, pipes above freezing, zoning unused rooms. Deep hero setbacks in a sieve can cost more.
  • Envelope first (air sealing, attic) before gadgets. A heat-pump conversation belongs after the house can hold heat — and after you price live provincial stacks. Greener Homes and OHPA intakes are closed.

Diagnose your heat source: gas furnace, electric baseboard, heat pump, oil

Walk to the appliance, not the group chat.

  • Gas furnace — flame, vent, filter slot, thermostat on the wall. You buy m³ (or GJ) plus delivery. Electric air handler still adds a hydro tail.
  • Electric baseboard or electric furnace — long heaters under windows, or a coil in a plenum. COP ≈ 1. Every useful kWh is a purchased kWh. See the baseboard playbook.
  • Heat pump — outdoor unit, maybe backup strips or a gas hybrid. You buy kWh divided by a real cold-weather COP, not the brochure COP at +8°C.
  • Oil — still in older Atlantic and rural houses. Price the tank and the service contract. Federal oil-to-heat-pump (OHPA) intake closed 31 Jul 2026.

If you cannot name it, you cannot pick a lever. A “hydro is high” complaint in a gas house is the furnace fan, DHW, or a dryer — or you are not in a gas house.

Equal/budget billing as cashflow vs true conservation

Enbridge EMPP, FortisBC EPP, Hydro-Québec equalized payments, and most hydro “budget” plans estimate annual use and divide it by 12 (or review quarterly). That protects a January Visa. It does not burn less gas.

If you only watch the installment, you will not notice a 3,400 kWh January or a 450 m³ February. Open the usage graph once a quarter. A true-up is not a rebate. Details and who should enrol sit in equal billing.

Thermostat strategies that respect household comfort and pipes

  • Hold a boring daytime setpoint you can live with (many households land around 18–21°C). Hero 15°C days in an occupied house are how pipes and people complain.
  • Modest setbacks of 1–3°C when asleep or out, if the house recovers in under an hour. Heat pumps and leaky plexes often prefer flatter settings.
  • Zone unused rooms — close the door, drop that stat. Do not freeze a plumbing wall.
  • Pre-condition on a clock plan — on Ontario ULO, move recovery before 4 p.m. On BC Hydro time-of-day, avoid a 4–9 p.m. blast.
  • A smart thermostat is a schedule you will actually keep. A cheap accurate dial beats an unprogrammed “smart” brick. Line-voltage units on baseboards; low-voltage on furnaces and most heat pumps.

Envelope first: air sealing before buying gadgets

Heated air that leaves is the bill. Weatherstripping, attic hatch, outlet gaskets, the dryer flap, and then attic R-value toward the NRCan zone target beat a $300 gadget. Walk through attic insulation and, for a large contract, EnerGuide. Tenants: only what you can undo.

Worksheet, 20 minutes: last January’s m³ or kWh; this January’s; whether the winter felt colder (or look up nearby degree-days if you enjoy homework); one envelope job you will actually do this month. If usage jumped in a mild month, look at the machine — Enbridge spike signs.

When a heat-pump conversation beats another winter of spikes

Have the conversation when the furnace is near end of life, the panel can take a compressor (or you can fund a service upgrade), the envelope is no longer a barn, and you have live provincial or utility stacks — not a 2023 Greener Homes blog. Federal Grant/Loan and OHPA intakes are closed. Québec Rate D often favours a real heat pump; an Ontario leaky house on resistance backup often does not.

A $12,000 increment that saves $600/year is 20 years of simple payback before comfort and cooling. That can still be the right house. It is not a slogan. Walk the COP and hybrid questions in the companion guide, then come back to this winter’s filter and door snakes.

Frequently asked questions

Is equal billing a discount on winter heat?

No. It spreads estimated annual cost into installments. You still use the same GJ or kWh. A true-up is a credit or a balance owing — the bill arriving later.

Should I turn the heat down 3°C at night everywhere in Canada?

A modest 1–3°C setback is the usual comfort/pipe-safe range if the house can recover without a two-hour blast. Deep setbacks in a leaky plex or a heat-pump house can cost more in recovery. Keep rooms with pipes above freezing.

Does a heat pump always beat another winter of gas?

No. It depends on your all-in ¢/kWh, cold-weather COP, backup fuel, panel, and capital. Federal Greener Homes and OHPA intakes are closed. See the heat-pump vs furnace companion.

I rent. Which lever is mine?

Behaviour, draft stopping you can undo, and the lease comparison. You do not buy the furnace. Start with heat-included vs hydro.

Where do I see last winter versus this winter?

The usage graph in the utility portal — m³ or kWh, not the equalized debit. Compare January to January, then glance at whether the winter was colder.
