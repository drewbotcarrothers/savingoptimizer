Competitive Dance and Gymnastics in Canada: The Full Season Cost

Tuition is only the start; solos and travel drive the bill · About 6 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 24 Nov 2026

A competitive dance or gymnastics season costs far more than tuition. One BC studio's sample budgets put competition entries and costumes alone at $1,765 to $3,715 a year, before tuition, choreography and travel. Solos are the biggest driver. Ask for the full season budget in writing before your child joins a team.

Key takeaways

  • Group competition fees: $40 to $90 per dancer, per dance, per competition (one studio, checked 24 Nov 2026).
  • Solo competition entries: $75 to $130 each.
  • New costumes from about $100; buy used when allowed.
  • Sample budgets: $1,765 to $3,715 a year before tuition.
  • Ask for the full season budget in writing.

Ways to cut the cost

  • Limit solos; group dances cost far less.
  • Buy or rent used costumes.
  • Choose competitions close to home.
  • Share hotel rooms and carpool.
  • Apply for Jumpstart or KidSport help, and check provincial activity credits.

A full season: an example

Example: tuition and extra training at an example $3,000, plus the studio's lower sample budget of $1,765, plus an example $300 for travel, comes to about $5,065. Gymnastics clubs often have the same structure: training hours, meet fees, a club leotard and travel.

Frequently asked questions

How much does competitive dance cost in Canada?

One BC studio's sample budgets put entries and costumes at $1,765 to $3,715 a year, before tuition and travel.

What costs the most in competitive dance?

Solos, because each has its own entry fees, costume and choreography.

Is competitive gymnastics cheaper?

It depends on training hours and meets, but the cost structure is similar.

Can I get help paying?

Jumpstart and KidSport offer grants, and some provinces have activity tax credits.

When should we step back?

If the cost strains your budget or your child isn't enjoying it, a recreational program can be a good fit.
