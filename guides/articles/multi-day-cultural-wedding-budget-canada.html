Multi-Day Cultural Weddings in Canada: Budgeting Several Events, Splitting Costs and Managing Vendors

Budget each event as its own small wedding, then add them up · About 12 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 1 Oct 2026

To budget a wedding with several events over multiple days, treat each event as its own small wedding: list its venue, food, decor, music, attire, transport and guest count, price it with tax and service charges, then add the events together. Decide early which family pays for which event, and put it in writing. Rules and fees can repeat per event: in Ontario, a private special occasion permit costs $50 per day without sales or $150 per day with sales, and recorded music at each reception may carry a Re:Sound licence fee of $9.25 to $78.66, usually through the venue. Booking the same vendors across several days can save money and coordination time; ask for a multi-event package.

Key takeaways

  • Budget each event separately, then total.
  • Agree in writing which family pays for which event.
  • Ontario permits are per day: $50 no-sale, $150 sale.
  • Music licensing and service charges can apply at each event.
  • Ask vendors for multi-event packages.
  • Example with made-up inputs: a four-event budget.

List every event

What to price for each event. Your events and their order depend on your traditions. As of 1 Oct 2026.

Religious or civil ceremony Venue or place of worship fees, officiant, decor, musicians, guest count

Guest counts can differ a lot by event. Smaller family events at home or in a rented hall may cost less per person than the main reception, so getting the guest list for each event right is the biggest lever on total cost.

Splitting between families

Multi-day weddings may involve both families hosting different events. There is no rule on who pays for what. Discuss it early, agree on a budget for each event, and decide who signs each vendor contract, because the person who signs is responsible for paying. Keep a shared spreadsheet with each event, its host and its payments. If one family is contributing money to the couple rather than hosting, record the amount and timing.

Vendors across days

  • Ask photographers, videographers, decorators, caterers and DJs for a multi-event package with a single contract.
  • Confirm hours per day, travel between venues, overtime rates and meals for vendor staff.
  • Check that each venue's insurance and vendor rules allow your chosen vendors.
  • Leave set-up and tear-down time between events; overlapping bookings can trigger extra fees.
  • Get one master timeline covering all days, shared with every vendor.

Food, alcohol and permits

Menus often change from event to event. Health Canada lists the most common food allergens as peanuts, tree nuts, sesame, milk, eggs, fish, crustaceans and molluscs, soy, wheat and triticale, sulphites and mustard; ask caterers how they label and separate dishes. Under CRA rules, a mandatory service charge is part of the taxable price, so in Ontario 13% HST applies to it. If you serve alcohol at an event outside a licensed venue, helloLCBO says a private-event special occasion permit costs $50 per day for no-sale events and $150 per day for sale events, and applications for private events are due at least 10 days before. The AGCO says no permit is needed to serve invited guests in your own home, but liquor cannot be sold at a residence.

Music and noise

Re:Sound's Tariff 5.B sets fees for recorded music at receptions, from $9.25 to $78.66 per event depending on room capacity and whether there is dancing; venues normally report and pay it, and SOCAN has a separate tariff. For outdoor events in Toronto, the noise bylaw limits amplified sound heard in a neighbour's outdoor living area to 55 dB(A) from 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. and 50 dB(A) from 11 p.m. to 7 a.m., unless you hold a noise exemption permit. Check your own municipality's bylaw.

Attire for each event

Several events can mean several outfits for the couple and sometimes the wedding party and close family. Budget each outfit with alterations and tax, consider renting or borrowing for some events, and set dates for final fittings well before the first event. See wedding dress costs and suit or tux, rent or buy.

Guest counts

Send separate invitations or clear event cards so guests know which events they are invited to, and collect RSVPs per event. Caterers and venues price per head, so a 20-guest difference at one event matters. Out-of-town guests attending several days may need accommodation guidance and transport between venues.

Cash flow across events

With several events, deposits and balances come due at different times, sometimes months apart. Build one payment calendar across all events and hosts, so nobody is surprised by a large balance a week before the first event. Where vendors offer a multi-event package, ask for a payment schedule that matches the families' cash flow, and make sure cancellation terms are clear for each day separately. If one event is cancelled or moved, you want to know what is refundable without affecting the others. Paying for a wedding without borrowing is easier when every payment is visible; see paying for a wedding without debt.

Plan

  1. List events, hosts and guest counts.
  2. Set a budget per event, including tax, service charges and tips.
  3. Book multi-event vendor packages.
  4. Check permits, music licensing and noise rules for each venue.
  5. Plan attire and fittings.
  6. Build one master timeline and payment schedule.

Example with made-up inputs

These numbers are an example with made-up inputs, apart from published permit and music fees. Event 1, a 60-guest family evening at home with catered food: $3,200, no permit needed and no sales. Event 2, an 80-guest daytime gathering in a rented hall serving free drinks: $4,800 plus a $50 no-sale permit. Event 3, the ceremony for 200: $6,500. Event 4, the 250-guest reception at a banquet hall: $32,000 including service charges and HST. A multi-event photography and video package costs $7,500 instead of a made-up $9,800 booked separately. Total: $54,050.

Common mistakes

  • Budgeting only the main reception.
  • Not agreeing who pays for each event.
  • Missing a permit for an event with alcohol outside a licensed venue.
  • Booking vendors separately for each day without asking for a package.
  • Unclear invitations leading to surprise guests.

Frequently asked questions

How do you budget a multi-day wedding?

Budget each event separately with its own guest count, venue, food, music and attire, then add them up.

Do I need a separate special occasion permit for each event in Ontario?

Permit fees are per day: $50 for no-sale and $150 for sale private events, if the event needs a permit.

Who pays for each event in a multi-day wedding?

There is no rule. Agree early and in writing, and remember the person who signs a contract is responsible for paying it.

Do music licensing fees apply at each reception?

Re:Sound's Tariff 5.B fees apply per reception with recorded music, usually paid by the venue.

How can we save on vendors across several days?

Ask for multi-event packages with one contract, and leave time between events to avoid overtime.

Is HST charged on service charges?

Under CRA rules a mandatory service charge is taxable, so 13% HST applies in Ontario.
