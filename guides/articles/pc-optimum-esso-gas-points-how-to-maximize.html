How to maximize PC Optimum points at Esso and Mobil without chasing bad gas prices

Esso Extra is gone — price vs points · About 17 minutes · 20 Sep 2026

Canadians will drive an extra six minutes for “points” and then pay 7¢ more per litre than the independent they passed. That is not maximizing PC Optimum. That is buying a more expensive fill and getting a sticker. Esso Extra is gone. Participating Esso and Mobil stations pay PC Optimum. The earn is real. The posted price is still the adult variable.

This is a 2026 Canada price-versus-points worksheet — litres, not gallons, and no Costco membership detour. Pair it with the three-brand comparison in PC Optimum vs Petro-Points vs Scene+ and the grocery stack in how to maximize PC Optimum. If you already shop Loblaw banners, Esso is a second till. If you do not, it is just a pump.

Disclosure: PC Optimum co-branded credit cards (PC Financial Mastercard, PC Insiders World Elite) and other gas-rewards cards are offer types. Saving Optimizer may earn a commission if we later add partner links. We do not currently claim card-issuer partnerships. Compare applications on the issuer’s site, and never carry a balance for points.

Key takeaways

  • Base earn at participating Esso/Mobil is 10 PC points per whole litre — about 1¢/L at the published 10,000 = $10 redemption (reviewed Sep 2026).
  • A no-fee PC Mastercard is the usual stack: public copy still markets 30+ pts/L (~3¢/L). Insiders World Elite advertises up to 70 pts/L (7¢/L in points) plus a volume kicker after 150 L in a calendar month — confirm live issuer FAQ.
  • If Esso is 6¢/L more than a station on your route, a 3¢ card stack loses. Shop GasBuddy or the street first, then add points you will actually redeem.
  • Redeem 4,000 pts for 10¢/L on the first 40 L, or 10,000 pts for $10. Same 0.1¢/point. Grocery bonus-redemption weeks can beat a convenience-store burn.
  • A Mobil beside Superstore or No Frills is worth the extra block when the grocery trip was happening anyway — not when you invent a detour.

Base earn at Esso/Mobil vs paying more than nearby stations

PC Optimum’s fuel FAQ (reviewed 20 Sep 2026, consistent with Esso’s public loyalty pages) still describes:

  • 10 points per whole litre at participating Esso and Mobil
  • Default burn: 10,000 points = $10, or 4,000 points = 10¢/L on the first 40 litres
  • Premium-grade tiles that add more points per litre when they are loaded — not a reason to buy Synergy Supreme if you do not already

10 points × 0.1¢ = 1¢ per litre. On a 40 L fill that is $0.40. A station that is 4¢/L cheaper saves $1.60 on the same 40 L before anyone opens an app. That is the whole framework: net ¢/L = posted − instant card − points you will redeem this quarter.

If earning the stack adds more than about a kilometre each way, you are buying loyalty with gasoline. At 8 L/100 km, an extra 2 km round trip is 0.16 L — more than a 1¢/L bonus on a 40 L fill once the extra fuel and time are honest.

PC Mastercard and Insiders stack rates explained carefully

Treat issuer marketing as a ceiling, then check the card you actually hold:

  • PC Financial Mastercard (no annual fee, typical grocery card): public Esso/PC copy still uses 30 points per litre or “30+” when combined with the base 10. That is ~3¢/L if you redeem at 0.1¢. Enough for most households that already shop No Frills or Superstore.
  • PC Insiders World Elite: Esso’s own FAQ has used the phrase up to 70 pts/L (7¢/L in points). That bundle usually assumes the Insiders card, sometimes premium fuel, and sometimes a monthly volume kicker. Public copy has also described +20 pts/L after 150 litres in a calendar month. 150 L is about four 40 L fills. Do not drive extra kilometres in week four to “hit Insiders.”
  • Interest: one month of revolving balance erases a year of 3¢/L. If you would carry a balance, pay debit and take the 1¢.

Insiders is a delivery-and-volume product. It is not a gas hobby. If you do not already want PC Insiders for grocery delivery, do not open it to chase 70-point tiles you may never see. See the PC Financial Mastercard grocery worksheet before you add a second card.

Redeeming points for litres vs grocery — opportunity cost

Default math is the same 0.1¢ per point:

  • 4,000 points = 10¢/L × 40 L = $4 off this fill
  • 10,000 points = $10 at Loblaw banners, Shoppers, or other published redemptions

Use the fuel burn when you are filling anyway and the grocery till does not have a better event. Bank points into a Shoppers bonus-redemption week when those events actually multiply value — that is when grocery can beat 10¢/L. Do not redeem 20,000 points on a $18 convenience-store basket of drinks and chips. The markup on the shelf is larger than the points.

If you never redeem, points ¢/L is zero. That is how people “earn” for three years and still pay the posted litre.

When Mobil beside a Loblaw banner is worth the detour

A Mobil on the Superstore or No Frills lot is a trip-chain, not a pilgrimage:

  1. You were already buying groceries there this week.
  2. The Mobil posted price is within about 3¢/L of the cheapest station you will pass on the same loop.
  3. Offers are loaded in the PC Optimum app before you tap.

If the Mobil is 8¢/L higher than the Esso (or independent) on the way home, fill on the way home. The “I was already here” argument dies when the litre is that wrong. Warehouse-club gas is a different membership question — see Costco gas vs Gold Star if that is your other till.

Offer loading and flash point promotions

  1. Sunday, with the grocery loop: open PC Optimum. Load fuel offers you would use on the commute — not a car-wash tile you never buy.
  2. Watch for 4,000 = 10¢/L versus a grocery 10,000 = $10. Same point value; use fuel when the tank is empty anyway.
  3. Flash “20× points on fuel this weekend” tiles expire. Screenshot if the till is fussy. They sit on top of base earn, not instead of the posted price.
  4. Insiders 150 L and any premium-fuel bonus: check once a month, only if the litres already happened.
  5. Redeem in chunks you feel ($4 at the pump, $10 at No Frills) so the balance does not become wallpaper.

Mistakes: burning points on convenience junk food

  • Redeeming 10,000 points on a $14 Esso café combo you would not have bought. The points are worth $10; the sandwich is not.
  • Chasing a Mobil that is 9¢/L high because it is “on the way to Shoppers.” Fill cheaper; shop Shoppers.
  • Opening Insiders World Elite for gas when you do not want the grocery delivery product.
  • Leaving 80,000 points unredeemed for two years. That is a 0¢/L stack.
  • Paying the Mastercard interest to “keep the points engine on.”

The commute test: if the Esso is not on the route you already drive, you do not have a PC gas strategy. You have a hobby. Loyalty is a rebate on the station you should have used.

Frequently asked questions

Is Esso Extra still a thing in Canada?

No. Participating Esso and Mobil stations pay PC Optimum. Treat any leftover Esso Extra language as expired. Confirm the live pump brand FAQ.

How much are PC Optimum gas points worth?

At the published 10,000 points = $10 redemption, each point is 0.1¢. Base 10 pts/L is about 1¢/L. A PC Mastercard at 30 pts/L is about 3¢/L. Points are worth zero until you redeem them.

Should I go out of my way for a Mobil next to Superstore?

Only if the grocery trip was happening anyway and the Mobil posted price is close to the cheapest litre on that loop. An 8¢/L premium is not “convenient.”

Is PC Insiders World Elite worth it for gas?

Only if you already want Insiders for grocery delivery and will hit the advertised fuel tiles without extra driving. Do not open the card as a gas hobby. Interest erases the stack.

Should I redeem points at the pump or at No Frills?

Same default 0.1¢/point. Use 4,000 = 10¢/L when you are filling anyway. Bank for a Shoppers bonus-redemption event when that event actually raises value. Do not burn points on convenience junk food.
