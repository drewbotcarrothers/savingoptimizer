Buying Used Electronics Safely in Canada: Scams, Checks and Payment

See it, test it, then pay · About 8 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 1 Oct 2026

In Canada, you can buy used electronics safely by seeing and testing the device before you pay, meeting in a public place, and checking that a phone is not reported lost or stolen. Common scams include asking for a deposit before you see the item, shipping-only sellers, fake payment links, and phones that are activation-locked to someone else's account. For phones, check the IMEI at devicecheck.ca and make sure the seller has signed out of their account before you hand over money.

Key takeaways

  • Never send a deposit before seeing the item.
  • Meet in a busy public place; many police services offer safe exchange zones.
  • Phones: check the IMEI at devicecheck.ca and confirm the device is signed out and reset.
  • Test everything: screen, battery, ports, cameras, buttons, charging.
  • Pay after inspection. Report fraud to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.

Common scams

Scam Warning sign What to do
Deposit to hold Seller wants money before you see it Do not pay until you inspect
Shipping only Seller cannot meet and asks for e-transfer first Buy locally or through a platform with buyer protection
Fake payment or verification link Link or code requests by text or email Never click or share codes
Activation-locked device Seller cannot sign out or reset Walk away
Stolen or blacklisted phone IMEI reported lost or stolen Check devicecheck.ca before paying

Device checklist

  • Phone: IMEI check at devicecheck.ca; signed out of the seller's account; factory reset in front of you; battery health; unlocked.
  • Laptop: boots normally; battery holds charge; keyboard, trackpad, ports and webcam work; no firmware password.
  • Console or tablet: account signed out; controllers work; no bans or locks.
  • Bring a charger, cable and a SIM card or earbuds to test.

Paying safely

Pay only after inspecting the device. Cash or a payment you send in person once satisfied are common for local deals. An Interac e-Transfer is hard to reverse once accepted, so do not send one before seeing the item. If something goes wrong, report it to the platform, local police and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check if a used phone is stolen in Canada?

Enter the phone's IMEI at devicecheck.ca, which checks phones reported lost or stolen with participating Canadian carriers.

Is it safe to send an e-Transfer to a seller?

Not before you have seen and tested the item. Transfers are hard to reverse once accepted.

What is an activation lock?

A security feature that ties a device to the owner's account. If the seller cannot sign out, the device may be unusable. Walk away.

Where should I meet a seller?

In a busy public place. Many police services offer safe exchange zones.

Where do I report a marketplace scam?

To the platform, local police and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501.
