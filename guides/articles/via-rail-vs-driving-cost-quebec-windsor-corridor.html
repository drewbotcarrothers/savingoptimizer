Via Rail vs driving the Québec–Windsor corridor: commuter and errand cost math

Toronto–Ottawa, Montréal–Toronto · About 17 minutes · 20 Sep 2026

Corridor travellers default to the 401 because “gas is $60” and ignore downtown parking, wear, and a day that starts with a slush parking-lot search. Via Rail’s Québec–Windsor trains are a transportation product: Union to Ottawa, Central Station to Toronto, Kingston, Québec, Windsor. Advance Economy can beat a solo drive. Four last-minute Economy seats usually lose to one Civic. This is cost math for meetings and family errands — not a vacation itinerary (that lives in Travel).

All-in driving: car TCO and 407 vs time. Mode chooser: flight vs train vs drive. GO last-mile: PRESTO ladder.

Key takeaways

  • Driving ~450 km Toronto–Ottawa: fuel at 8 L/100 km and $1.60/L is about $58 one way; add downtown parking and a 10–15¢/km wear line. “Just gas” is the lie.
  • Via Economy on that route often advertised in 2026 write-ups around $45–$150 one way (demand-based). Escape is cheap and rigid. Flexible fares are a meeting product.
  • Solo plus downtown-to-downtown: train usually wins money and nerves if you book ahead. Family of four: the car usually wins money unless someone was going to rent a vehicle anyway.
  • Last-mile is TTC, OC Transpo, STM, UP Express, or a short rideshare — not a downtown garage “because we have a car.”
  • Employer travel policies often prefer rail receipts over mileage plus parking. Ask before you default to the 401.

All-in driving: fuel, parking, wear, tolls, stress time

Worked one-way sketch (labelled) for Toronto–Ottawa, ~450 km:

  • Fuel: 8 L/100 km × 4.5 × $1.60/L ≈ $58. Use your trip computer and yesterday’s litre.
  • Wear / “not just gas”: 10–15¢/km for tires, service, and depreciation on a corridor day ≈ $45–$70. This is not the CRA mileage allowance; that is a reimbursement rule, not your cost.
  • Parking: $15–$40 downtown if you do not have a free stall. Green P and hotel lots vary.
  • 407: optional. A peak through-trip can add tens of dollars — calculator first.

Solo all-in often lands $90–$140 one way before you value four to five hours of driving. Two people split that; four people crush it. Stress and weather are why people still buy the train after they “did the gas math.”

Via fares: advance purchase vs flexible

Via prices the seat, not the kilometre. Escape (online, limited) is non-refundable and non-exchangeable. Economy is cheaper than Economy Plus; change fees apply (Via’s fare-conditions page: Economy exchanges often 25% or a $20 minimum plus fare difference — confirm live). Business is a workplace with a meal, not a family errand class.

Book mid-week, two to three weeks out, when you can. Same-week flexible is how a $55 problem becomes a $180 problem.

When the train wins for solo travellers vs families

Solo / duo: downtown meeting, no suitcase mountain, booked ahead — train. You work on the seat; you arrive at Union or Central, not a distant lot.

Family of three or four: the car usually wins cash unless you were renting anyway, someone cannot drive, or parking at the destination is a horror. Kids plus Escape-fare rigidity is a bad mix — buy a fare you can change or drive.

GO, UP, and local transit last-mile costs

Union Station is a feature. Pair Via with TTC, GO, or a short walk. Ottawa: OC Transpo from the station. Montréal: Métro from Central. Pearson is a different trip — UP Express is an airport product, not a corridor product. Do not add a downtown rental car “to feel safe” and then claim the train was expensive. If the last mile is a $60 rideshare each way, you priced the wrong station pair — or you should have driven.

Employer travel policy and expense claims

Many Canadian employers prefer a Via receipt to mileage + parking + 407. Some still pay cents per kilometre that exceed your true fuel cost (that is their policy, not your household TCO). If you keep the difference, you have an incentive to drive; if you care about the department budget, book the $70 Economy. Ask before you leave. Taxable benefits on personal-looking trips are a payroll question, not this page.

Sample Toronto–Ottawa and Montréal–Toronto day trips

  • Toronto–Ottawa day meeting: 06:xx train, 17:xx return. Two advance Economies $90–$220 versus one car $180–$280 plus a day of attention. Train unless the meeting is in Kanata with no bus and a $12 lot.
  • Montréal–Toronto overnight family: four Economies can be $400+; the car is one fuel tank and a hotel parking line. Drive, or book Escape months ahead if the dates are stone.

Frequently asked questions

Is Via cheaper than driving Toronto to Ottawa?

For one adult on an advance Economy fare, often yes once you add parking and wear — not if you only count $58 of gas. For four adults, the car usually wins money. Check live fares.

What is an Escape fare?

Via’s cheapest online bucket: limited seats, non-refundable, non-exchangeable. Fine for a date you will not move. Bad for a meeting that slides.

Should I take 407 to beat the train?

Rarely on money. Time the 401 first. A peak 407 through-trip is a separate bill. The train’s product is downtown arrival, not a faster highway.

Do I need a car in Ottawa or Montréal after Via?

Often no if the day is downtown. Use OC Transpo or STM. A client in the suburbs can flip the math back to driving.
