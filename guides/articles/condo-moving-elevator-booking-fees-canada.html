Condo moving rules in Canada: elevator bookings, deposits, and fee traps

The elevator is a first-class moving cost · About 16 minutes · 20 September 2026

Condo moves blow budgets when the freight elevator is booked in two-hour weekday windows and the deposit cheque is still in a kitchen drawer. Canadian corporations and strata councils treat access as a reserved utility, not a hallway courtesy. A $1,200 Saturday crew that cannot enter the dock is not cheaper than a Tuesday window with a certificate of insurance.

Patterns below are labelled 20 September 2026 from Canadian condo/strata moving explainers (Toronto and Vancouver city pages especially). Your declaration, bylaws, and management form win.

Key takeaways

  • Book both buildings 2–4 weeks out. Weekends are often closed.
  • COI naming the corporation — commonly $2 million liability — before you pay a mover deposit.
  • Photograph pads and get the damage-deposit refund path in writing.
  • Overtime on the dock is a second hourly invoice plus a possible building fee.
  • DIY vans fail more on paper than on muscle. Hybrid packing + pro lift is the usual adult outcome.

Typical booking windows and weekend restrictions

Management offices do not invent windows to annoy you. They keep resident elevators for residents. Freight or “service” cars are reserved in blocks:

  • Lead time: two to four weeks is the sentence Canadian mover city pages keep repeating. August 31 condos and 1 July in Québec book out earlier.
  • Length: two to four hours is common. A 2-bed that needs six hours needs two slots or a very honest inventory — cull volume first.
  • Days: weekdays, often 9 a.m.–4 p.m. or 10–2 / 1–5. Evenings and Sundays are frequently prohibited.
  • Passenger cars: using the pretty elevator with a sofa is how you lose the deposit and a neighbour’s goodwill.

Certificates of insurance movers must provide

A certificate of insurance (COI) is a one-pager from the mover’s insurer. The office wants to see:

  • Liability limit — $2 million is the number Canadian condo/strata pages most often cite.
  • The condominium corporation or strata named as additional insured or certificate holder (wording differs; they will hand you the exact line).
  • The move date and sometimes the unit number.
  • WCB / workers’ compensation proof in some buildings.

Reputable local companies issue this in a business day. A cash crew with a rented van often cannot. That single line is why the DIY vs pro worksheet treats downtown 2-beds as a different sport from a driveway bungalow. Put COI turnaround on the three-quote scorecard before you send an e-transfer.

Damage deposits and how to get them back

Buildings take a refundable deposit against scuffed pads, broken mirrors, and dock gates left open. Public city guides talk mid-hundreds; some towers ask $500–$1,000. Cheques are still a thing. Ask:

If you are renting the unit, confirm whether the landlord or you posts the deposit — it belongs on the condo rental checklist.

Loading dock and time-slot overtime fees

Two clocks run at once: the mover’s hourly rate and the building’s slot. If the sofa is still in the hallway at 3:02 p.m., you can owe:

  • Crew overtime or a second trip at the full minimum.
  • A building overtime or “after-hours” fee (some docks bill by the half hour).
  • A rescheduled elevator that does not exist until next Thursday — overlapping rent, hotel, or storage.

Long carries, stairs to a mezzanine locker, and a shuttle because the 26-foot truck cannot enter the ramp are quote lines, not surprises. Put them on paper. Month-end downtown parking tickets are the amateur tax.

Coordinating old and new buildings on one day

One reserved elevator is not a move. You need a window that releases you at building A with enough drive time to start building B. Sketch:

  1. Call both offices the same morning. Write contact names.
  2. Book A 8:30–12:30 and B 1:30–5:00 only if the drive plus dock queue is honest. Cross-town Toronto at 12:15 is not honest.
  3. If windows cannot stack, budget a night of storage or a split move (boxes one day, furniture the next). Split is cheaper than a blown deposit plus a hotel.
  4. Purchases and sales: lawyers and occupancy dates are a third calendar. Closing week is not “we’ll figure out the elevator later.”

If the old lease is still live, do not create a double-rent week because the only matching slots were 10 days apart — see early-exit timing.

DIY vs pro implications when elevators are scarce

Friends with a cargo van still have to pad the elevator, finish in the window, and produce insurance the office accepts. Many will not accept a personal auto policy. Pros win when:

  • The COI is mandatory and same-week.
  • The slot is three hours and the inventory is a 2-bed.
  • There is no resident parking for a 16-foot truck without a booked dock.

DIY still wins on a mid-size walk-up with a driveway and a superintendent who waves you through. Hybrid — you pack for two evenings, they take the sofa in the booked window — is how you stop paying $160/hour to wrap mugs. Details on the break-even page.

City notes: Toronto and Vancouver strata/condo patterns

Toronto / GTA: High-rise corporations commonly want a reserved freight car, COI, and a deposit. Month-end and Labour Day week are surge. Some older walk-ups have no freight car — that is stairs and a long-carry surcharge, not a free elevator. Downtown loading zones are timed; a ticket is not “part of DIY savings.”

Vancouver / Metro: Housing stock is heavily strata. Mover city pages describe weekday-only bookings, 3–4 week lead times, and named COIs. A townhome strata with a garage is a different sport from a downtown tower with a shared dock. Ask whether moving days are limited per floor — two units cannot share one Tuesday if the bylaw says one move per day.

Other cities (Calgary, Ottawa, Halifax) copy the same three ideas: reserve, insure, deposit. The form name changes. Call before you e-transfer anyone.

Frequently asked questions

How far ahead should I book a condo freight elevator in Canada?

Many Toronto corporations and Vancouver strata councils want two to four weeks, sometimes more around 1 July and 31 August. Weekday two- to four-hour windows are common; weekends are often closed or surcharge-only. Ask both buildings on the same call. Your declaration or bylaws beat a blog.

What is a certificate of insurance for a condo move?

A COI is a one-page proof of liability insurance, often $2 million, naming the condominium corporation or strata. Reputable movers commonly issue it within a day. A consumer truck rental may not satisfy the office. No COI, no elevator is a real sentence.

How do I get the elevator damage deposit back?

Photograph pads, walls, and the loading dock before and after. Get the refund path in writing (cheque, e-transfer, applied to fees). Walk the superintendent or concierge at the end of the slot. Deposits cited in city mover guides often land in the mid-hundreds; some towers ask $500–$1,000. Confirm yours.

Can I DIY a condo move?

Sometimes, if the building accepts a rental-truck COI or a resident move without one, and you can finish inside the window. Many downtown buildings will not. Hybrid — you pack, they lift inside a booked slot — is the usual adult outcome. Price a missed window as a second trip.
