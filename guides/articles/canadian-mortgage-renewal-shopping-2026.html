How to shop a Canadian mortgage renewal in 2026—and when switching lenders saves shelter cost

Shelter-cost shopping, not an investing lecture · About 16 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 11 Nov 2026

To shop a Canadian mortgage renewal in 2026, start 120 days out and get your current lender’s offer in writing. Since 21 November 2024, an uninsured straight switch between federally regulated lenders is no longer subject to an OSFI-prescribed minimum qualifying rate if you add no money or amortization. Borrowers accept the first renewal letter because it arrives on letterhead. That letter is an opening bid on your largest monthly bill. Shopping a renewal is shelter-cost reduction, not a personal-finance lecture on asset allocation. As of 21 November 2024, OSFI no longer prescribes the minimum qualifying rate for an uninsured straight switch at renewal between federally regulated institutions when you do not increase the loan amount or remaining amortisation. That is a big deal for people who were trapped by the stress test. Insured mortgage holders could already switch at renewal without a new stress test under the Canadian Mortgage Charter. Neither rule covers cash-out refinances or a longer amortization.

One number to keep in mind before you read your letter: the big banks’ posted 5-year conventional rate was 6.19% in the week of 4 November 2026 (Bank of Canada), while the average rate lenders actually charged on new uninsured fixed mortgages of 5 years or more was 4.58% in August 2026 (Bank of Canada / Statistics Canada). The posted rate is a sticker, not the market.

Key takeaways

  • Posted 5-year rate 6.19% vs 4.58% actually charged on new uninsured 5-year+ fixed loans (Aug 2026): don’t anchor to posted.
  • Start 120 days out. Documents first, shopping second.
  • Get the current lender’s offer in writing before you negotiate anyone else.
  • Uninsured straight switch: OSFI MQR prescription change 21 Nov 2024 — no extra money, no extra amort, federally regulated to federally regulated. Verify.
  • Score rate, cashback, fees, and prepayment privileges together.
  • Staying can still win if the gap is tiny after fees or if a switch would break a privilege you use.

Start 120 days out: calendar and document checklist

Gather: remaining balance, remaining amortisation, whether the loan is insured, prepayment history, portability, and any collateral charge vs standard charge (this affects how easy a switch is — ask your institution, do not guess from a forum). Write the maturity date in three calendars.

Get your lender’s offer in writing before you negotiate

If your mortgage is with a federally regulated lender such as a bank, it must send your renewal statement at least 21 days before the term ends, listing the balance, rate, payment frequency, term and fees, and it must tell you if the renewal will be automatic (Financial Consumer Agency of Canada). Twenty-one days is a deadline, not a shopping window, which is why this guide starts at 120 days.

Call or inbox: “Please send my renewal offer including rate, term, cashback, fees, and prepayment privileges.” Verbal “we’ll match” is not a document. You cannot shop a shrug. If they delay past 90 days, keep asking; your calendar does not care about their campaign season.

How far below the posted rate are lenders actually lending?

Well below. In 2026 the posted 5-year conventional rate tracked by the Bank of Canada stayed between 6.09% and 6.19%, while the average rate on new uninsured fixed mortgages of 5 years or more ran between 4.17% and 4.58%. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada says you may qualify for a discounted rate lower than the one quoted in your renewal letter.

Why it matters now: the Bank of Canada’s Financial Stability Report (May 2026) says the last pandemic-era five-year fixed-payment mortgages renew over the next 12 months. They are about 12% of all outstanding mortgages, and on average their payments rise about 15% (compared with December 2025 payments).

Straight switches and stress-test changes for uninsured mortgages

OSFI’s 21 Nov 2024 announcement: it will no longer prescribe the MQR it expects FRFIs to apply when uninsured mortgage borrowers switch to a new institution at renewal, provided it is a stand-alone uninsured mortgage, FRFI to FRFI, with no increase in remaining contractual amortisation or loan amount. OSFI still expects Guideline B-20 sound underwriting.

The ordinary MQR for new uninsured originations remains a greater-of contract+2% or 5.25% style floor in OSFI’s public MQR material — that is for originations, not the straight-switch exemption. Insured mortgage holders could already switch lenders at renewal without requalifying under the Canadian Mortgage Charter (Budget 2024), and the Department of Finance aligned the mortgage-insurance rules for low-ratio straight switches from 16 December 2024. Both straight-switch rules allow the balance to rise by up to $3,000 to cover transaction costs such as penalties or fees; equity take-out is not allowed. Extra money, a HELOC add, or resetting amortisation is not a straight switch.

Switching can cost money: discharge, registration, transfer or assignment fees, an appraisal, legal or notary fees. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada suggests asking the new lender whether it will pay some or all of those costs. If your mortgage is registered as a collateral charge, removing it can add fees too.

Do not tell yourself you “don’t need to qualify.” Lenders can still say no. This paragraph is a date-stamped map, not an approval.

Broker vs bank vs credit union shopping paths

Bank: incumbency, payroll, maybe a bundle. Broker: multiple lenders, still not every credit union, still a licensed professional you should treat as such — we are not introducing one.

Credit union: membership, sometimes slower, sometimes sharper on a remaining amort they like. Run all three if the file is large. Identical remaining amort and balance, or you are not comparing straight switches.

When staying put still wins

  • The gap after fees is a rounding error and you use a 20% prepayment every bonus year.
  • A switch would be cash-out or amort-extending — that is not a straight switch, and you might not like the new underwriting.
  • You are moving in 11 months and portability on the current product is actually usable.
  • Condo fees are about to jump $180 (see reserve-fund flags) — fix that stack first.

Write the scorecard once. The expensive mistake is treating the letter as a law of physics.

Frequently asked questions

When should I start shopping a Canadian mortgage renewal?

About 120 days before maturity. That is long enough to get your current lender’s offer in writing and to collect comparable terms without living in the letter that arrived last week.

Do I still need to pass the stress test to switch lenders?

OSFI said that as of 21 November 2024 it would no longer prescribe the MQR for uninsured straight switches at renewal between federally regulated lenders when the loan amount and remaining amortisation do not increase. Insured mortgage holders could already switch without requalifying. Extra cash and longer amortisation are different. Lenders still apply B-20 judgment. Confirm with institutions — this is not a guarantee you will be approved.

Is the rate in my renewal letter the best rate I can get?

Not necessarily. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada says you may qualify for a discounted rate lower than the one in your renewal letter. In August 2026 lenders charged an average of 4.58% on new uninsured 5-year-plus fixed mortgages, while the posted 5-year rate was above 6%. Get written offers and ask your lender to beat the best one.

Should I take the first renewal letter?

Not without a scorecard. Compare rate, cashback, fees, prepayment privileges, and portability. A 0.10% “win” that kills prepayments can be a loss if you get a bonus next year.

Broker vs bank vs credit union?

Three paths, same documents. A broker shops multiple lenders; your bank already has the file; a credit union may price loyalty differently. None is automatically cheapest. This is comparison education, not a referral.

Is this mortgage advice?

No. It is a household calendar for shelter cost. A licensed mortgage professional should structure a switch. We do not claim brokerage partnerships.
