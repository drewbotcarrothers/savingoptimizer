$75 Grocery Week in Canada: the series hub

Published episodes, dated flyers and honest receipt status · About 8 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 24 Nov 2026

Watch the video

Latest published series episode: a one-person Food Basics flyer example, Toronto, 19–25 Nov 2026. This is a flyer-built cart, not a photographed till receipt.

Watch on YouTube · Subscribe to Saving Optimizer on YouTube

The $75 Grocery Week series is a way to build a shop from real, dated prices, not a claim that every Canadian household can eat on the same number. This page collects the published episodes, the store/flyer evidence behind them and the limits of each example. Start with the newest episode, then use the earlier guides for protein, meal planning and second-stop decisions.

Key takeaways

  • Every episode needs a household size, date, location, flyer/price source and pantry assumption.
  • A flyer-built basket is not a till receipt. None of the episodes below is labelled as a photographed receipt.
  • $75 a week averages $325 a calendar month, not $300. Four-week envelopes need a fifth-week reserve.
  • Old flyer prices are evidence of the method, not offers you can still claim today.
  • Medical diets, northern prices, transport and cooking access can make a higher budget necessary.

The latest week: what $46.61 covers, and what it does not

The Food Basics flyer example for Toronto M4C, 19–25 Nov 2026, in episode 4 covers protein, produce, pasta/passata and milk for one adult for $46.61. It leaves $28.39 of a $75 envelope for oats, rice, dried beans and a flex line. Those remaining shelf prices were not supplied by the flyer, so the guide does not pretend the whole week is a verified $75 receipt. Check your shelf before committing the rest.

Episode 3 compared six items at Metro and Food Basics on Toronto flyers for 5–11 Nov 2026: $39.90 versus $25.70, a $14.20 difference. Its example 12 extra kilometres at a 73¢/km all-in planning ceiling cost $8.76, leaving $5.44 before time. That is a useful second stop only if you avoid adding an impulse shop. The driving distance is an example, not a route recommendation.

The linked $300 guide uses a four-week $75 envelope in parts of its explanation. For a real annual plan, use the calendar arithmetic above. A practical method is to fund $325 a month for a $75 weekly target, or keep $300 and aim at about $69 a week. Do not discover the difference in the fifth shopping week.

Build your own week in five lines

  1. Protein: choose the flyer price per usable portion, including bones, skin or waste you will not eat.
  2. Produce: buy what keeps and what your plan uses. Frozen vegetables can bridge the end of the week.
  3. Starch and pantry: record rice, oats, pasta, beans, oil and spices already on hand. A carry-in pantry is not free forever; reserve for replacement.
  4. Breakfast and lunch: plan these before allocating the rest to five attractive dinners.
  5. Flex: leave room for a missing staple or local price difference. Restaurants and household supplies stay outside unless explicitly included.

Receipt and flyer record for the next episode

Keep the flyer dates and store/postal code, the exact product size, quantity, unit price, tax/deposit where applicable and substitutions. After shopping, record the actual till total separately from the planned cart. Note pantry carry-in and food left over. If no real receipt is available, label the result a flyer-built example as this hub does.

Frequently asked questions

Can two people eat for $75 a week in Canada?

This series is not a promise for every household. Episode plans state their household size, assumptions, pantry carry-in and dated flyer evidence. The latest $75 example is for one adult; local prices, diet and access can require more.

Is $75 a week the same as $300 a month?

Only in a four-week envelope. Across 52 weeks, $75 a week is $3,900 a year or $325 per calendar month on average. A true $300 monthly budget averages about $69.23 a week.

Are these receipts from an actual grocery shop?

No. The published episodes use dated flyer or price-list calculations and labelled examples. The hub labels the receipt status and does not present a reconstructed cart as a photographed till receipt.

Where is each week’s flyer?

Each episode guide links or cites its dated flyer sources and records the store or Toronto region used. Old flyers may no longer be available at the original public URL.

Does the $75 include restaurants and household supplies?

The series grocery envelope is food from stores. Keep restaurants, cleaning supplies and toiletries in separate lines unless an episode explicitly says otherwise.

What if my city, diet or household cannot fit the number?

Raise the budget and use the method: compare unit prices, choose flyer proteins, plan leftovers safely and count waste. A repeatable plan matters more than hitting a headline number.
