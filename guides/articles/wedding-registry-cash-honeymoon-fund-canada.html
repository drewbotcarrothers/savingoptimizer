Wedding Registry vs Cash Fund vs Honeymoon Fund in Canada: Pros, Fees, Etiquette and Taxes

The best registry is the one that gets you what you need with the fewest fees · About 10 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 1 Oct 2026

Choose a traditional registry if you need household items, a cash fund if you would rather save toward a home or pay down costs, and a honeymoon fund if you want guests to contribute to experiences on your trip. You can also mix them. Before choosing a cash or honeymoon platform, read its fee page: some charge guests or couples a percentage, and payouts to a Canadian bank may carry a fee. For registries, use Canadian retailers where possible, because the CBSA says items mailed from outside Canada worth more than CAN$20 face duty and tax, and the CAN$60 gift exemption does not apply to items sent by a business. Gifts are not taxable income: the CRA lists most gifts among amounts not reported or taxed.

Key takeaways

  • Registry for things you need; cash or honeymoon fund for goals or experiences.
  • Read each platform's fee page for guest and payout fees.
  • Items shipped from outside Canada can face duty and tax above CAN$20.
  • Most gifts are not taxable income, according to the CRA.
  • Track every gift for thank-you notes.
  • Example with made-up inputs: what reaches you after fees.

Platform fees

Fees vary by platform and change over time, so read the platform's own fee page before choosing. As one example, Honeyfund's fee page says its Wallet is available to couples using U.S. dollars, that redeeming through its prepaid Mastercard or gift cards is fee-free, that paying out to PayPal, Venmo or a bank carries a small third-party fee, and that guests may be asked to add an optional amount at checkout to support the platform, which couples can turn off. For Canadian couples, check which options are available in Canadian dollars and what a bank payout costs. A simple alternative is to share an e-transfer address or a card box at the reception.

Registry items shipped from abroad

The CBSA says that under the Postal Imports Remission Order, items mailed to you worth CAN$20 or less are free of duty and tax, and above that you pay duty, GST or HST and any PST on the full value. Gifts worth CAN$60 or less are exempt only when a friend or relative sends them personally with a card or note saying it is a gift, and items sent by a business do not qualify. A registry item shipped directly from a U.S. or overseas store is sent by a business, so duty and tax can apply on delivery. Favour Canadian retailers or stores that ship duty-paid.

Etiquette

  • Put registry or fund details on the wedding website, not the invitation itself, if you want to keep invitations traditional.
  • Describe what a cash fund is for: a down payment, a first-year fund, or the honeymoon.
  • Offer a range of price points on any registry.
  • Never state a minimum gift amount.
  • Thank every guest for any gift, or for coming.

Store terms to check

  • Return and exchange policy for registry gifts, including duplicates and gift receipts.
  • Whether the store offers a completion discount on unpurchased items after the wedding, and for how long.
  • Shipping: to whom, at what cost, and whether items can be held until after the wedding.
  • Price changes: what happens if a guest buys an item that later goes on sale.
  • Whether guests can buy in store and online from the same list.

Group gifts

For expensive items, some registries allow guests to contribute part of the price. That keeps big-ticket items on the list without asking anyone for a large amount. If the platform charges for partial contributions, compare that cost with simply listing a cash fund for the same purpose.

Thank-you tracking

Keep a list of every gift: who, what or how much, and when the thank-you went out. Registry and fund platforms may provide a gift log; export it into one spreadsheet along with cash and cheques from the card box. Write notes within a few weeks while details are fresh, and mention how you will use the gift. Note cheques that still need depositing.

Mixing approaches

A small registry of essentials plus a cash or honeymoon fund covers most guests' preferences. Keep the registry short so items are bought, and remove items once you have them. Some guests will bring cash or cheques regardless; have a secure card box and one person responsible for it at the reception.

Taxes

The CRA lists most gifts and inheritances among amounts that are not reported or taxed. Income earned on gifts, such as interest after you invest them, is taxable. If you put cash gifts into a TFSA or FHSA, normal contribution room rules apply.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a platform without reading its fee page.
  • Registering at a foreign store that ships with duty due.
  • Listing only expensive items.
  • Losing track of who gave what.
  • Leaving the card box unattended.

Frequently asked questions

Is it rude to ask for cash for a wedding?

Not if it is done politely. Put details on the wedding website, explain what the money is for, and never state an amount.

Do honeymoon fund sites charge fees?

Some do, to guests, couples or on payout. Read the platform's fee page; for example, Honeyfund says payouts to a bank carry a third-party fee.

Are wedding gifts taxable in Canada?

The CRA lists most gifts and inheritances among amounts not reported or taxed. Income earned on them is taxable.

Do I pay duty on registry gifts from a U.S. store?

Possibly. The CBSA says items over CAN$20 face duty and tax, and the CAN$60 gift exemption does not apply to items sent by a business.

Should we have a registry and a cash fund?

It can work well. A short registry plus a fund covers different guest preferences.

How should we track gifts?

Keep one list with each giver, gift and thank-you date, combining platform logs and the card box.
