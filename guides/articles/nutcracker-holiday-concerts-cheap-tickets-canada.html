Holiday Concerts and The Nutcracker in Canada: Cheaper Seats, Rush Tickets and Free Alternatives

Pick the performance, then the seat, then the date · About 10 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 1 Oct 2026

Seeing The Nutcracker or a holiday concert in Canada costs less when you choose the performance first, then the cheapest seat that still gives you a good experience, then the least popular date. Prices vary widely by company and city. As of 1 Oct 2026, the National Ballet of Canada's Nutcracker in Toronto, running 5 to 31 December, lists tickets from $72, does not offer rush tickets for this production, and sells $12 standing-room tickets on the day of each performance. The Royal Winnipeg Ballet's Nutcracker, 18 to 27 December, starts at $45. Les Grands Ballets' Casse-Noisette in Montreal, 5 to 30 December, lists $99 to $194 including taxes and fees. Community performances, church concerts and free holiday events cost far less, or nothing.

Key takeaways

  • National Ballet of Canada, Toronto: 5 to 31 Dec 2026, from $72; no rush tickets for The Nutcracker; $12 standing room on performance day.
  • Royal Winnipeg Ballet: 18 to 27 Dec 2026, from $45. Alberta Ballet: Edmonton 3 to 6 Dec, Calgary 11 to 24 Dec. Les Grands Ballets: 5 to 30 Dec, $99 to $194 including taxes and fees.
  • Weekday evenings and early-December dates usually have more low-priced seats than weekends close to Christmas.

Seats: where the value is

In large theatres, the cheapest seats are usually at the top level or at the sides. For ballet, a higher seat is not always worse: from above, you see the patterns the dancers make on the stage, which is part of the choreography. Side seats can cut off part of the stage, so read the seat map and any partial-view note before buying.

The National Ballet's standing-room tickets, at $12 each, go on sale at 11 a.m. on the day of the performance, according to the company. For the 11 a.m. shows on 24 and 31 December, they go on sale at 9 a.m. Standing room means standing for the whole performance, which is a long time for young children, but it is the cheapest way into the theatre for teens and adults.

Dates: when prices are lower

Demand rises as Christmas approaches and on weekends. Weekday evenings early in the run, and weekday matinees, tend to have more seats in lower price zones. If your schedule allows, compare the first week of December with the week before Christmas. Performances on 24 December and between Christmas and New Year's are popular with families and often sell out at the higher prices.

Fees and how to compare prices

Canada's Competition Act prohibits drip pricing, which is advertising a price that cannot be obtained because mandatory fees are added later. The Competition Bureau treats unavoidable fees as part of the price that must be shown up front, with only taxes added. In practice, ticket pages still show prices in different ways, so compare the final amount before payment. Buying directly from the company's or venue's box office avoids resale mark-ups.

Watch for look-alike resale sites. A search for the show may show resale listings above the official box office, at higher prices. The official company website links to its own ticketing page. Resale tickets for sold-out performances can be legitimate, but buy only through a platform that guarantees the ticket and transfers it through the official ticketing account.

Discounts to ask about

  • Subscription or multi-show packages, which can lower the per-show price if you would attend other performances in the season.
  • Youth, student or under-30 pricing, where a company offers it; ask the box office.
  • Group rates for a set number of tickets, useful for extended families or school groups.
  • Accessible seating and companion tickets, sold through the box office.
  • Library culture passes, which in some cities include performing arts venues; see the guide to library passes and free outings.

Free and low-cost holiday music and dance

A professional Nutcracker is a treat, not the only option. Many ballet schools stage their own Nutcracker or a holiday recital at much lower prices, often in school auditoriums or community theatres. Community choirs, orchestras and church concerts perform holiday music through December, often with free admission or a suggested donation. Shopping centres, city halls and public squares host free carol sings and performances. Libraries run holiday programs for children.

These performances are also a better first experience for small children, because they are shorter, cost little if you leave early, and are often less formal about noise.

Experience gifts: tickets as a present

Tickets make a good gift for someone who would not buy them for themselves. Buy the tickets, not a voucher, if you know the date works. If you are not sure, a gift card from the company's box office may be safer than tickets for a fixed date. Read whether the gift card has an expiry; provincial gift card rules differ for cards tied to a specific service. The guide to experience gifts covers this in more detail.

Common mistakes

  • Buying from a resale site that ranks above the official box office in search results.
  • Comparing a price before fees with one that includes fees.
  • Choosing the most popular date when an earlier weekday would do.
  • Bringing young children to a long evening performance when a shorter community show would suit them better.
  • Forgetting parking, dinner and a babysitter in the total.

Holiday shows belong under outings in the December spending plan. Set the cap first, then pick the seat.

Frequently asked questions

How much are Nutcracker tickets in Toronto in 2026?

As of 1 Oct 2026, the National Ballet of Canada lists tickets for The Nutcracker, 5 to 31 December 2026, from $72, with prices by seat zone shown at purchase. Standing-room tickets are $12 on the day of each performance.

Does the National Ballet of Canada have rush tickets for The Nutcracker?

No. As of 1 Oct 2026, the company says rush tickets are not offered for The Nutcracker. Standing-room tickets at $12 go on sale at 11 a.m. on performance day, or 9 a.m. for the 11 a.m. shows on 24 and 31 December.

When is The Nutcracker in Winnipeg, Calgary, Edmonton and Montreal in 2026?

Royal Winnipeg Ballet: 18 to 27 December, from $45. Alberta Ballet: Edmonton 3 to 6 December and Calgary 11 to 24 December. Les Grands Ballets in Montreal: 5 to 30 December, $99 to $194 including taxes and fees.

How can I see a holiday show for less?

Pick a weekday early in the run, choose upper-level seats, buy from the official box office, ask about youth or group pricing, and consider ballet school, choir and church performances that cost little or are free.
