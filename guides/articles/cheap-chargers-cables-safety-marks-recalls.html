Cheap Chargers and Cables: Safety Marks and Recalls

A cheap charger is fine. An uncertified one isn't · About 5 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 24 Nov 2026

You don't need the brand-name charger, but you do need a certified one. Health Canada says plug-in chargers must carry a recognized Canadian certification mark such as CSA, cUL or cETL. Uncertified chargers can cause shocks, burns or fires. Check the mark, search the Government of Canada recalls site, and be wary of prices that seem too good.

Key takeaways

  • Look for a Canadian mark: CSA, cUL, cETL and others (Health Canada, checked 24 Nov 2026).
  • Warning signs: poor quality, very low price, spelling errors.
  • Check recalls-rappels.canada.ca before you buy.
  • Match the voltage and current to your device.
  • Don't charge on a bed or couch.

Buying cheap but safe

  • Buy from stores you can return to.
  • Check the mark on the charger itself, not just the box.
  • Search the recalls site for the brand and model.
  • Replace frayed or damaged cables.
  • Use the charger that came with your device when you can.

Example: a certified third-party charger at $25 vs an uncertified one at $8. Saving $17 isn't worth a fire risk.

Frequently asked questions

Are cheap chargers safe?

They can be, if they carry a recognized Canadian certification mark.

What marks should a charger have in Canada?

Look for marks such as CSA, cUL or cETL.

How do I check for charger recalls?

Search recalls-rappels.canada.ca for the brand and model.

How can I spot a counterfeit charger?

Poor quality, a very low price and spelling errors are warning signs.

Is it safe to charge my phone on the bed?

No. Soft surfaces can trap heat and cause overheating.
