Razor Costs in Canada: Cartridges vs Safety Razors vs Subscriptions, per Shave

Cost per shave · About 12 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 29 Sep 2026

In Canada as anywhere, the cost per shave of a razor is the upfront cost plus refills, divided by the shaves you will take. Over 200 made-up shaves in two years, the four options in this guide cost between $50 and $220. A cartridge looks cheap until you divide the year by the number of shaves you get from each one. This page is that division. The prices in the comparison are an example with made-up inputs. Nothing here ranks a razor. This page does not say a razor is subject to the September 2026 surtax. Tariff items 8510 and 8516.31 do not appear in the schedules of SOR/2026-186 as read on 29 Sep 2026. Whether a particular electric shaver falls under some other listed item is a classification question: check with CBSA at 1-800-461-9999 or your carrier.

Cost per shave method

Write four numbers before you compare systems. How many shaves you expect over the period you care about.

The upfront price of the handle, the safety razor, or the electric body. The price of one refill, blade, or head. How many shaves you will take from that refill before you replace it. Cost per shave is upfront plus all the refills you will buy, divided by shaves. A refill you stretch past the point of irritation is not a lower cost. It is a different product experience, and this page states no extra-shave count from a manufacturer.

Example with made-up inputs. The period is two years and 200 shaves, about two shaves a week. Disposable: 2 dollars, 4 shaves, 50 razors, 100 dollars, 50 cents a shave. Cartridge: handle 20 dollars, refill 5 dollars, 5 shaves, 40 refills, 200 dollars of refills, 220 dollars in total, 1 dollar and 10 cents a shave. Safety razor: 40 dollars, blade 50 cents, 10 shaves, 20 blades, 10 dollars of blades, 50 dollars in total, 25 cents a shave. Electric: body 90 dollars, one head at 40 dollars bought at month 12, 130 dollars, 65 cents a shave. The head interval is also a made-up input. None of these are prices from a store.

Cartridge systems and store-brand compatible refills

A cartridge system is the handle plus the refills that seat on it. Price the handle once.

Price every refill you will buy. If the starter kit includes a few cartridges, subtract those from the refill count so you do not pay for them twice in the worksheet. In the made-up example the 20 dollar handle does not include the 40 refills. Change that cell when your kit does.

A store-brand refill is compatible only when the package names the handle. This page does not claim that any store brand fits any name-brand handle. If the package is silent, treat it as its own system and give it its own row. The unit-price habit for other household goods is the store-brand household guide. Safety razors: upfront cost vs blade cost

The safety-razor row is cheap in the made-up example because the blade is 50 cents and the razor is paid once. That result collapses if you replace the razor, if you buy blades you do not use, or if you abandon the razor after a month. Spread the 40 dollars over the shaves you will actually take. If you take 20 shaves and quit, the razor alone is 2 dollars a shave before blades, which loses to the disposable row in this example.

Blade price still has to be a price you can buy in Canada. The made-up 50 cents is not that price. When you find a tuck, divide the tuck price by the number of blades, then divide by shaves per blade. A subscription can change the blade price. It does not change the method.

Razor subscriptions that ship to Canada

Name a club only when a page of its own said a Canadian address can receive it. On 29 Sep 2026 two did.

Rocky Mountain Barber Company’s Great Canadian Shave Club page describes five blades and a handle on the first order, five blades on later orders, a frequency you choose, and the ability to pause or cancel. The social description says delivery every month, a handle with the first month, and free shipping to all provinces and territories. The price meta on that page listed 13 dollars and 99 cents CAD. Checkout can still differ from a meta tag. Read the total before you subscribe.

Henson Shaving Inc.’s Blades for Life terms say you must be a resident of Canada or the United States, buy a Henson razor and the subscription together, and not have bought the subscription before. The terms define a lifetime supply as 100 razor blades every two years, for a term of 50 years unless it ends sooner. The first shipment is 100 blades. Every two years the company emails a coupon for the then-current cost of the next 100 blades, including shipping and handling. A price was not copied, because the terms say the price is whatever checkout shows that day. A satisfaction return starts by contacting them within 100 days of delivery and must include the razor and the two-year supply. You pay return shipping. The terms say Ontario law governs. Any other club, including clubs that market to the United States only, was not confirmed. Check whether it ships to Canada before you subscribe. A renewal you can miss is the renewal calendar. Amazon’s own refill program, if you use it for blades, is Subscribe and Save, which is not a razor club.

Extending blade life

Rinse the blade and let it dry. Replace it when it tugs.

This guide does not cite a manufacturer page that promises a number of extra shaves from a trick, so it states none. The shave counts in the made-up table are inputs so the division has a denominator. They are not a claim that a cartridge lasts five mornings on your face.

A dull blade you keep to protect the arithmetic is a poor trade if it irritates your skin. The saving is the shaves you get comfortably, not the longest number you can force. Do not share a blade. That is hygiene, not a price tip, and it is not medical advice.

Electric shavers and trimmers: head replacement costs

An electric shaver moves the cost from blades to a head or a foil. In the made-up example the body is 90 dollars and one head at 40 dollars is bought at month 12, and no second head is bought before month 24. If the head you own dies sooner, add another 40 dollars and the total is no longer 130. The month-12 interval is a made-up input.

A trimmer with no separate head is a different row: upfront only, until it fails. This page does not put a failure month on it. Tariff item 8509.80.90 is on the 25 percent schedule of the surtax order. This guide does not confirm whether an electric shaver or toothbrush is classified there. Check with CBSA. This page does not say that a razor is subject to the surtax. The toothbrush-head guide is the same kind of ownership math for a different tool.

Frequently asked questions

What's the cheapest way to shave in Canada?

This page does not rank a system, because the prices in the table are made-up inputs. The method is cost per shave: upfront cost plus refills, divided by the shaves you will take. In the labelled example the safety-razor total is the lowest of the four, and that result is not a shelf price. Check a Canadian checkout before you treat any club as available.

Are safety razors really cheaper?

They can be, after the upfront razor is spread over the shaves you will take, because the made-up blade price in the example is far below the made-up cartridge price. That gap is not a measured Canadian average. A safety razor you will not learn to use is waste, whatever the arithmetic says.

Do razor subscriptions ship to Canada?

Two did, on pages checked 29 Sep 2026. Rocky Mountain Barber Company's Great Canadian Shave Club lists free shipping to all provinces and territories, and Henson Shaving's terms require the buyer to be a resident of Canada or the United States. Any other club was not confirmed. Check the checkout for a Canadian address before you subscribe.

How can I make blades last longer?

Rinse and dry the blade, and replace it when it tugs rather than on a slogan. This guide does not cite a manufacturer page with an extra-shave count, so it states none. A made-up shave count in the cost table is an input to the division, not a promise the blade will last that many mornings. Stretching a blade that irritates your skin is not a saving.

Are store-brand cartridges compatible with name-brand handles?

Only if the package says so. This page does not claim that a store-brand cartridge fits a name-brand handle. If the package is silent, treat it as a different system and price it on its own. Fit comes before the per-shave division.
