Destination Weddings From Canada: Costs for Couples and Guests

Cheaper for the couple can mean dearer for every guest · About 11 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 1 Oct 2026

A destination wedding can cost a Canadian couple less than a large wedding at home, because the guest list is usually smaller, but it moves much of the cost onto guests, who pay for flights, hotels and time off, as of 1 October 2026. Before booking, couples should decide whether to marry legally abroad or at home: Global Affairs Canada says marriages legally performed in another country are usually valid in Canada without registering them here, but the destination may require documents such as a single status affidavit, translations and authentication. Travel medical insurance is essential for everyone, because provincial health plans cover very little outside Canada.

Key takeaways

  • A smaller guest list can cut the couple's costs, but guests pay for travel, so expect fewer to attend.
  • Read room block terms: minimum nights, release dates and who pays for unbooked rooms.
  • Marriages legally performed abroad are usually valid in Canada and do not need to be registered here. Canadian embassies do not perform marriages.
  • The destination may ask for a passport, birth certificate, marriage search letter, single status affidavit or a statement in lieu of a certificate of non-impediment, sometimes translated and authenticated.
  • Many couples marry legally at home first and hold a symbolic ceremony abroad.
  • Everyone needs travel medical insurance; consider trip cancellation coverage too.

Asking guests to fly abroad is a big request. Give at least a year's notice, choose a destination with direct flights from major Canadian cities if possible, and accept that some people will not be able to come. Some couples hold a casual party at home afterwards for people who could not travel.

Resort packages and room blocks

Resorts and hotels often offer wedding packages that include the ceremony set-up, an officiant, a small reception and sometimes free events if enough guests book. They may require a room block: a set number of rooms held for guests at a group rate. Read the terms carefully.

  • Attrition: whether you, the couple, must pay for rooms guests do not book.
  • Release date: when unbooked rooms go back to the hotel.
  • Minimum nights: whether guests must stay a set number of nights.
  • What "free wedding" offers actually require, such as a number of booked room-nights.
  • Taxes, resort fees and service charges, and whether prices are in Canadian or U.S. dollars.

A courtesy block, where unbooked rooms are released without penalty, carries less risk for the couple than a contracted block with attrition charges.

Marrying legally abroad or at home

Global Affairs Canada says you cannot get married at a Canadian embassy or consulate, and Canadian consular officials do not perform ceremonies. Marriages legally performed in another country are usually valid in Canada, and you do not need to register them here. Each country has its own rules, so contact the embassy, high commission or consulate of the destination country for the documents needed.

Getting documents, translations and authentication takes time and costs money, and some destinations also require residency days before the wedding or blood tests. Many couples avoid this by marrying legally in Canada, with a licence and a short civil ceremony, and then holding a symbolic ceremony at the destination. See the city hall wedding guide and the marriage licence guide. Same-sex marriages are not recognized in many countries, so check the destination's laws and Global Affairs Canada's travel advice first.

Insurance and health coverage

Provincial health plans pay little or nothing for care outside Canada, so every traveller needs travel medical insurance. Our guide to travel medical insurance and provincial gaps explains what to look for. Trip cancellation and interruption insurance can cover non-refundable costs if illness or other covered reasons stop you from travelling; read the covered reasons and exclusions. Credit cards may include some coverage; check the details before relying on it.

Flight disruptions

For flights to, from and within Canada, the Air Passenger Protection Regulations set airline obligations for delays, cancellations, denied boarding and lost baggage, with rules depending on whether the cause was within the airline's control. Arrive at least a day or two before the wedding, so a delayed flight does not mean missing it. The travel money guide covers paying abroad without large foreign exchange fees.

In this example, the couple save $13,100. Each guest spends $2,400, which across 30 guests is $72,000 of total spending by guests. That is not a reason not to do it, but it is a reason to give notice early, choose an affordable destination and be gracious with people who cannot come.

A plan

  1. Decide between a legal ceremony abroad or at home, and check the destination's requirements.
  2. Choose a destination with direct flights and a range of hotel prices.
  3. Read room block and package terms before signing.
  4. Give guests a year's notice and clear costs.
  5. Buy travel medical insurance and consider cancellation coverage.
  6. Arrive early to allow for flight delays.

Common mistakes

  • Signing a contracted room block with attrition charges without understanding it.
  • Starting legal paperwork for a foreign marriage too late.
  • Assuming provincial health coverage applies abroad.
  • Flying in the day before the wedding.
  • Expecting the same guest numbers as a home wedding.

Frequently asked questions

Is a destination wedding legal in Canada?

Global Affairs Canada says marriages legally performed in another country are usually valid in Canada, and you do not need to register them here. Check the destination's requirements with its embassy or consulate.

Can I get married at a Canadian embassy abroad?

No. Canadian embassies and consulates do not perform marriages, and consular officials do not have to attend. They can share information about local laws and service providers.

What documents do I need to marry abroad?

It depends on the country. Global Affairs Canada lists documents that may be requested, such as a passport, birth certificate, marriage search letter, single status affidavit and a statement in lieu of a certificate of non-impediment, sometimes translated and authenticated.

Do destination wedding guests need travel insurance?

Yes. Provincial health plans cover little or nothing outside Canada, so every guest should buy travel medical insurance. Trip cancellation coverage can also protect non-refundable costs.
