How to get and compare Canadian moving quotes without overpaying

Condo access fees on the scorecard, not the homepage band · About 16 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 20 Sep 2026

To compare moving quotes in Canada, send the same inventory to every mover and ask whether each number is an estimate, a binding quote or a not-to-exceed. In a condo, book the freight elevator and get a certificate of insurance before you pay anyone. Three mover sites can show three calm numbers and still describe three different jobs. One forgot the long carry. One is a cash crew with no shop. One priced a Saturday in a downtown elevator window. The DIY vs movers worksheet already said hidden truck costs and COIs matter. This page is the quote-comparison scorecard: same inventory, binding language, valuation, seasonality, and the condo access fees Canadian buildings actually charge.

Figures below are labelled 2026 industry ranges from public Canadian mover explainers — not a bid for your two-bed. Get three written quotes.

Key takeaways

  • Send the same inventory to every shop. Photos of closets beat “a few boxes.”
  • Ask whether the number is an estimate, a binding quote, or a not-to-exceed. Estimates need a buffer.
  • Red flags: huge lowball, cash-only, no local address, no COI, a fat deposit before anyone has seen the piano.
  • Condo: book the freight elevator and demand a COI (often $2 million naming the corporation) before you pay anyone.
  • Month-end, 1 July in Québec, and August 31 condos are surge windows. A Tuesday mid-month is a different sport.

What a complete inventory looks like for accurate quotes

One shared list, in writing:

  • Room-by-room furniture, including IKEA pieces that die on stairs.
  • Box count you will actually pack, plus what you want them to pack.
  • Appliances, gym gear, plants (many crews will not take plants or paint).
  • Access: floors, elevator vs stairs, walk to truck, parking, loading-zone permits.
  • Both addresses’ condo rules: elevator hours, COI, pads, no-move Sundays.

If you omit the treadmill, they will not omit the hourly. Virtual quotes fail when the closet is a storage unit. Film every door if they will not visit.

In-home vs virtual estimates—and binding vs non-binding language

In-home still wins for stairs, long carries, and “we have a piano.” Virtual is fine for a sparse one-bed if you are honest on camera. Then read the verbs: estimate means the clock can run; binding or not-to-exceed means they ate the risk if the inventory was true.

If the inventory was a lie, binding will not save you. Some consumer explainers suggest adding about 25% to a non-binding local estimate. Label that as a buffer, not a law.

Ask whether travel time, fuel, stairs, long carry, shuttle (when a truck cannot reach the door), packing, and storage are in or out. HST is extra in Ontario. A $120–$180/hour public band for two movers + truck (2026 explainers) is not your total if they billed 90 minutes from the yard.

Red flags: huge lowball, cash-only, no local address

  • Quote A is 40% under B and C with the same list.
  • Cash only, e-transfer to a personal name, or “we’ll settle on the truck.”
  • No street address, no WCB talk, no insurance certificate they can issue in a day.
  • Deposit larger than a modest booking hold before anyone has seen the unit.
  • A new website, stock photos, and a phone that goes to voicemail after you book.

The hostage-fee pattern is: load the truck, then discover “extra weight” or “stairs.” If the crew will not start until you pay a new number, you are not saving. You are stuck. Reputable shops put access fees on page one.

Insurance valuation options explained simply

Basic released valuation (a low per-pound default on some contracts) is not “my sofa is covered.” Declared-value or full-value options cost more and need a list. Your tenant or home policy may cover some contents in transit — call and ask; comparison education only. Photograph serial numbers before the truck. We do not rank movers or insurers.

Seasonality: summer weekend premiums

Canada’s move calendar is lumpy. Month-end, the last weekend of August, and 1 July in Québec are when crews are scarce and hourly minimums grow.

A Tuesday in February in a mid-size city is cheaper than a Saturday downtown Toronto condo. If your lease ends on the 31st, ask whether a 28th or 2nd window exists — one extra night of overlap can beat surge labour. Pair with increase walk-away math so you are not booking a van you do not need.

Vancouver and Toronto condo pages from movers themselves talk about 3–4 week elevator bookings and COIs. A DIY truck may not satisfy the office. That single line is why downtown 2-beds fail the “we’ll just rent a van” test.

Deposit and cancellation terms to negotiate

A modest booking deposit that is refundable outside a stated window is normal. A 50% cash hold to a personal e-transfer is not.

Ask: when is the deposit refundable, what happens if the elevator is revoked, who pays if they miss the window, and whether weather or truck breakdown slides you without a new surge fee. Get the COI issued before move morning. If this move is a walk-away from a rent fight, do not give notice until the quote and the next unit are real.

Frequently asked questions

How many moving quotes should I get in Canada?

Three written quotes on the same inventory list. Public 2026 explainers often cite about $120–$180 per hour for two movers plus a truck. Those are bands, not your job. The cheapest number with no elevator plan is not a quote.

What condo access fees do Canadian movers forget to price?

Freight-elevator reservations (often two to four weeks ahead, weekday windows), a certificate of insurance naming the corporation — commonly described as $2 million liability — and sometimes a refundable elevator damage deposit. A missed slot is a second trip at full hourly.

What are red flags on a cheap moving quote?

A huge lowball versus the other two, cash-only, no local street address, no WCB or insurance talk, a demand for a large deposit before a site visit, and “estimate” language with no inventory. Hostage-fee patterns show up after the truck is loaded.

Is a virtual estimate as good as an in-home visit?

Virtual can work for a sparse one-bed if you film every closet. In-home still wins for stairs, long carries, and pianos. Binding or “not-to-exceed” language beats a hopeful estimate. If it is an estimate, add a buffer — some consumer explainers use about 25%.
