Gel and Regular Manicures at Home vs Salon: Cost and Nail Health

Cost per manicure you finish · About 12 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 29 Sep 2026

A standing gel appointment is a small bill on a short cycle. The useful number is the cost of one manicure you finish, not the price of a kit on a shelf. This page puts a West Vancouver menu next to a made-up home kit, and it says what the surtax order actually lists for manicure preparations. Adult haircuts are the haircut guide. Student nail clinics, if a school offers them, are the beauty school guide. Nothing here ranks a salon or a lamp, and nothing here is medical advice.

Key takeaways

  • La Rouge Hair & Spa, West Vancouver, lists a regular manicure at $40 and a gel manicure at $65. Take off gel and acrylic is $15. Checked 29 Sep 2026. The menu does not say tax is included.
  • Example with made-up inputs: ten gel visits at $70 are $700. A $90 kit plus $40 of refills, spread over ten manicures, is $13 each.

  • A store-brand remover or polish is a label comparison, on the toiletries guide, not a claim that it removes gel.

Salon pricing

La Rouge Hair & Spa’s nails page, checked 29 Sep 2026, prints a regular manicure at $40: length, shape, cuticle work, and regular polish. A gel manicure is $65: length, shape, cuticle work, builder gel, and gel polish.

An express gel manicure is $45. A gel polish change is $45. A regular polish change is $30. Take off gel and acrylic is $15. A combo of hands and toes takes $10 off, on that page. The salon describes the work as water-free. The page does not say the prices include tax, and it does not print a tip.

Use the line you will book. A $45 express gel is not the $65 gel if the express service is the one on the appointment. Removal at $15 belongs in the visit when you pay it, including when you switch colours or stop. A regular manicure at $40 that you redo twice as often is not automatically cheaper than gel. Divide by the manicures, which is the last section. Other salons are other menus. This page does not turn La Rouge into a national price.

At-home kit costs

A home kit is a lamp, a colour, a base and top if the instructions require them, and the remover the same instructions name. Price the set you will finish, not a lamp you use twice.

This page does not quote a shelf price. Watch a listing with the Amazon.ca price guide. Example with made-up inputs: a kit at $90 and refills at $40 over the manicures in the annual table. Those dollars are not La Rouge and not a store.

A bottle marked as a good of the United States can add a surtax before it is a bargain. Manicure or pedicure preparations are tariff item 3304.30.00 in the 2026 Customs Tariff, chapter 33, checked 29 Sep 2026, at a most-favoured-nation rate of 6.5 percent, with United States Tariff treatment Free. The same item is on Schedule 3 of the United States Surtax Order (2026), SOR/2026-186, which is a 50 percent surtax of the value for duty. The order, checked the same day, is current to 2026-09-21 and was last amended on 2026-09-08. It comes into force on 8 September 2026. The full comparison of beauty lines is the US beauty orders guide.

UV/LED safety notes

This guide does not cite a Health Canada page, or a Canadian Dermatology Association page, on UV or LED nail lamps. It makes no claim that the lamps are safe and no claim that they are unsafe. Follow the instructions that came with the lamp: the time it names, the distance it names, and who it says should not use it.

Do not borrow a rule from a different machine. Health Canada’s tanning-equipment advice is about tanning beds and sunlamps. A nail lamp is not given that label on this page. If your hands react, stop and speak to a clinician. That is not a product ranking, and it is not medical advice. Sunscreen rules for skin, which are a separate regime, are the sunscreen guide if you are deciding what to put on skin, not a manicure rule.

Removal without damage

Count removal as its own line. At La Rouge, take off gel and acrylic is $15.

If you pay that every visit, a $65 gel manicure is $80 before tax and tip, on their menu plus that add-on. If removal is included in the service you booked, do not add it twice. The menu’s gel manicure line does not say removal is included.

At home, the only directions this page will point to are the ones in the kit you bought. It does not cite a soak time, a percentage of acetone, or a Health Canada method. Stop if it hurts. A nail that splits because removal was forced is a salon repair, which on La Rouge’s menu is $6 per finger, and that repair can erase the saving. This is not medical advice.

Cost per manicure

Divide what you paid by the manicures you finished. Salon: the menu line, plus removal if you paid it, plus a tip if you paid one.

Home: kit plus refills, divided by manicures, with the lamp spread across the ones you will do before you replace it. Example with made-up inputs. Ten salon gel visits at $70 are $700, or $70 each. A $90 kit plus $40 of refills, used for ten manicures, is $130, or $13 each. The gap, $570, exists only in this example. Two home manicures on that same kit are $65 each before you have even matched the salon, and you still own the bottles.

Days worn change the year, not the division, unless you book more visits because the manicure failed early. This page does not cite a standard wear time. Write the days you got. A regular polish at La Rouge’s $40 that you replace weekly is a different year from gel at $65 that you replace monthly, and only your calendar knows which one you live. Store-brand polish is a different comparison, on the toiletries guide.

Frequently asked questions

Is a home gel kit cheaper than the salon?

It is cheaper only across the manicures you finish, after the lamp and the refills. Example with made-up inputs: ten salon gel visits at $70 are $700, and a $90 kit plus $40 of refills is $130, which is $13 a manicure if you finish ten. La Rouge Hair & Spa in West Vancouver lists a gel manicure at $65, which is their menu, not the example. If you abandon the kit after two uses, the home cost per manicure goes up.

Are UV nail lamps safe?

This guide does not cite a Health Canada page or a Canadian Dermatology Association page on UV nail lamps, so it makes no safety claim about them. Follow the instructions that come with the lamp you own. A tanning bed is a different device. This page does not treat a nail lamp as tanning equipment.

How do I remove gel without damage?

This guide does not cite a Health Canada method for taking gel off. La Rouge lists take off gel and acrylic at $15 on the menu checked 29 Sep 2026. At home, follow the kit’s own directions and stop if it hurts, because a removal that lifts the nail plate is not a saving. This is not medical advice.

How long should a manicure last?

This page does not cite a wear-day rule, and La Rouge’s menu does not print one. Cost per manicure falls as the days you actually get from it rise. Write down the days you got, then divide the visit or the kit by the manicures you finished. An advertisement’s two-week claim is not your number.

Does the counter-tariff apply to nail polish?

Manicure or pedicure preparations are tariff item 3304.30.00, which is on Schedule 3 of the United States Surtax Order (2026) at 50 percent of the value for duty when the goods originate in the United States. For a personal import, that means they are marked as a good of the United States. Whether your bottle is that tariff item depends on classification: check with CBSA (1-800-461-9999) or your carrier. The list covers specific tariff items, not all cosmetics.
