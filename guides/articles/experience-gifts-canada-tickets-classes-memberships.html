Experience Gifts in Canada: Tickets, Classes and Memberships That Get Used

A date on the calendar beats a voucher in a drawer · About 10 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 1 Oct 2026

An experience gift in Canada is good value when it has a date on the calendar, matches something the person already wants to do, and costs less per use than they would pay on their own. Tickets to a specific event, a class with a start date, or a membership the person will use several times usually get used. Open-ended vouchers often do not. Read the expiry terms before buying: Ontario's gift card rules ban expiry dates on most gift cards but exempt cards for a single specific service, such as a spa treatment or a class, so a voucher for one experience can expire. As a gift for the outdoors, the Parks Canada Discovery Pass for 2026 costs $83.50 for an adult or $167.50 for a family or group, and youth 17 and under get in free.

Key takeaways

  • The best experience gifts have a date: tickets to a specific show, a class with a start date, or a planned outing together.
  • Cost-per-use test: a membership is good value if the person will use it enough times that each visit costs less than a single admission.
  • Read voucher terms. Ontario exempts single-service gift cards from its no-expiry rule, so a voucher for one experience can expire.
  • Parks Canada Discovery Pass 2026: $83.50 adult, $167.50 family or group of up to seven in one vehicle; youth 17 and under free. The free Canada Strong Pass ended 7 September 2026.
  • Example with made-up inputs: a $150 membership used six times costs $25 a visit, against $40 single admissions.

Why experience gifts often go unused

The problem with many experience gifts is not the experience. It is the gap between the gift and the day it happens. An open voucher for a cooking class, a spa or a hot-air balloon ride requires the recipient to book, find a free day and sometimes pay extra. Months pass, and the voucher stays in a drawer. Some expire.

The fix is to close that gap. Give tickets to a specific date the person can attend, sign them up for a class with a start date, or offer to go together and put the date on the calendar when you give the gift. If you cannot pick a date, choose an experience with flexible booking and no expiry.

The cost-per-use test for memberships

A membership looks expensive next to a single ticket. It is good value only if it gets used. Divide the membership price by the number of visits the person will realistically make in a year, and compare the result with the single-admission price. A family that visits a science centre three times a year may come out ahead with a family membership; a family that goes once will not.

Ask before buying: Does the person live close enough to visit often? Do they already go? Does the membership include guests or children? Many museums and galleries have free days or free evenings, and some public libraries lend passes; for a person who goes once a year, those may be better than a membership. The guide to library passes and free outings lists options.

Parks Canada passes

For someone who visits national parks, the Parks Canada Discovery Pass covers admission to more than 80 Parks Canada places for 12 months from the month of purchase. According to Parks Canada's fee pages for 2026, the adult pass is $83.50 and the family or group pass is $167.50, covering up to seven people arriving together in one vehicle. Youth 17 and under get in free. The pass covers daily admission only; camping, some guided activities and other services cost extra.

In 2025 and 2026 the federal government offered free Parks Canada admission through the Canada Strong Pass in set periods. The 2026 summer period ended on 7 September 2026, and as of 1 Oct 2026 no holiday extension had been announced. A Discovery Pass bought as a gift should start in the month the recipient will first use it, because it runs 12 months from purchase.

Gift cards, vouchers and expiry rules

Many experience gifts are sold as gift cards or vouchers. Provincial consumer protection rules apply, and they differ. Ontario bans expiry dates on most gift cards, but its rules exempt cards for a specific single good or service, such as one spa treatment or a specific class. That means a voucher for one experience may carry an expiry date while a general dollar-value card for the same business may not. Quebec, British Columbia, Alberta, Manitoba, Saskatchewan and Nova Scotia also restrict expiry on ordinary gift cards, with their own exceptions. Read the terms printed on the voucher before buying.

A small business may close before a voucher is used. For a voucher from a small business, encourage the recipient to book soon. A dollar-value card from a larger organisation, or tickets for a specific date, reduce that risk. For more on gift card rules, see the adult gift budget guide.

Experiences for kids

For children, a class or a membership to a place they love can last longer than a toy. Swimming lessons, a skating program, a science camp or a zoo membership often get more use than a one-time outing. Many municipalities sell recreation program gift certificates. Pair the gift with a printed card that shows the date or the first visit, so the child has something to open.

How to give an experience well

  1. Choose something the person has said they want to do, not something you want them to try.
  2. Pick the date with them, or choose one you know works.
  3. Read the fees, cancellation and rescheduling terms.
  4. Buy from the venue, company or official ticketing site.
  5. Add a card or printout that explains the gift and the date.
  6. Put it on both calendars.

Common mistakes

  • Giving an open voucher with no date that expires unused.
  • Buying a membership for someone who lives too far away to visit.
  • Ignoring fees and taxes added at checkout.
  • Buying a Parks Canada pass in a month the recipient will not use it.
  • Choosing an experience the giver wants rather than the recipient.

Frequently asked questions

What are good experience gifts in Canada?

Tickets to a specific event, a class with a start date, a membership to a place the person already visits, or a planned outing together. Choose something the person wants to do and put the date on the calendar.

Do experience vouchers expire in Canada?

They can. Ontario bans expiry dates on most gift cards but exempts cards for a specific single good or service, such as one spa treatment or a class. Other provinces have their own rules and exceptions. Read the terms on the voucher before buying.

How much is a Parks Canada Discovery Pass in 2026?

According to Parks Canada's 2026 fee pages, the adult Discovery Pass is $83.50 and the family or group pass is $167.50, covering up to seven people arriving together in one vehicle. Youth 17 and under get in free. The pass runs 12 months from purchase.

Is a museum membership worth it as a gift?

Divide the membership price by the visits the person will make in a year and compare with a single admission. If the cost per visit is lower, it is good value; if they visit once a year, single tickets or free days are better.
