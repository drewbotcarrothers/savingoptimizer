How to shop auto insurance in Canada: broker vs direct vs aggregator without getting lost

FSRA tips, coverage match, provincial quirks · About 16 minutes · 16 Sep 2026

Canadians renew auto insurance like a streaming app: the email arrives, the payment leaves, the coverage is a mystery until a claim. The other failure mode is chasing the cheapest aggregator number without matching deductibles. This guide is how to shop — broker vs direct vs aggregator — with Ontario FSRA consumer tips and a high-level map of other provinces. It is not a broker, not a quote, and not a promise that a channel is “cheapest.”

Ratehub has published a national average around $1,973 a year at the start of 2026 in its quoting materials, and FSRA-linked Ontario figures in the same neighbourhood as $2,164 late-2025 provincial average with GTA much higher. Your number is not the average. Your number is three comparable quotes.

Key takeaways

  • Shop identical coverage — limits, deductibles, listed drivers, km, accident-benefits options — not the first monthly price on a banner ad.
  • FSRA: at least three quotes; mix channels; do not assume last year’s winner is this year’s.
  • Wait for renewal to switch in Ontario (FSRA), unless you have a documented reason and you understand penalties.
  • Ask every quote about winter tires, multi-policy, multi-vehicle, telematics, commute vs pleasure, and group rates.
  • Public auto (ICBC, MPI, SGI) and Quebec’s SAAQ + private damage split change the menu. You still shop optional cover.

What brokers, direct insurers, and aggregators each do

Ontario language follows FSRA’s “Purchasing auto insurance” page. Other provinces use different regulator names.
Channel What they do What you still must do
Broker Sells for multiple companies they represent. You may ask for the full list. Check RIBO licence. Ask which markets declined you and why.
Direct writer The company sells its own product (phone, web, or captive agent). Still get a broker quote. Direct is not automatically cheaper.
Aggregator / comparison site Collects data, shows indicative prices or sells leads. Re-key the same worksheet with a licensed seller before you buy. Watch pre-checked add-ons.

Service is part of price: FSRA tells you to pick a representative you can reach at claim time. A $120 saving that becomes a 40-minute phone tree in a ditch is not a win.

Compare identical coverage and deductibles—not headline premiums

Two quotes are incomparable if one has $1 million liability and $500 collision deductible and the other has $200,000 liability (Ontario minimum) and $2,500 deductibles. FSRA notes it may not be cost-effective to carry collision on a car worth under $2,000, and that raising deductibles lowers premium if you can pay that deductible in cash tomorrow.

Accident benefits in Ontario have been in flux (optionality changes have been discussed for 2026). Do not copy a friend’s coverage. Read the options on the quote, including whether workplace or group health already duplicates some medical rehab. That is a conversation with a licensed person, not a Reddit thread.

Same-sheet rule: If the PDF does not list the same drivers, the same annual km, the same postal code, and the same vehicle VIN, you did not shop. You collected advertisements.

Provincial quirks: Ontario FSRA tips vs other provinces

  • Ontario: Private market, FSRA + RIBO. Shop around; winter-tire discount is mandatory to offer. GTA rates run hotter than rural in public averages.
  • Quebec: SAAQ covers bodily injury on the public side; you shop private insurers for damage to vehicles (collision, theft, etc.). Do not import an Ontario “full coverage” label.
  • B.C.: ICBC basic is public; optional cover is the shopping layer (ICBC optional vs some private optional). Know what is already in basic before you buy a duplicate.
  • Manitoba / Saskatchewan: MPI and SGI boards. Shopping looks like choosing packages and deductibles inside a public auto system, plus any permitted extras.
  • Atlantic / Alberta: Mostly private. Use the provincial superintendent / AIC-style licence lookup. Hail, theft, and winter each move the market differently — local brokers know the year.

We are not mapping every 2026 reform. If your province just changed accident benefits or medical deductibles, the regulator’s consumer page beats this article the next morning.

Discounts checklist: multi-policy, winter tires, telematics, mileage

FSRA’s discount list (ask every year; companies apply them differently):

  • Driver training / graduated licence (including stated 10% steps in G1/G2/G paths when clean).
  • Group (employer, union, professional, some non-profits).
  • Mature / retiree.
  • Multi-policy (home + auto often 5–15%).
  • Multi-vehicle (5–15%).
  • Renewal / loyalty (5–20% in FSRA’s ranges — irony: still re-shop).
  • Winter tires: must be offered in Ontario; bring receipts; ask about 3PMSF all-weathers.
  • Low mileage, alarms, telematics / UBI.

Telematics can cut or raise premium (Intact my Drive, Desjardins Ajusto, TD MyAdvantage style programs have said rates can go up for harsh driving). Read the consent. Winter “slow acceleration” should not be punished if the program is honest — ask.

Tell the truth about commute km. FSRA: pleasure vs to-work ratings differ. Understating km is how claims get messy.

How often to re-shop (and why mid-term switches can hurt)

Default: every renewal, with a 45-day calendar reminder. FSRA: the company that won last year may not win now; also, do not switch mid-term to dodge a small increase — cancellation penalties exist. If you sell the car, that is a different transaction (cancel/rewrite), not “rate shopping for fun in July.”

After a ticket or at-fault, re-shop at renewal anyway; some markets price a hit less badly than your incumbent.

FSRA also: pay on time. Policies cancelled for non-payment more than twice make you look expensive; monthly payment privileges can disappear.

Red flags: too-good rates with coverage gaps

  • Price 40% below the other two with a lower liability limit or missing specified perils.
  • “We don’t need the VIN yet.”
  • Pressure to bind on the phone before you read the quote summary.
  • Unlicensed Facebook “brokers.”
  • Telematics discount advertised as guaranteed forever.
  • Ignoring a young principal driver by calling them occasional when they take the car daily (FSRA: occasional vs principal is a real rating difference — fraud is not a discount).

Put the winning premium into car TCO. If insurance is the reason you keep a second car, read car-share math before the next renewal, not after another 12 months of debiting.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a broker and a direct insurer?

In Ontario, FSRA describes brokers as selling on behalf of many companies, and direct writers as companies that sell their own policies. You may ask a broker which insurers they represent. Only licensed brokers, agents, or companies can sell auto insurance in Ontario — check RIBO and FSRA registries.

How many quotes should I get?

FSRA tells Ontario consumers to get at least three quotes from a mix of direct writers, brokers, and agents. Rates differ for the same benefits. This is shopping education, not a requirement in every province, but three comparable quotes is a sound habit.

Should I switch insurers mid-term to save money?

FSRA’s how-to-save page says not to switch midway through the year — wait until renewal to avoid cancellation penalties. Ask your current insurer to re-price at renewal with an updated km and discount list first.

Is there an Ontario winter-tire insurance discount?

Yes. Ontario requires insurers to offer a discount for qualifying winter tires. Amounts and documentation (often four 3PMSF tires and a receipt) vary by company — typically discussed in the 2–5% range in consumer explainers. Confirm with the insurer; all-weather tires may or may not qualify.

Are you a broker? Can you sell me a policy?

No. Saving Optimizer publishes comparison-shopping education. We do not bind coverage, place risk, or recommend a specific insurer. Use licensed sellers in your province. Quote comparison tools, if we add them later, would be optional offer types with disclosure — not advice.
