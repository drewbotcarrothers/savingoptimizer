Honeymoon on a Budget From Canada: Timing, Points, Mini-Moons, Honeymoon Funds and Travel Insurance

Book in the name on your passport, insure the trip, and let the date be flexible · About 11 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 1 Oct 2026

A budget honeymoon from Canada starts with flexible timing and a few rules that prevent expensive mistakes. Book flights in the name on the passport you will travel with, because IRCC says changing your name after marriage needs a new passport application, not a renewal. Buy travel health insurance before you leave: travel.gc.ca warns that provincial plans may cover none, or only a small part, of medical costs abroad and never pay up front. Watch foreign transaction costs: FCAC's example shows a 2.5% conversion charge adding $36.25 to a €1,000 purchase. A mini-moon close to home, with a bigger trip later, can cut costs further.

Key takeaways

  • Book tickets in the name on your current passport.
  • Buy travel health and trip insurance before leaving Canada.
  • Card conversion charges add up; FCAC's example uses 2.5%.
  • Points help only if you pay balances in full.
  • A mini-moon now and a bigger trip later spreads the cost.
  • Example with made-up inputs: mini-moon vs trip abroad.

Timing and shoulder seasons

Travel prices change with demand, so being flexible on dates and destination is the simplest saving. Shoulder seasons, between a destination's peak and low seasons, can offer lower prices with reasonable weather; check typical weather and hurricane or rainy seasons for your destination. Leaving a few days or weeks after the wedding, rather than the next morning, can open cheaper flights and gives you time to rest and handle post-wedding tasks. A honeymoon months later also lets you use cash gifts and points earned on wedding spending.

Passport and names

IRCC says that after a name change due to marriage you must apply for a new passport rather than renew, with ID in your new name and the marriage certificate. Adult passports cost $163.50 for 10 years or $122.50 for 5 years as of 31 March 2026. If you travel soon after the wedding, keep your current passport and book tickets in that name, then change your name after you return. See name change after marriage.

Points and welcome bonuses

Points earned on wedding spending can pay for flights or hotels, but FCAC cautions that carrying a monthly balance and the interest paid can reduce the value of rewards. Pay the full statement every month and redeem points for the trip itself. Watch for merchant credit card surcharges of up to 2.4% outside Quebec. See credit card points on wedding spending.

Spending abroad

FCAC explains that card issuers apply a foreign currency conversion charge to purchases in other currencies. In its example, a €1,000 purchase at an exchange rate of 1.45 becomes $1,450, and a 2.5% conversion charge adds $36.25, for $1,486.25. FCAC also says foreign cash advances typically carry higher fees, plus conversion charges, with interest from the day you withdraw. Check your card agreement for its conversion rate before you travel, and avoid cash advances.

Mini-moons

A short trip within driving distance or a short flight keeps costs low and avoids jet lag right after the wedding. In cities, check accommodation taxes: the City of Toronto's Municipal Accommodation Tax is 6% on stays beginning 1 August 2026, after a temporary 8.5% rate ended, and Ontario adds 13% HST. You can plan a larger trip for the first anniversary, when you have had time to save. A mini-moon also leaves room in the budget if wedding costs ran over, and avoids paying peak prices for the week right after a popular wedding date.

Honeymoon funds

Guests can contribute to the trip through a honeymoon fund or a labelled cash fund. Read the platform's fee page for guest and payout fees before choosing. See registry vs cash fund vs honeymoon fund.

Travel insurance

Types of travel insurance, travel.gc.ca, as of 1 Oct 2026. As of 1 Oct 2026.
Type What it covers
Travel health Medical emergencies abroad; should include medical evacuation and return of remains
Trip cancellation Cancelling before departure for a covered reason
Trip interruption The unused portion of a trip if you must return early for an unforeseen reason

travel.gc.ca says to buy trip interruption and travel health insurance before you leave Canada, even for a day in the United States. It advises getting written confirmation that any pre-existing medical condition is covered, understanding the stability clause, and checking deductibles, regional exclusions and whether the plan covers drug or alcohol-related incidents. Check whether a credit card or workplace plan already covers you, and for how many days. Also check the destination's travel advice on travel.gc.ca before booking and again before you leave, and keep digital copies of your passports, insurance policy and bookings somewhere you can reach from any phone.

Flight problems

The Canadian Transportation Agency says that if a delay or cancellation is within the airline's control and not required for safety, and you were told 14 days or less before departure, a large airline owes $400 for arriving 3 to 6 hours late, $700 for 6 to 9 hours and $1,000 for 9 hours or more; small airlines owe $125, $250 and $500. Claim in writing within one year.

Plan

  1. Set a honeymoon budget separate from the wedding budget.
  2. Choose dates and a destination with flexibility.
  3. Book in the name on your current passport.
  4. Buy travel health and trip insurance.
  5. Use points and cash gifts; avoid carrying balances.
  6. Check your card's foreign conversion charge.

Example with made-up inputs

These numbers are an example with made-up inputs, apart from Toronto's 6% MAT, Ontario's 13% HST and FCAC's 2.5% conversion example. Option A, a three-night mini-moon in Toronto: hotel at $280 a night, $840 plus $50.40 MAT and $109.20 HST on the room rate, $999.60; meals and activities $700; train $180. Total: $1,879.60. Option B, seven nights in Portugal: flights $1,900 for two, a made-up €1,400 in accommodation and €900 in spending at 1.45 ($3,335) plus a 2.5% conversion charge ($83.38), travel insurance $220. Total: $5,538.38. Using 60,000 points worth a made-up $600 toward flights brings B to $4,938.38.

Common mistakes

  • Booking flights in a new name before the new passport arrives.
  • Travelling without health insurance.
  • Paying conversion charges without checking the card.
  • Carrying a balance on a rewards card.
  • Overbooking the first days after the wedding.

Frequently asked questions

Should I book my honeymoon in my married name?

Book in the name on the passport you will travel with. IRCC says a name change after marriage needs a new passport application.

Do I need travel insurance for a honeymoon?

travel.gc.ca advises buying trip interruption and travel health insurance before leaving Canada, since provincial plans may cover little or nothing abroad.

How much do foreign transaction fees cost?

It depends on your card. FCAC's example uses a 2.5% conversion charge, adding $36.25 to a EUR 1,000 purchase at 1.45.

What is a mini-moon?

A short, nearby trip right after the wedding, often with a bigger trip later.

What if our honeymoon flight is delayed?

Large airlines owe $400 to $1,000 for long delays within their control and not safety-related, if claimed within a year.

Can guests contribute to our honeymoon?

Yes, through a honeymoon or cash fund. Check the platform's fees first.
