OAS clawback in Canada: thresholds and how to reduce it

The income year matters more than the cheque date · About 9 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 24 Nov 2026

OAS clawback is a 15% recovery tax on income over a threshold, not an instant loss of the whole pension when you cross one dollar. The most expensive mistake is mixing the income year with the year of the monthly deductions. The second is trying to fix it with a transaction that costs more in tax or fees than the OAS it preserves.

Key takeaways

  • July 2026–June 2027 recovery uses 2025 income and the $93,454 starting threshold.
  • For 2026 income, the starting threshold is $95,323 and the recovery period is July 2027–June 2028.
  • The test is individual income. It is not the GIS household-income test.
  • Plan RRSP/RRIF withdrawals, taxable investment income and one-time gains together. TFSA withdrawals do not affect OAS.
  • CPP timing is a separate decision with its own guide and video, linked below.

Do not turn an upper threshold into a personal guarantee: partial OAS, deferral and other circumstances affect the actual pension available for recovery. Service Canada notes that current-year upper thresholds are estimates from January to September and final from October to December. Your return and notice of assessment establish the result.

Example: crossing the threshold does not lose the whole pension

Government example, 2025 income: $100,000 minus $93,454 is $6,546. At 15%, recovery is $981.90 for July 2026–June 2027, about $81.83 a month before rounding. It is not $6,546 and it is not the whole OAS pension.

Example for 2026 income: at $100,000, the excess over $95,323 is $4,677; 15% is $701.55, subject to the pension and tax-return calculation. A $1,000 taxable-income reduction while in the recovery range can reduce recovery by $150, in addition to any ordinary income-tax effect. It does not make a $1,000 expense free.

What can push income into the recovery range?

  • RRSP and RRIF withdrawals: generally taxable. A large withdrawal for a roof or vehicle can affect more than that year’s tax bill.
  • CPP, pensions and OAS: pension income counts under the applicable tax rules. More CPP later can be valuable and still interact with OAS.
  • Taxable investment income: interest, taxable capital gains and taxable dividends can raise net income. Dividend gross-up means the taxable figure can exceed the cash dividend.
  • A one-time event: selling an investment or receiving taxable severance can produce a year unlike the years around it.

The calculation uses income under tax-return rules, not simply cash deposited into the chequing account. Have an accountant model a large transaction before the year ends, particularly if it involves capital gains, a corporation, non-residency or foreign pensions.

OAS recovery is not GIS reduction

For October–December 2026, Service Canada lists maximum monthly OAS of $762.50 at ages 65–74 and $838.75 at 75+. Those are maximums, not what every senior receives. The Guaranteed Income Supplement is a separate, income-tested benefit with different thresholds and marital-status rules. Do not apply the $95,323 OAS threshold to a GIS decision.

Your annual planning checklist

  1. Start with the correct income year and individual threshold.
  2. List expected pensions, taxable withdrawals, investment income and one-time events for that year.
  3. Separate mandatory withdrawals from optional ones. Compare using TFSA savings rather than one large taxable withdrawal.
  4. Ask whether eligible pension splitting changes the two spouses’ total tax and benefits, not just one person’s clawback.
  5. Compare the year before and after the transaction. Saving $150 of OAS now can be a poor trade if it causes more tax later.
  6. Check the notice of assessment and monthly deductions. Request a review through CRA’s prescribed process if estimated deductions no longer reflect lower income.

Frequently asked questions

What is the OAS clawback threshold for 2026?

For 2026 income the recovery threshold is $95,323, affecting the July 2027–June 2028 recovery period. The July 2026–June 2027 period instead uses 2025 income and a $93,454 threshold. Do not mix the income year with the payment year.

How is the OAS clawback calculated?

The basic calculation is 15% of net world income over the year’s threshold, limited by the OAS subject to recovery. The government’s 2025 example uses $100,000 − $93,454 = $6,546, then 15% = $981.90.

Is OAS clawback based on household income?

No. It is based on the recipient’s individual income. GIS and some other benefits use different family-income rules.

Do TFSA withdrawals trigger OAS clawback?

TFSA income and withdrawals do not affect federal income-tested benefits such as OAS. An RRSP or RRIF withdrawal is generally taxable income and may increase recovery tax.

Does delaying CPP or OAS always prevent clawback?

No. Deferral changes when income starts and can increase later pension income. Model lifetime taxes, cash flow, life expectancy and other benefits; do not delay solely to avoid one year’s recovery tax.

Can OAS itself be split with a spouse?

OAS is not eligible for pension-income splitting. Some other eligible pension income can be split under CRA’s rules. CPP pension sharing is a separate arrangement, not the same tax election.
