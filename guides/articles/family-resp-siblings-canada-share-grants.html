Family RESPs for Siblings in Canada: Sharing Grants Without Mixing Up Beneficiaries

One plan for all the kids, with limits tracked per child · About 5 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 24 Nov 2026

A family RESP holds savings for two or more related children in one account. Growth and the Canada Education Savings Grant (CESG) can be shared, so a child who skips school doesn't strand the money. But contribution and grant limits are still tracked per child, so keep a simple ledger.

Key takeaways

  • Each child: $50,000 lifetime contribution limit across all plans.
  • Each child: $7,200 lifetime CESG limit.
  • CESG: 20% on the first $2,500 a year per child, or $500.
  • Children must be related by blood or adoption and under 21 when added.
  • BC adds a one-time $1,200; Quebec adds up to $250 a year.

Keep a per-child ledger

  • Log each year's contributions by child.
  • Track CESG received by child.
  • Note any BCTESG or QESI paid in.
  • Check the $50,000 and $7,200 limits each year.
  • Save annual statements.

Provincial grants that stack

BC's Training and Education Savings Grant pays a one-time $1,200 if you apply between the child's 6th birthday and the day before the 9th. Quebec's QESI adds 10% of net contributions, up to $250 a year and $3,600 lifetime.

Two kids: an example

Example: contributing $2,500 a year for each of two children earns $1,000 a year in CESG. Over 14.4 years each child reaches the $7,200 limit.

Frequently asked questions

Can siblings share an RESP?

Yes, in a family plan, if they are related to the subscriber by blood or adoption.

Can CESG be shared between siblings?

Yes, but each child can receive at most $7,200 in CESG.

What is the RESP contribution limit per child?

$50,000 lifetime across all plans.

Can I add a child to a family RESP later?

Yes, if they are under 21 and related by blood or adoption.

Do provincial grants work in a family plan?

BC's BCTESG and Quebec's QESI can be paid in for eligible children.
