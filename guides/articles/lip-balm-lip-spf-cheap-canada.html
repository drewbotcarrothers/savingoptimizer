Lip Balm and Lip SPF: Cheap Options That Work

A basic SPF stick, reapplied often, beats an expensive balm · About 5 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 24 Nov 2026

A basic lip balm with SPF does the job. A pharmacy sells ChapStick SPF 15 for $3.29, and Well.ca sells a Blistex SPF 15 twin pack for $4.79. What matters most is that it has SPF, shown by a DIN or NPN on the label, and that you reapply often. Pricey tinted balms don't protect any better.

Key takeaways

  • ChapStick SPF 15: $3.29; Blistex SPF 15 twin pack: $4.79 (checked 24 Nov 2026).
  • Lip products with SPF are regulated as sunscreens, so look for a DIN or NPN.
  • Reapply often, and after eating or drinking.
  • At night, plain petroleum jelly works as a balm.
  • Example year: $9.58 with twin packs.

What to look for

  • SPF 15 or higher for daily use; higher for skiing or the beach.
  • A DIN or NPN on the label.
  • Fragrance-free if your lips get irritated.
  • Multi-packs for the car, bag and coat pockets.

Example: you go through four sticks a year. Four single ChapSticks cost $13.16. Two Blistex twin packs cost $9.58.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need SPF in lip balm?

Lips can burn, so SPF helps outdoors, especially in sun and snow.

How do I know a lip balm's SPF is real?

In Canada, products with SPF should show a DIN or NPN.

How often should I reapply lip SPF?

Often, and after eating or drinking.

What's the cheapest good lip balm?

A basic SPF stick, like ChapStick or Blistex, or a multi-pack.

Is petroleum jelly OK for lips?

Yes, as a balm at night. It has no SPF.
