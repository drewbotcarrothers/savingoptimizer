Canadian apartment viewing checklist: catch costly problems before you sign

Lease inclusions and deposit photos, not a vibe tour · About 16 minutes · 20 Sep 2026

Renters rush viewings because Toronto, Vancouver, and central Montréal units priced near the market go in hours. Then the first hydro bill arrives, the sofa will not turn the stair, and the “included parking” was a hope. A 20-minute tour with a checklist is cheaper than a year of surprises. Statistics Canada’s national rent CPI was +2.8% year over year in August 2026 (Daily, 14 Sep 2026) — useless as your inclusions list.

This is a Canada lease-inclusion and deposit-protection viewing worksheet. It is not a home inspection and not a promise you will beat 40 other applicants.

Key takeaways

  • Run water, heat, and taps. Look up for stains. Listen at the window. These are later repair and sleep costs.
  • Confirm heat, hydro, water, parking, laundry in language that can go on the lease. An $80/month hydro miss is $960/year.
  • Ask condo/building rules before moving day: elevator booking, COI, guest parking, pets.
  • Measure the sofa path. Photograph every room the same day — B.C. tenants should expect an RTB-27 inspection.
  • Same-day decide from notes. Illegal fees and missing inclusions are still a walk-away. Send the package from the hallway if the unit is real.

Noise, water pressure, heat, and mould checks that save money later

Do the unglamorous things while the listing agent is still talking:

  • Water: hot and cold in kitchen and every bathroom. A trickle now is a plumber later — or a fight about “you overused it.”
  • Heat / AC: ask to run the system. Electric baseboards that will be on your hydro bill are a winter stack, not a feature. Depth: heat included vs hydro.
  • Moisture: window tracks, bathroom caulk, basement corners, closet ceilings. Mould is a health and a deposit fight.
  • Noise: open the window that faces the street or the elevator. A cheap rent beside a bar is a sleep tax.
  • Power: a phone charger in two rooms. Dead kitchens are a later power-bar invoice.

What inclusions to confirm: heat, hydro, water, parking, laundry

Write the listing claim, then write what the person with keys said, then write what you will put on the lease. If those three disagree, you do not have an inclusion. Parking “if a stall opens” is not parking. Laundry in the basement at $3.00 a load × three loads a week is about $35–$40/month before you count time. Water heaters and HVAC that sit on your account belong on the rental stack even if you are not buying.

Condo/building rules that affect moving day and guests

Ask before you celebrate the view:

  • Elevator booking window and certificate of insurance for movers — the DIY vs movers page prices this as a real line.
  • Guest parking, overnight guests, and short-term rental bans (your visiting parents are not an Airbnb, but the bylaw may still be picky).
  • Pet rules in the corporation documents, not the landlord’s shrug.
  • Quiet hours and renovation rules if you work from home.

Measuring for furniture so you do not overbuy later

Measure the elevator, the stair turn, and the longest wall in the bedroom. A $900 sofa that will not enter is a $900 lesson plus disposal. If you will buy after signing, take the measurements into the store; do not shop from the listing’s wide-angle photos. Furniture outlets are an offer type — shop dimensions first, brand second.

Photos and condition notes that protect your deposit

Photograph every room, the oven seal, floors, window screens, and the storage locker. Timestamp the album. Provincial money rules still apply:

Questions that reveal upcoming fee or utility surprises

  • What was last January’s hydro (or gas) on this meter? Can I see a bill?
  • Is any utility split with the house? How, and in writing?
  • Any condo special assessment or planned work I will feel as noise or a fee?
  • Who holds the keys today, and who is on title? (Scam filter — hunting guide.)
  • What fees sit beside rent: storage, locker, fob replacement, parking?

Application, holding, and key-money asks are a refuse — see illegal fees by province.

Same-day decision framework when competition is fierce

  1. Is the unit real (keys, ownership story, no wire-before-viewing)? If no, leave.
  2. Do inclusions and lawful deposit match the province? If no, leave or rewrite the offer.
  3. Would last-winter utilities plus rent still beat your next-best listing?
  4. Can your furniture and moving day survive the building rules?
  5. If yes: send the watermarked PDF now. If maybe: you will lose some units. That is cheaper than a bad lease.

Basements and “in-law suites” need extra safety checks — use the secondary-suite renter checklist. After you move in, shop tenant insurance for the lease’s liability limit, not the cheapest PDF.

Frequently asked questions

What should I check in a 20-minute Canadian viewing?

Run water, heat, and a tap in every bathroom; look for moisture; stand at the window for noise; confirm heat/hydro/water/parking/laundry in words you will put on the lease; photograph condition for the deposit; measure the sofa path. Then ask last-winter hydro and upcoming fees.

How do deposit rules change what I photograph?

Ontario last-month rent is not a damage hold — photos still protect you from a later claim. B.C. uses a condition inspection (RTB-27) and a half-month cap. Québec generally has no exacted deposit (C.c.Q. 1904), but condition notes still prevent “you wrecked it” arguments.

What inclusion surprises cost the most?

Electric baseboard billed to you, a 50/50 house-hydro split, parking that was “probably included,” and laundry that is $3.00 a load. An $80/month hydro miss is $960/year.

Should I decide the same day in a hot market?

In Toronto, Vancouver, and central Montréal, real units priced near the market often go in hours. Decide from your notes, not the music in the hallway. A missing inclusion or illegal fee is still a walk-away.

Is this legal advice?

No. It is a viewing worksheet. Confirm deposits and fees with the LTB, RTB, or TAL. Building rules come from the lease and the corporation, not this page.
