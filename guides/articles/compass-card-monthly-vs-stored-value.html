How to choose Compass Card monthly pass vs stored value for Metro Vancouver commuters

After the 1 July 2026 fare increase · About 16 minutes · 17 Sep 2026

Metro Vancouver riders overpay in two boring ways: they buy a 2-zone monthly pass for a hybrid week that never hits break-even, or they ride stored value five days across a zone boundary and never do the arithmetic. TransLink raised fares by an average of 5% on 1 July 2026. This is Compass math with those numbers — not a leftover 2024 screenshot, and not Toronto PRESTO. Pair it with the Canada transit overview and, if you still keep a car for the bridge, ownership TCO.

Disclosure: There is no natural affiliate product in Compass fare choice. Saving Optimizer does not sell transit passes. Fares below are from TransLink’s pricing page and Tariff Bylaw 162-2026, reviewed 20 Sep 2026. Confirm translink.ca before you AutoLoad a pass.

Key takeaways

  • Adult stored value (1 Jul 2026): $2.85 / $4.20 / $5.40 for 1 / 2 / 3 zones. Adult monthly: $117.20 / $156.70 / $211.65.
  • Break-even taps: ~42 (1-zone), ~38 (2-zone), ~40 (3-zone). A 3-day office week (~26 taps) should almost never buy the adult monthly.
  • After 6:30 p.m. weekdays, weekends, and holidays: the whole system is 1-zone. Buses are always 1-zone.
  • Concession monthly is $66.95 all zones. Adult DayPass $12.55. U-Pass BC $47.85/month from Sep 2026 for eligible students.
  • Tap out on rail/SeaBus. A 1-zone pass plus stored value covers AddFare if you cross an extra zone. Contactless debit/credit pays cash adult fares — no stored-value discount.

Zone math: 1-, 2-, and 3-zone commuting patterns

Three zones. The fare is how many boundaries you cross on SkyTrain or SeaBus during paid hours — not how many municipalities your employer lists on LinkedIn.

  • Bus and HandyDART: always a 1-zone fare.
  • SkyTrain: 1, 2, or 3 zones by time of day and boundaries.
  • SeaBus: 1 or 2 zones by time of day.
  • Evenings (after 6:30 p.m. weekdays), weekends, statutory holidays: 1-zone everywhere on conventional transit.
  • YVR AddFare: $6.50 extra when you start at YVR–Airport, Sea Island Centre, or Templeton (exemptions exist — see TransLink’s AddFare page).

A New Westminster–downtown weekday job is typically 2-zone on Expo Line during the day and 1-zone coming home after 6:30. A Surrey Central–Waterfront daytime commute is the 2-zone problem. A bus-only commute is 1-zone even if the bus crosses the planet. Count paid taps that leave the 90-minute window, not “I took transit that day.”

Monthly pass break-even vs stored-value taps after 2026 fare changes

Adult Compass products as of 1 July 2026 (TransLink pricing page). Break-even = monthly ÷ stored-value fare, rounded up. Cash/contactless is higher ($3.50 / $5.10 / $6.70) and should not be your commute product.
Product Stored value Monthly pass Taps to break even
1-zone $2.85 $117.20 42 taps ($119.70 would be 42 × $2.85)
2-zone $4.20 $156.70 38 taps
3-zone $5.40 $211.65 40 taps

Worked months (labelled, 22 weekdays):

  • Five-day 1-zone: 44 × $2.85 = $125.40 vs $117.20 pass. Pass wins by $8. Weekend rides are then “free” on the pass (and would have been 1-zone stored value anyway).
  • Five-day 2-zone daytime: 44 × $4.20 = $184.80 vs $156.70. Pass wins by $28. This is the commute the pass was built for.
  • Three-day hybrid 2-zone: 26 × $4.20 = $109.20 vs $156.70. Stored value wins by $47. Buying the pass “for simplicity” is a $47 habit.

A monthly pass is unlimited SkyTrain/SeaBus in the zones you bought, unlimited bus/HandyDART anytime, and all zones on all modes after 6:30 p.m., weekends, and holidays. If your extra rides are evening and Saturday, they do not help a 2-zone pass beat stored value as much as you think — those trips were already 1-zone.

DayPass and Concession: when they beat adult monthly

Adult DayPass $12.55 (all zones, first service until 4 a.m. next day; not valid on West Coast Express). Versus stored value: fifth 1-zone tap is $14.25; third 2-zone is $12.60; third 3-zone is $16.20. Visitors doing four SkyTrain hops and a SeaBus should load a DayPass, not bleed stored value. Commuters who ride two taps should not.

Concession (youth 13–18 with ID, 65+, HandyCard): stored value $2.30 / $3.40 / $4.60 (same as cash concession). Concession monthly is $66.95 all zones — one product, not three. Break-even vs 1-zone stored: $66.95 / $2.30 ≈ 30 taps. Vs 2-zone $3.40: ≈ 20 taps. A concession rider on a 2-zone commute hits the monthly faster than an adult. Carry the ID; fare checks are real ($173 infraction in TransLink copy).

Children 12 and under ride free. Do not buy them a pass.

U-Pass BC and employer Compass products

Eligible post-secondary students pay U-Pass BC in student fees: $47.85/month from 1 Sep 2026–31 Aug 2027 (was $46.90 the prior year). Load it each month at upassbc.translink.ca onto an Adult Compass Card (allow 24 hours). It covers TransLink bus, SkyTrain, and SeaBus across Metro Vancouver, with West Coast Express discounts — not BC Transit in Victoria. If you have it, you do not also need a $117 1-zone pass.

Some employers load Compass or offer payroll transit. Ask. A corporate product that is already paid is the cheapest “pass vs stored value” answer: take it. Do not AutoLoad a personal monthly on top of U-Pass or an employer card.

Tap rules, transfer windows, and SeaBus/West Coast Express quirks

  • 90 minutes to transfer across bus, SkyTrain, SeaBus, HandyDART from first tap. West Coast Express: 120 minutes.
  • Rail and SeaBus: tap in and out. Tap-in reserves up to a 3-zone fare; tap-out settles. Missed tap-out is a 3-zone charge you will feel.
  • Bus: tap in only. Cash on a bus is a paper transfer between buses only — not onto SkyTrain.
  • Passes are used first, then DayPass/WCE return, then stored value. Keep a little stored value on a 1-zone pass for AddFare when you cross an extra zone.
  • Contactless Visa/Mastercard/Amex/Interac and wallets charge adult cash fares. Physical card and the same card in Apple Wallet are different tokens — same trap as TTC. Pick one.
  • West Coast Express is a different tariff. A DayPass is not valid. WCE monthly is its own product. Do not assume your 2-zone Compass monthly is a Mission–Waterfront pass.

Register the card and turn on AutoLoad carefully. AutoLoad of a monthly pass will buy next calendar month whether you are on vacation or not. AutoLoad of stored value at $5 is the hybrid-WFH default.

A simple weekly ride log to pick the cheaper product

For two weeks, write every paid tap: date, time, from/to, zones Compass actually charged (from compasscard.ca history). Then:

  1. Count weekday daytime taps that were 2- or 3-zone. Ignore evening 1-zone conversions.
  2. Multiply by 2.2 to sketch a month (or just use four weeks of history).
  3. Compare to $117.20 / $156.70 / $211.65. If you are more than ~15% under break-even, stay on stored value.
  4. If you are a visitor-heavy month (family in town, DayPasses), price DayPasses separately — they are not a reason to buy a monthly.
  5. Re-run after a job or office-policy change. A 5-day mandate flips the sheet; a 2-day mandate flips it back.

Mistakes newcomers make with zone upgrades

  • Buying 3-zone “to be safe” when evenings are already 1-zone and the commute is 2-zone. That is $55 extra vs a 2-zone pass ($211.65 − $156.70).
  • Buying 1-zone then taking Expo across a boundary at 8 a.m. without stored value — AddFare needs value on the card, or you eat a fare inspection problem.
  • Using contactless “because PRESTO in Toronto capped.” Compass contactless is cash adult, not stored-value, and has no monthly cap like TTC’s 47-trip product.
  • Not tapping out at Waterfront. The system keeps the 3-zone reserve.
  • Loading a monthly on 29 September for a month you will spend in Kelowna. Compass months are calendar months.

If the car is the real leak, a $157 pass will not fix a $1,373 ownership stack. If transit is the commute, stop guessing zones. Log two weeks. Buy the product the log names.

Frequently asked questions

When does a Compass monthly pass beat stored value?

After the 1 July 2026 fares, an adult 1-zone pass ($117.20) beats stored value ($2.85) at about 42 taps. A 2-zone pass ($156.70 vs $4.20) breaks even near 38 taps. Five-day commuters usually win with the pass; three-day hybrid weeks usually should not buy it.

Are evenings and weekends 1-zone?

Yes. All trips starting after 6:30 p.m. weekdays, all weekend travel, and statutory holidays are 1-zone system-wide. Buses are always 1-zone. A 2-zone pass still matters for weekday daytime SkyTrain/SeaBus across a boundary.

Should I buy a DayPass instead?

An adult DayPass is $12.55 all zones until 4 a.m. It beats stored value around the fifth 1-zone tap ($14.25) or the third 2-zone tap ($12.60). It is not valid on West Coast Express.

What is U-Pass BC in 2026?

$47.85 per month from 1 Sep 2026 to 31 Aug 2027 for eligible students, loaded monthly on an Adult Compass Card. It beats every adult monthly pass. If your school bills it, do not also buy a 1-zone pass.

Do I need to tap out?

On SkyTrain, SeaBus, and West Coast Express, tap in and out so Compass can charge the right zones (it reserves a 3-zone fare on tap-in). Buses: tap in only. Missed tap-out is how people pay a 3-zone they did not ride.
