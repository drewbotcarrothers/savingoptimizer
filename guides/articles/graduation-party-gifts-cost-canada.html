Graduation Party and Gift Costs in Canada: A 2027 Budget Guide

Pick the party size first; the guest count drives almost every cost · About 10 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 1 Oct 2026

A graduation party in Canada can cost anything from the price of a cake and pizza at home to several thousand dollars at a rented hall, and the single biggest driver is the guest count. For most families the best-value plan is an open house at home or in a park, with a set menu and a firm guest list. For gifts there is no official guideline; give what fits your budget and relationship, and consider cash a graduate can put toward school or, if they are 18 or older, into a TFSA, where the 2026 annual dollar limit is $7,000. In Ontario, you do not need a special occasion permit to serve alcohol to invited guests at home, but you do if you sell it.

Key takeaways

  • Guest count drives cost: set it before anything else.
  • Home, backyard or park parties cost the least.
  • Ontario: no permit to serve alcohol to invited guests at home; a permit is needed to sell it (AGCO).
  • TFSA room starts at 18; the 2026 dollar limit is $7,000 (CRA).
  • There is no official gift amount; pick a range by relationship.
  • Example with made-up inputs: a 40-guest open house for $760.

Party size sets the budget

Food, drinks, rentals and even the cake scale with the number of guests. An open house, where people drop in over three or four hours, spreads the crowd and lets you serve snacks instead of a full meal. A sit-down dinner at a restaurant is the opposite: easy to host, but priced per person. Joint parties, where two or three graduating friends share one event, divide the venue and decor costs.

Typical cost lines

  • Food: platters, a barbecue, or catering trays. Ordering from a grocery deli is usually cheaper than a caterer.
  • Cake or dessert: a sheet cake feeds a crowd for less than cupcakes from a bakery.
  • Drinks: non-alcoholic drinks, plus alcohol only if hosts choose to serve it.
  • Decor: school colours, a photo backdrop and a few printed photos.
  • Rentals: tables, chairs, a tent or a hall if you need one.
  • Invitations: digital invitations are free.

Alcohol at a graduation party in Ontario

The Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario (AGCO) says you do not need a special occasion permit if you are serving alcohol to invited guests in your own home or a private place. It defines a private place as an indoor place where the public would not normally be and that is not rented out to the public. If you sell alcohol, such as through drink tickets, you need a permit. The AGCO lists private events with invited guests only, such as birthday parties, as events that can use a private event permit. If you rent a licensed restaurant or banquet hall, the venue's own licence may already cover alcohol service, so ask before applying. Hosts are responsible for guests' safety, so plan rides home, serve food and offer good non-alcoholic choices. Other provinces have their own rules; check your provincial liquor regulator.

How much to give as a graduation gift

There is no official guideline, and gifts vary with family custom, region and budget. A useful way to decide is by relationship: close family usually gives more than friends or neighbours, and a high school graduation often draws smaller gifts than a university one. For scale, Statistics Canada reports that the average household gave $386 in money to people in Canada over all of 2023, across every occasion.

Cash, a TFSA top-up or something useful

Cash is the most flexible gift. For a graduate who is 18 or older and a Canadian resident, the CRA says TFSA contribution room starts accumulating the year they turn 18, and the TFSA dollar limit for 2026 is $7,000. A family member can give cash that the graduate then contributes to their own TFSA, where investment growth is tax-free. For a younger graduate, cash in a savings account works until they turn 18. For students heading to college or university, practical gifts such as a laptop, a transit pass, kitchen basics for residence or a textbook fund are often more useful than decor or jewellery.

Example with made-up inputs

These numbers are an example with made-up inputs. A family in Ontario hosts a backyard open house for 40 guests after a high school graduation. They spend $380 on deli platters and a barbecue, $90 on a sheet cake, $110 on non-alcoholic drinks and a small amount of beer and wine served free to invited adults, $60 on decor and photo prints, and $120 renting a tent and extra chairs. Invitations are digital. The total is $760, or $19 per guest. No permit is needed because nothing is sold and the party is at home.

Steps

  1. Set the guest count and choose a format.
  2. Pick a date early, since many graduations fall in the same weeks.
  3. Price food from a grocery deli and compare one caterer.
  4. Decide on alcohol and check your provincial rules if anything is sold or the venue is rented.
  5. Agree on a gift range with family so no one feels outspent.
  6. If the graduate is 18 or older, suggest a TFSA top-up with cash gifts.

Common mistakes

  • Inviting before setting a budget, then finding the list has doubled.
  • Selling drink tickets at a hall without checking whether a permit is needed.
  • Over-ordering food for an open house where guests come and go.
  • Buying decor for one day that costs more than the food.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a graduation party cost in Canada?

It depends mostly on guest count and venue. A home or park open house costs far less than a rented hall or restaurant.

How much should I give for a graduation gift?

There is no official guideline. Choose a range by relationship and budget; close family usually gives more than friends.

Do I need a permit to serve alcohol at a graduation party at home in Ontario?

No. The AGCO says you do not need a special occasion permit to serve alcohol to invited guests in your own home or a private place.

When do I need a special occasion permit in Ontario?

When alcohol is sold, such as through drink tickets or a cash bar. A licensed venue's own licence may already cover the event.

Can a graduate put gift money in a TFSA?

Yes, if they are 18 or older and a Canadian resident. The CRA says the TFSA dollar limit for 2026 is $7,000.

How do I keep a graduation party cheap?

Host an open house at home or in a park, set a firm guest list, order from a grocery deli and use digital invitations.
