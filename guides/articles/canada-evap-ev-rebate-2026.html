How Canada’s EVAP EV Rebate Works in 2026 (What Replaced iZEV)

Federal point-of-sale incentive, live 16 Feb 2026 · About 15 minutes · 16 Sep 2026

Shoppers still Google “iZEV” and get a dead program. The federal replacement is the Electric Vehicle Affordability Program (EVAP), run by Transport Canada, live 16 February 2026. It still pays up to $5,000 on many new battery-electric and fuel-cell vehicles — but the price rule, the yearly step-down, and the “made in Canada” exception are not iZEV.

This is the current federal how-to, with a high-level provincial stack map. It is not a list of every eligible VIN. Confirm the vehicle list and remaining funds on tc.canada.ca the week you buy. Used vs new TCO still matters even with a rebate — see used vs new car TCO.

Disclosure: EV shopping / quote tools and home charger deals are opportunity types. Saving Optimizer may earn a commission if we later add partner links. We do not sell vehicles or file EVAP claims. The dealer submits the incentive.

Key takeaways

  • EVAP replaced iZEV on 16 Feb 2026. 2026 amounts: up to $5,000 BEV/FCEV, $2,500 PHEV.
  • Most deals must have a final transaction value of $50,000 or less. Canadian-made EVs have no FTV cap.
  • The dollar amount follows the dealer’s eligibility-assessment date, not your handshake date. 2027 is already a smaller cheque.
  • Leases 12 months or longer are pro-rated: (full amount ÷ 48) × months.
  • Federal funding: $2.275 billion over five years; $2.131 billion remaining as of 1 June 2026 on the program landing page. Recheck before you shop.

EVAP vs old iZEV: what changed for buyers

iZEV paid similar headline dollars off a manufacturer suggested retail price band (commonly $55,000 for many models, with higher-trim headroom to about $65,000). EVAP looks at final transaction value — the number on the bill of sale after discounts, with Transport Canada treating certain dealer discounts as reducing that value. A loaded trim that would have squeaked under iZEV’s MSRP ceiling can fail EVAP if you actually pay $54,000. A heavily discounted car can pass even if the window sticker was higher. Ask the dealer to show the FTV they will submit.

Origin rules tightened: eligible vehicles are made in Canada or in countries with a Canadian free-trade agreement. That is why some imported EVs never appear on the EVAP list even if they are for sale here. Chinese-origin models are the example every explainer uses; trust the official list, not a forum rumour.

Individuals get one EVAP incentive over the five-year window. Businesses and governments have higher caps; approved car-share fleets can claim more. If you already used EVAP (or you are swapping a leased EV), read the Q&A before you assume a second hit.

Program window: purchases and leases of 12 months or more from 16 Feb 2026 through 31 March 2031, or earlier if the envelope is empty. A 1 January 2027 assessment is a 2027-dollar claim even if you ordered in December.

$50,000 final transaction value rule—and Canadian-made exceptions

For most cars, FTV must be $50,000 or less. Transport Canada’s landing page is equally clear: no FTV limit on EVs made in Canada. That exception is the shopping instruction for higher-trim domestic models. “Made in Canada” is a program definition, not a patriotic feeling — it has to match how the vehicle is listed.

Taxes and fees: dealer guidance says the incentive is applied to the final transaction after taxes and fees in the portal workflow, and that manufacturer or dealer discounts come off before FTV is tested. Do not add GST/HST onto $50,000 in your head and panic, and do not ignore them either. Have the dealer walk the line items against the applicant guide. If they cannot, they are not ready to claim.

Used vehicles are not an EVAP purchase. Federal money here is for new eligible EVs. Some provinces (Manitoba’s pre-owned tier, Québec used BEV amounts) run separate used programs — different forms, different caps.

Point-of-sale process at participating dealers

You do not mail Ottawa a rebate form. An enrolled dealership or authorized seller submits an eligibility assessment through the EVAP portal, then applies the incentive on the deal. If the store was in iZEV, Transport Canada says they are auto-enrolled in EVAP but must accept new terms.

  1. Confirm the exact model/trim on the EVAP vehicle list.
  2. Ask whether the dealer is actively submitting EVAP (not “we used to do iZEV”).
  3. Get the FTV they will submit, in writing, including discounts.
  4. Do not take delivery on a handshake that “the rebate will show up later” unless the paperwork shows the incentive as a line item after assessment.
  5. Keep copies. If funding is tight, late submissions lose.

Private sales, grey-market imports, and most brokered internet deals are the wrong channel. EVAP is a dealer program.

Lease length pro-rating basics

Dealer resources publish: (full incentive ÷ 48) × number of months. A 48-month BEV lease in 2026 can see the full $5,000. A 24-month lease sees $2,500. Twelve months is the minimum lease length in the overview. Walk-away residual and kilometre caps are separate lease problems — the rebate does not fix a punitive residual.

Worked example (labelled): 36-month PHEV lease, 2026 assessment. ($2,500 ÷ 48) × 36 = $1,875 off, if the vehicle and FTV qualify. Not $2,500. Not $0. The pro-rate is why a cheap short lease can be a worse federal deal than a modestly longer one, all else equal.

Stacking with provincial programs (high-level map)

Federal and provincial money are different cheques. Stacks that were publicly described in 2026 (verify before you buy — programs sunset):

  • Québec — Roulez vert: ThinkEV and provincial materials describe about $2,000 on many new BEVs (MSRP-style provincial cap around $65,000), smaller PHEV amounts, used BEV help, and a current end date of 31 December 2026. Headline stack with EVAP: up to about $7,000 on an eligible new BEV. Québec publishes its own eligible-vehicle list.
  • Manitoba: $4,000 on many new eligible EVs, lease amounts $1,000–$4,000, $2,500 on some pre-owned, MSRP cap $70,000. Manitoba’s FAQ says you can also claim federal EVAP. Apply via the MPI-linked process, not through wishful stacking at the till.
  • Prince Edward Island: commonly $4,000 new BEV / $2,000 PHEV in 2026 explainers, with its own paperwork. Confirm pei.ca / transportation pages.
  • Ontario, Alberta, and several others: no matching provincial purchase rebate in many 2026 snapshots. Utility charger rebates may still exist. Do not invent a Queen’s Park cheque.
  • B.C.: CleanBC purchase incentives have been on, off, and redesigned in recent years. Check the current Go Electric / CleanBC page; do not rely on a 2023 screenshot.

Home Level 2 chargers are a separate envelope (federal and some provincial/utility programs). They do not use the EVAP vehicle portal. Price the charger after you know the car actually qualifies.

Verify funding remaining before you shop

iZEV died of an empty tank. EVAP’s landing page, reviewed for this article, showed $2.275 billion authorized and $2.131 billion remaining as of 1 June 2026. That is a dated snapshot. Refresh the program page and the statistics CSV before you order a vehicle that only makes sense with $5,000 off.

Year-end risk is double. 2027 dollars are smaller, and provincial programs (Québec’s 31 Dec 2026 note) can end on a different clock. If you need both cheques, you are shopping on the earlier deadline.

A rebate is not a reason to buy a car you cannot insure or park. Run ownership TCO with electricity instead of gasoline, then subtract only the incentive you can actually document.

Frequently asked questions

Is iZEV still available in 2026?

No. The federal iZEV program ended when its funds ran out. The Electric Vehicle Affordability Program (EVAP) opened 16 February 2026 and is the current Transport Canada incentive for eligible new purchases and leases. Search EVAP, not iZEV, when you talk to a dealer.

How much is the federal EV rebate in 2026?

Up to $5,000 for eligible battery-electric and hydrogen fuel-cell vehicles, and up to $2,500 for eligible plug-in hybrids. Amounts fall on a published calendar (for example $4,000 / $2,000 in 2027). The dollar amount follows the date the dealer submits the eligibility assessment, not the day you shook hands.

What is the $50,000 EVAP price cap?

Most vehicles must have a final transaction value of $50,000 or less. Transport Canada says there is no final-transaction-value limit on EVs made in Canada. iZEV used MSRP bands (often $55,000 / $65,000). EVAP uses the deal you actually sign, after discounts. Vehicles must be made in Canada or in a country with a Canadian free-trade agreement.

Can I stack EVAP with a provincial rebate?

Often yes, where a province still runs a purchase program. Québec’s Roulez vert (amounts declining; program currently described through 31 December 2026), Manitoba’s EV rebate ($4,000 on many new eligible EVs; stacks with EVAP per Manitoba’s FAQ), and PEI’s rebate are the usual stacks. Ontario and several other provinces have no matching purchase rebate in 2026 — verify the provincial page the week you buy. Funding can exhaust independently of EVAP.

How are EVAP lease incentives calculated?

Transport Canada’s dealer materials use (full incentive ÷ 48) × lease months, for leases of 12 months or more. A 24-month BEV lease in 2026 is half of $5,000 = $2,500 before other rules. Individuals can receive one EVAP incentive over the five-year program.
