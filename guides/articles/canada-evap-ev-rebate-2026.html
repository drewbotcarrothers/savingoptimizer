How Canada’s EVAP EV rebate works in 2026 (what replaced iZEV)

iZEV replacement, $50,000 rule, provincial stack map · About 15 minutes · 16 Sep 2026

Search results still say “iZEV $5,000.” That program is over. Transport Canada’s Electric Vehicle Affordability Program (EVAP) is the federal passenger incentive from 16 February 2026, aimed at cheaper EVs, with a $50,000 final transaction value cap — and a Canadian-made exception that iZEV did not work the same way.

Shoppers miss three things: the cap is out-the-door-ish not a $55k/$65k MSRP sticker; amounts decline through 2031; remaining money is first-come. This is the current federal how-to, dated 16 Sep 2026. It is not a model review and not a promise that your trim qualifies.

Disclosure: EV shopping/quote tools and home charger deals are offer types. Saving Optimizer may earn a commission if we later add partner links. No dealer, OEM, or charger partnerships are claimed. Incentive rules can change; Transport Canada has final say on eligibility.

Key takeaways

  • 2026: BEV/FCEV up to $5,000; PHEV up to $2,500 at point of sale. Program window 16 Feb 2026 to 31 Mar 2031 or until funds run out.
  • Generally $50,000 final transaction value or less. No cap if the EV is made in Canada. Must be new, light-duty, made in Canada or an FTA country.
  • Amount follows the dealer’s eligibility-assessment date, not when you take delivery.
  • One EVAP incentive per individual over five years. Leases 12–47 months are pro-rated; 48+ months get the full year amount.
  • Budget: $2.275 billion over five years; $2.131 billion remaining as of 1 June 2026 on the EVAP landing page. Recheck before you shop.

EVAP vs old iZEV: what changed for buyers

Transport Canada’s Q&A: EVAP is more targeted than iZEV, focused on affordable models. Headline shifts buyers feel:

  • Price test: iZEV used MSRP bands (commonly remembered as $55,000 / $65,000). EVAP uses final transaction value ≤ $50,000 unless the vehicle is made in Canada.
  • Origin: Canada or FTA partners — a rule that excludes some low-priced non-FTA production.
  • Decline path: published yearly step-downs so the market is not cliff-edged in 2031.
  • Volume: one incentive per person for the whole program; organizations up to 10; approved car-share fleets up to 50 per calendar year.

Transactions started before 16 Feb 2026 do not qualify, even if the car arrives later. Do not let a dealer “backdate” a January bill of sale in conversation; the portal will not care.

The “vehicle list” on tc.canada.ca is a shopping aid for models with MSRP ≤ $50,000. Transport Canada says a vehicle can still qualify if it is not on the list but meets the rules and the final value cap (or is Canadian-made). A listed trim can fail if the dealer loads options past $50,000.

$50,000 final transaction value rule—and Canadian-made exceptions

Think of final transaction value as the contracted vehicle price that Transport Canada counts: typically vehicle, factory options, destination, and dealer fees — not a licence to add $8,000 of accessories and still wink. Taxes are generally treated separately in program explainers; confirm the Q&A’s current definition with the dealer’s assessment, not a TikTok.

Made in Canada: no final-value cap. That is the industrial-policy hole in the $50k ceiling. It does not mean every Canadian-assembled nameplate is on a secret extra-$5,000. It means a loaded Canadian-made EV can still get the federal amount if other eligibility holds.

Used EVs: EVAP is a new vehicle program. Provincial used-EV rebates (Manitoba has advertised pre-owned amounts) are separate. See used vs new TCO if the honest move is a cheaper gas or used EV without federal money.

Point-of-sale process at participating dealers

  1. Confirm the dealer is enrolled in EVAP (Transport Canada dealer portal). A random independent with a used lot cannot “apply iZEV leftover.”
  2. Get the offer in writing with a line that shows the incentive and the final transaction value they will submit.
  3. Ask when they will submit the eligibility assessment — that date picks 2026 vs 2027 dollars if you are shopping near New Year.
  4. Do not take delivery until the discount is on the bill. EVAP is designed as a point-of-sale reduction, not a mail-in coupon you chase in June.
  5. Keep the paperwork. One incentive per person: if a spouse already claimed, you are done for the five-year window.

Funding is first-come. The landing page showed $2.131B remaining as of 1 Jun 2026 against $2.275B total. That is not a monthly household entitlement. If the public tracker drops, pause the signing appointment.

Lease length pro-rating basics

Q&A Table 1 (2026 full amounts): a $5,000 incentive is $5,000 on a 48-month lease, $4,062 at 39 months, $2,292 at 22 months, $1,250 at 12 months. The $2,500 PHEV column halves those examples ($2,031 / $1,146 / $625). Vehicle-list columns also show 36- and 24-month interpolations (e.g. $3,750 and $2,500 on a $5,000 BEV). Under 12 months: not eligible.

If you always lease 36 months, you are not getting the brochure $5,000. Budget the table, not the headline.

Municipal charger rebates and federal home-charger programs are another stack. Price the Level 2 plug only after the car qualifies — a $1,200 charger on a non-eligible SUV is not “part of EVAP.”

Frequently asked questions

What replaced the iZEV rebate?

The federal Electric Vehicle Affordability Program (EVAP). Eligible new purchases and leases from 16 February 2026. iZEV-era MSRP caps and amounts do not apply. Confirm on Transport Canada’s EVAP pages before you shop.

How much is the 2026 EVAP rebate?

Up to $5,000 for battery-electric and hydrogen fuel-cell vehicles, and up to $2,500 for plug-in hybrids, for purchases or leases of 48 months or more. Shorter leases (12–47 months) are pro-rated. Amounts fall in later years.

What is the $50,000 final transaction value rule?

Non-Canadian-made eligible vehicles must have a final transaction value of $50,000 or less (options, destination, dealer fees — a tighter test than iZEV’s MSRP bands). EVs made in Canada have no final-value cap. Country-of-origin rules also require Canada or an FTA partner.

Is the rebate paid at the dealership?

Yes — point of sale, if the dealer is enrolled and submits an eligibility assessment. The incentive year (2026 vs 2027 amounts) follows the submission date, not delivery day. Ask the dealer to show the portal submission.

Can I stack provincial rebates with EVAP?

Often yes where a provincial program still exists (for example Manitoba states EVAP can combine; Quebec Roulez vert has been a stack). Ontario, Alberta, and Saskatchewan generally have no provincial passenger EV rebate in 2026. Funding runs out — verify the provincial page the week you buy.
