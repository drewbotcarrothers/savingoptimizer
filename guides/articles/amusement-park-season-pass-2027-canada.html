Amusement Park Season Passes for 2027: Canada's Wonderland, La Ronde and When Prices Are Lowest

A pass pays off on visits, not on perks · About 10 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 1 Oct 2026

An amusement park season pass for 2027 is good value in Canada when you will visit at least two or three times, and buying in the fall can add months of use, because some 2027 passes also cover the rest of the 2026 season. As of 1 Oct 2026, Canada's Wonderland lists its 2027 Silver Pass at $89, Gold Pass at $105 and Prestige Pass at $239, each plus a processing fee of up to $9.99 and tax. The Silver Pass includes Halloween Haunt and WinterFest in 2026 and the 2027 season through Labour Day. In Montreal, La Ronde lists its 2027 Gold passport at $79.99 and Prestige at $149.99, plus taxes and surcharges, valid until 31 October 2027 and including unlimited admission to the Calypso, Valcartier and Wet'n'Wild Toronto water parks. Single-day La Ronde tickets start at $50.

Key takeaways

  • Canada's Wonderland 2027, as of 1 Oct 2026: Silver $89, Gold $105, Prestige $239, plus a processing fee of up to $9.99 and tax.
  • Silver covers Wonderland and Splash Works through Labour Day 2027 plus Haunt and WinterFest 2026. Gold covers all of 2026 and 2027 and Midwest Six Flags parks.
  • La Ronde 2027, as of 1 Oct 2026: Gold $79.99, Prestige $149.99, plus taxes and surcharges, valid to 31 October 2027, with Calypso, Valcartier and Wet'n'Wild Toronto water parks.
  • Break-even: divide the pass price by the visits you will really make and compare with a single-day ticket.
  • Parking, food and fast passes can cost more than admission; a pass that includes parking can change the maths.
  • Example with made-up inputs: a family of four that visits three times spends $360 less with season passes than with day tickets.

The break-even test

A season pass is a bet that you will go often enough. Divide the pass price, plus the processing fee and tax, by the number of visits you will realistically make, and compare that with the single-day ticket price you would otherwise pay, including any online discount.

  1. Write down how many times you went last year, or will realistically go next year.
  2. Price a single-day ticket for the dates you would go, using the park's online price.
  3. Price the pass with fees and tax.
  4. Divide the pass total by your visits and compare.
  5. Add parking per visit to both sides if the pass does not include it.

When pass prices are lowest

Amusement parks often sell next year's passes in the fall and change prices at set points through the winter and spring. Prices and inclusions can change without notice, so the prices in this guide are a snapshot as of 1 Oct 2026. Buying in the fall has a second advantage when the pass includes the rest of the current season, such as Wonderland's Silver Pass including Haunt and WinterFest 2026: the extra visits lower the cost per visit.

Do not buy early just because a deadline is coming. A pass you use once costs more than a day ticket. Put the pass in a sinking fund and buy when you are sure of the visits.

Food, drinks and fast passes

Food inside parks is a large part of a day's cost. Some parks offer dining plans or refillable drink bottles; they pay off only for frequent visitors who would buy those items anyway. Fast-lane products can cost more than admission and are optional. Read the park's rules on bringing your own food; some allow it in a picnic area outside the gates.

Common mistakes

  • Buying a pass for a family that visits once a year.
  • Comparing the pass with the gate price when an online day ticket is cheaper.
  • Forgetting the processing fee and tax.
  • Ignoring parking, which can change which pass is best.
  • Assuming La Ronde passport perks apply at the water parks; La Ronde says they do not.
