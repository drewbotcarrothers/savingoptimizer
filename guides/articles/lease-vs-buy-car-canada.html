Lease vs buy a car in Canada: total cost compared

Cash paid, equity left and the cost of getting out · About 9 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 24 Nov 2026

A lease can put the smaller number on the dealer’s window and still cost more over the years you actually need a car. Buying can build equity and still be the wrong decision if the loan is long, the rate is high or you will sell before the balance falls. Start with your ownership period and kilometres, then compare the same vehicle over the same horizon.

Key takeaways

  • Monthly or weekly payment is a cash-flow measure, not total cost.
  • Include the down payment, interest, taxes, fees, required maintenance, kilometre/wear charges and the vehicle value or debt left at the end.
  • Use a dealer’s written disclosure. The lease residual and buyout tax matter if you intend to keep the car.
  • A used-car purchase is a third option, not a footnote to two new-car quotes.

A real Canadian offer: the smaller payment is not the whole deal

Shop Toyota Ontario’s 2026 Corolla L page, checked 24 Nov 2026, displayed a vehicle price of $27,987. It advertised a lease at $89 weekly, 5.89%, 52 months, $1,000 down, versus finance at $122 weekly, 5.19%, 60 months, $0 down, including delivery and destination charge. The page’s lease calculator showed 225 weekly payments. These are dated advertised terms, not an all-in dealer quote or a national price. Dealer legal disclosure must establish taxes, fees, kilometres, residual and eligibility.

The lease’s stated 225 payments at $89, plus $1,000 down, total $21,025 before any amounts not included in that advertisement. That figure does not buy the car. It is not valid to compare it directly with a longer finance term and declare a saving. Ask for the same term and the total obligation on both disclosures, with the same trade-in value and taxes.

The lease has the lower payment but costs $2,800 more in this example. Lower the assumed resale value to $16,000 and the financed net cost becomes $24,800; the result reverses. That is why a resale assumption needs a stress test. Keep common fuel and insurance in your affordability budget even when they cancel in a like-for-like comparison.

Six questions before you sign

  1. Kilometres: how many do you drive, including winter trips, and what is the per-kilometre excess charge? Multiply a realistic overage by the contract rate.
  2. Wear: ask for the lease-end standard for tires, glass, dents and interior condition. Winter tires still need storage and replacement.
  3. Exit: what does early termination cost? Is transfer allowed and who remains liable?
  4. Buyout: what is the residual, purchase-option fee and tax if you keep it? Add any borrowing needed to fund that buyout.
  5. Trade-in: show its value separately. Rolling negative equity into a new contract hides old debt in a new payment.
  6. Ownership period: if you intend to drive it for ten years, compare ten years of ownership with multiple leases, not one lease with the whole purchase price.

When the third option wins: buy a suitable used car

A lower purchase price can matter more than a small rate gap. Inspect the vehicle, obtain a vehicle-history report and follow your province’s title, lien, safety and tax rules. A longer loan on an older car can leave a balance when repairs arrive; the “cheap” payment does not solve that. If a car is optional, compare car share or dropping the second car before negotiating either contract.

Frequently asked questions

Is a lower lease payment cheaper than financing?

Not by itself. A lease generally leaves no vehicle equity when you return the car. Compare cash paid over the same period, end-of-period resale value or loan balance, and kilometres, wear and other fees.

What is the real Toyota offer used here?

Shop Toyota Ontario displayed a 2026 Corolla L at $27,987, a $89 weekly lease at 5.89% for 52 months with $1,000 down, or $122 weekly finance at 5.19% for 60 months with $0 down when checked November 24, 2026. Read the dealer’s full legal disclosure before comparing after-tax totals.

Should I put a large down payment on a lease?

A down payment lowers the periodic payment but does not make that money disappear. Include it in total lease cost and ask how a theft or total loss would be settled. Do not compare zero-down financing with a down-payment lease by payment alone.

When is buying usually the better fit?

Buying can suit long ownership, high or unpredictable kilometres, modifications and keeping a paid-off vehicle. It still depends on the purchase price, borrowing rate, repairs and resale value.

Does the total-cost example predict resale value?

No. The example uses a clearly labelled $20,000 resale assumption after four years. Replace it with a conservative local estimate and test a lower value before deciding.

Can I end a car lease like a monthly subscription?

No. Read the early termination and transfer terms. Remaining payments, fees and approval requirements can make an early exit expensive.
