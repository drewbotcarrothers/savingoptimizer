Holiday Shipping in Canada (2026): Canada Post Deadlines, Costs and Cheaper Options

Send early, send light, or do not send at all · About 11 minutes · By Andrew · Updated 1 Oct 2026

Holiday shipping in Canada costs least when you send early by the slowest service that still arrives in time, ship less weight, or skip the parcel altogether. As of 1 Oct 2026, Canada Post had not yet published its official 2026 holiday send-by dates. Until it does, you can plan from its published delivery standards: between major urban centres, a national Regular Parcel takes 4 to 10 business days and an Expedited Parcel 2 to 8, while Xpresspost takes 2 and Priority arrives the next business day. Counting back from Thursday 24 December, the slowest national Regular Parcel standard between major centres means mailing by Thursday 10 December. Check Canada Post's own holiday dates once they are posted. A domestic stamp costs $1.24 in a booklet or $1.44 on its own, and Canada Post is closed on Friday 25 December and Monday 28 December 2026.

Key takeaways

  • Canada Post's official 2026 holiday send-by dates were not out as of 1 Oct 2026. Plan from its delivery standards, which count business days and skip weekends and holidays.
  • Between major urban centres, national delivery standards are 4 to 10 business days for Regular Parcel, 2 to 8 for Expedited, 2 for Xpresspost and next day for Priority.
  • Stamps as of 1 Oct 2026: $1.24 each in a booklet, $1.44 single, $1.75 to the U.S. and $3.65 international for a standard letter up to 30 g.
  • Canada Post is closed 25 and 28 December 2026. Christmas is a Friday and Boxing Day a Saturday.
  • The cheapest parcel is the one you send early by the slowest service, or do not send: ship online orders direct, or send a gift card or e-gift.
  • Example with made-up inputs: shipping four gifts by 5 December instead of 18 December cuts a $118 bill to $62.

Send-by dates: what is known so far

Canada Post usually publishes holiday send-by dates late in the fall. As of 1 Oct 2026 it had not posted its 2026 dates, so this guide does not list any. What Canada Post does publish all year is a set of delivery standards: the expected transit time for each service between local, regional and national points. Its Parcel Services guide, last updated 7 September 2026, says the standards are counted in business days from the next business day after you mail, and exclude the day of mailing, weekends and statutory holidays. A parcel dropped off after the post office's cut-off time counts as mailed the next business day.

Outside major urban centres the standards are longer: a national Regular Parcel can take 4 to 13 business days and an Expedited Parcel 4 to 12, and northern and remote centres can take up to 14. Regional means within the same region, such as Vancouver to Winnipeg in Canada Post's own example, and national means between regions, such as Vancouver to Halifax. The exact standard for your postal codes is in Canada Post's online delivery tool.

Counting back from 24 December

Canada Post is closed on Friday 25 December and Monday 28 December 2026, so Thursday 24 December is the last delivery day before Christmas. For the United States and other countries, Canada Post publishes separate delivery standards by destination, and the time a parcel spends with customs or other regulatory authorities is outside Canada Post's control. Look up the standard for the destination and add a margin for customs.

What it costs: stamps and parcels as of 1 Oct 2026

Cards are the cheap part. Canada Post's stamp prices page lists a standard domestic letter up to 30 g at $1.44 for a single stamp or $1.24 a stamp in a booklet. A heavier card between 30 g and 50 g is $1.75. A standard letter to the U.S. is $1.75 and an international letter is $3.65. PERMANENT stamps marked with a P are always accepted at the current domestic rate, so a booklet bought now still works next year if the price rises.

Parcels are priced by size, weight, distance and speed, so there is no single number. Canada Post's online price calculator gives the price for your parcel and route. The pattern is consistent: a heavier, larger or faster parcel costs more, and a national parcel costs more than a local one. That is why the three biggest savings are time, weight and box size.

Five ways to ship for less

  1. Ship early by the slower service. Regular Parcel usually costs less than Expedited, and both cost less than Xpresspost or Priority. Missing the Regular date is the most common reason a holiday parcel costs more.
  2. Shrink the box. Carriers can charge a light gift in a large box as if it were heavier, based on its size. Use the smallest sturdy box that fits, and soft packaging such as a padded envelope for clothing.
  3. Ship the online order direct. If you buy online, have the store send the gift straight to the recipient. Many retailers offer free shipping over a threshold, and you skip a second shipping charge.
  4. Combine parcels. One box to a family in another city costs less than four boxes to four people at the same address.
  5. Do not ship at all. Gift cards, e-gifts, digital subscriptions and money sent by e-transfer have no shipping cost. For gift card rules by province, see the adult gift budget guide.

Labour and reliability in 2026

Canada Post's labour dispute with the Canadian Union of Postal Workers disrupted service in late 2024 and again in 2025. In June 2026 CUPW members voted to accept new collective agreements, with 89.0% of urban members and 85.9% of rural and suburban mail carriers in favour, according to CUPW and Canada Post. The agreements were signed on 18 June 2026 and run to 31 January 2029. That removes the strike risk that shaped the last two holiday seasons, though weather and holiday volume still cause delays.

Private couriers such as Purolator, UPS and FedEx are alternatives for parcels, especially when speed matters. For remote and many Indigenous communities, Canada Post often handles the last mile even when a courier carries the parcel most of the way, so price both options for northern or rural addresses.

Customs, duties and what you cannot send

Parcels to the United States and other countries need a customs declaration with an honest description and value. A vague description such as 'gift' can delay a parcel. Rules on duties and on items such as food, alcohol, batteries and aerosols vary by destination; Canada Post lists prohibited and restricted items. A gift that is not allowed at the destination can be returned or destroyed, and the postage is not refunded.

For gifts coming into Canada, the recipient may owe GST or HST and duty on items bought abroad, and the courier may add a brokerage fee. If you are asking family outside Canada to send gifts, an e-gift or a gift card from a Canadian retailer can avoid those charges.

Insurance, tracking and proof

Most Canada Post parcel services include tracking, and some include a set amount of liability coverage, with more available for a fee. Insure a parcel only when the value would hurt to lose; for most gifts, tracking and a receipt are enough. Keep the receipt and the tracking number until the gift is opened. Take a photo of the contents and the label before you seal the box.

Common mistakes

  • Mailing too late for Regular Parcel and paying for express shipping.
  • Using a large box for a small, light gift.
  • Shipping a gift that a store could have sent directly.
  • Writing 'gift' on a customs form instead of describing the item and its value.
  • Forgetting that Canada Post is closed on 25 and 28 December 2026.
  • Mailing to the U.S. or overseas in the same week as domestic parcels.

Shipping belongs on the holiday budget as its own line. The December spending plan gives it one, so a late parcel does not come out of the gift money.
