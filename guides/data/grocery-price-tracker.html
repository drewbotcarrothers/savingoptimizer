Canadian Grocery Price Tracker

Statistics Canada CPI, monthly, not seasonally adjusted · By Andrew · Updated 24 Nov 2026

This page tracks how grocery prices are moving in Canada. The figures are the Consumer Price Index, not the price of milk at a store near you. Statistics Canada sets 2002 as 100. An index of 150 means a basket that cost CA$100 in 2002 costs CA$150 at that reading, before tax. The categories below are food, food purchased from stores, meat, fish and seafood, dairy products and eggs, bakery and cereal products, fresh fruit, fresh vegetables, and all-items as the comparison.

The series is monthly and not seasonally adjusted, for Canada as a whole. A jump in fresh vegetables from August to October is partly the growing season. The year-over-year column compares a month with the same month a year earlier, which is the fairer read.

Key takeaways

  • Latest month: October 2026. Food purchased from stores is 197.7 (2002=100), +2.0% from October 2025. All-items is 170.4, +3.1%. Canada, not seasonally adjusted.
  • A positive percent means that category's index rose. It is not a discount.
  • Fresh fruit and fresh vegetables are not seasonally adjusted, so the line moves with the growing season as well as with price.
  • The dollar figures translate the 2002=100 index. They are not shelf prices.

What the index is in Canadian dollars

In Canadian dollars, using the index base of 2002=100: a store-food basket that cost CA$100 in 2002 costs CA$197.70 at the October 2026 index for food purchased from stores, compared with CA$193.80 in October 2025. That is CA$3.90 more, the same +2.0% year-over-year change. All-items moved from CA$165.30 to CA$170.40, or CA$5.10 (+3.1%). This is the index expressed in dollars, before tax. It is not a receipt from a store.

The processed monthly file is grocery-cpi.csv. It starts with every published month in the cube for these nine Canada series. Year-over-year is blank only when the same month a year earlier is not in the cube.

What this does not tell you

  • It is not a flyer price, a unit price, or a provincial index. City and provincial CPIs exist in the same table and are not on this page.
  • Food purchased from stores is the grocery basket. The broader Food series also includes food from restaurants.
  • Bakery and cereal products are Statistics Canada’s series “Bakery and cereal products (excluding baby food).” Fish and seafood is “Fish, seafood and other marine products.”
  • A higher index means that basket costs more Canadian dollars than it did. It does not say whether your household’s bill moved by the same percent. What you buy, and which banner you use, still matters. The Food & Groceries guides cover flyers, unit prices and loyalty.

How the numbers are updated

A script in this site’s repository downloads the official cube (18100004-eng.zip) and rewrites this page, the embed, and grocery-cpi.csv. If the download fails, or a category is missing for the latest month, the script stops and does not fill in a guess. Run it after Statistics Canada publishes the next monthly CPI.

Frequently asked questions

Does this tracker show the price at my grocery store?

No. It is the Canada-wide Consumer Price Index for food categories, not a shelf price in a city or at one banner. A basket that cost CA$100 in 2002 is scaled by the index. That is a way to read the index in Canadian dollars, before tax.

Which Statistics Canada table is this?

Table 18-10-0004-01, Consumer Price Index, monthly, not seasonally adjusted, for Canada. The page is rebuilt from the official CSV cube. The source line states the retrieval date.

Why do fresh fruit and vegetable indexes jump from month to month?

This table is not seasonally adjusted. Fresh fruit and fresh vegetables move with the growing season as well as with price. Compare a month with the same month a year earlier, which is what the year-over-year column does.
