Black Friday Real-Discount Checker

Sale price versus the was price, in Canadian dollars · By Andrew · Updated 24 Nov 2026

A “40% off” tag is the gap between today’s price and the crossed-out was price. The real discount is the gap between today’s price and the lowest price you actually saw. This checker does that math in CA$, before tax, in your browser. It does not look up a store, and it does not keep what you type.

How the verdict is chosen

  • Advertised discount = (was price − sale price) ÷ was price.
  • Real discount = (recent low − sale price) ÷ recent low. A negative number means you would pay more than that low.
  • Real deal: the sale price is at or below the lowest price you saw in the window you pick (30, 60 or 90 days).
  • Modest: the sale price is above that low, but by 5% or less.
  • Fake “was” price: the sale price is more than 5% above that low. The crossed-out number is doing the work.

Five percent is a household cutoff so the verdict stays plain. It is not a legal test. The Competition Bureau’s ordinary-price rules need the store’s own sales records. Our Black Friday and Cyber Monday guide explains what a regular price has to meet, and why a countdown timer is not proof of a bargain.

Check a price

Use the Canadian-dollar amount on the tag, before tax. For the optional low, use a price tracker such as Keepa or camelcamelcamel, the store’s price history, or a number you wrote down. The guides under this tool show where those figures live for clothing, Amazon.ca household items, and electronics.

Verdict CA$299.99 is below the recent low Real deal

The tag says 40%. Against the recent low, the saving is 11.8%. It still counts as a real deal under the rule above, because the sale price beats the lowest price the shopper recorded. If the sale had been CA$379.99 against that same CA$339.99 low, the sale would be about 11.8% above the low, and the verdict would be a fake “was” price.

Where to get the recent low

Write the number down before you open a checkout. Match the exact model, size and seller. A was price on a different variant is not this item’s regular price.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between the advertised discount and the real discount?

The advertised discount compares the sale price with the was price on the tag. The real discount compares the sale price with the lowest price you saw in the last 30, 60 or 90 days. A large advertised percent can still be a small saving against that recent low.

How does this page choose real deal, modest, or fake was price?

Real deal means the sale price is at or below your recent low. Modest means the sale price is above that low by 5 percent or less. Fake was price means the sale price is more than 5 percent above that low. This is a household rule of thumb, not a Competition Bureau finding.

Are the prices I type sent anywhere?

No. The checker runs in your browser. It does not store the prices and it does not send them to Saving Optimizer. There is no account.

Where do I find the recent low?

Use a price tracker such as Keepa or camelcamelcamel, the store’s own price history, or a price you wrote down. On Amazon.ca, the strike-through help page describes a lowest price in 30 days. The linked guides cover clothing, household items on Amazon.ca, and electronics.
