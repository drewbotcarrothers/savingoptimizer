Students and education hub

Education savings for Canadian households

Short answer: To pay less for school in Canada, compare the full cost of each route (college, university, or both), apply for grants before loans, cut the living-cost side of the budget, and claim the tuition tax credits. Grants come first because they are not repaid: for 2026-27 the Canada Student Grant for Full-Time Students pays up to $525 per month of study, or $4,200 over an eight-month year (Canada Student Grant 2026-27), and average undergraduate tuition is $8,021 across Canada (college vs university).

Education spending after school still matters: certificates, language courses, and skills that change a paycheck. Check employer tuition support, public college options, and whether the credential is actually required before you pay list price.

How to save on school costs in Canada: the overview

1. Compare routes by total cost

A year of college tuition usually costs far less than a year of university. For 2026/2027, Mohawk College charges $2,762.24 for two semesters in most programs, while Statistics Canada’s preliminary average undergraduate tuition is $9,306 in Ontario and $8,021 across Canada (college vs university). Ontario schools may raise domestic tuition by up to 2% a year for three years from fall 2026 (Ontario tuition increase). No RESP? Start with paying without an RESP.

2. Grants before loans

The full-time Canada Student Grant is paid in full to a family of four at or below $76,952 in income and phases out to nothing at about $129,769 (grant thresholds). Provincial aid is applied on top, and the mix changed in Ontario: from 1 August 2026, no more than 25% of the provincial part of an OSAP package can be a grant (OSAP 2026-27). Alberta, B.C. and Quebec have their own rules, linked below.

3. Cut the living-cost side

Housing and food can cost more than tuition. A residence meal plan at Western costs $8,300 for 2026-27, but only $4,100 of that is food credit (meal plan costs). Opt out of inclusive-access textbooks before the deadline when the library or a used copy will do (textbook costs), and compare residence, home and a shared rental on one sheet (student housing costs).

4. Repay on a plan

Repayment starts six months after you leave full-time studies. The federal Canada Student Loan charges no interest, but Ontario’s portion charges prime plus 1%, including during the grace period (student loan repayment). If income is low, the Repayment Assistance Plan can reduce the payment, possibly to $0.

5. Claim the tax credits

Unused tuition credits can be transferred to a parent on Schedule 11 (transfer the tuition credit). Adults aged 26 to 65 build $250 a year of Canada training credit room, up to $5,000 over a lifetime, and can claim half of eligible fees against it (Canada training credit).
